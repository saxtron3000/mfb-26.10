# MFB BD Keynote: Bitcoin Diploma 2026.10

## Purpose

This is the public keynote document for a 30-minute presentation of the Bitcoin Diploma 2026.10 redesign and October 2026 publication target.

It combines the public stakeholder brief and keynote handoff into one source for:

- explaining what is changing and why;
- reassuring educators, learners, communities, contributors, and supporters about what stays the same;
- building a 30-minute public keynote or slide deck;
- preparing answers for likely stakeholder questions.

---

## Public thesis

The Bitcoin Diploma is being reorganized, not replaced: shorter, clearer, more practical, and more locally adaptable while preserving its open-source, community-led, Bitcoin-only identity.

---

## Executive summary

The Bitcoin Diploma 2026.10 is a clearer, safer-to-deliver beginner pathway for money and Bitcoin education. It is designed to be easier to teach, easier to complete, and more useful in local communities.

This redesign is not a replacement of the Diploma's mission. It is a refinement of the structure, delivery, learner experience, educator support, and public readiness.

Key points:

- The Diploma remains open source.
- The Diploma remains community driven.
- The Diploma remains focused on Bitcoin education.
- The learner pathway becomes shorter and more coherent.
- The approximately eight-hour pathway includes teaching, practice, assessment, feedback, and recording.
- The four required modules follow a Problem → Solution → Action → Connect learning arc.
- Educators facilitate the Diploma in person or online, with low-tech and offline-capable routes available.
- Practical learning, safety, and educator support are more intentional.
- Module 4 is required and educator-curated, connecting shared learning to local reality within shared standards.
- Advanced technical and specialist study remains outside the beginner Diploma.
- The publication target is October 2026.

Stakeholder message:

The redesign changes the learning experience, not the mission.

---

## Why the redesign is needed

The Bitcoin Diploma has strong foundations, but the learner path needs clearer structure.

The redesign responds to several needs:

- more accessible beginner learning;
- clearer sequencing;
- less unnecessary cognitive load;
- better educator support;
- stronger practical learning;
- clearer distinction between required Diploma competencies and future Advanced content;
- better international and local adaptability;
- stronger alignment between competencies, activities, and assessment.

The issue is not lack of value. The issue is helping more learners reach the value.

Public framing:

From long and dense to short, coherent, practical, and teachable.

---

## What the Diploma must preserve

The redesigned Diploma must preserve the identity and purpose of the original program.

Non-negotiables:

- open-source accessibility;
- community-driven delivery;
- grassroots adaptability;
- Bitcoin-only educational focus;
- educational seriousness;
- independent reasoning;
- learner autonomy;
- educator empowerment;
- conceptual coherence;
- global accessibility;
- support for real-world implementation, not only ideal conditions.

What stays the same:

- the mission;
- the community-led model;
- the Bitcoin-only focus;
- the open-source spirit;
- the goal of helping learners reason independently.

---

## Design direction

The 2026.10 design commitments are:

- educator-facilitated delivery in person or online, with a preference for in-person learning where it supports discussion and practice;
- an approximately eight-hour pathway that includes required teaching, practice, assessment, feedback, and recording;
- four required modules that preserve the learning sequence without requiring four fixed two-hour classes;
- practical learning in every module, using an explain → practise → apply → reflect rhythm;
- educator-adapted practical learning in Modules 3–4, including a required educator-curated Module 4 Connect task;
- an Educator Guide for facilitation, pacing, adaptation, safety, and assessment;
- activities available through the [Resources Hub](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/resources/);
- low-tech, offline-capable learning with no app, device, or connectivity requirement for learning, assessment, completion, or certification;
- an optional app for recap, self-checks, discovery, and connection to further My First Bitcoin learning—not assessment evidence; and
- Advanced technical and specialist study outside the beginner Diploma.

The redesign should teach learners how to think, not what to think. Learners should leave with better questions, clearer reasoning, and more confidence.

Legacy content is being reorganized, not simply discarded. Foundational money and fiat material moves into Understanding Money; Bitcoin origins and key properties move into How Bitcoin Works; wallet, recovery, verification, and safety material moves into Using Bitcoin Safely; and local Bitcoin-as-money scenarios move into Bitcoin in Your Community. Deeper technical, philosophical, and specialist content remains outside the beginner Diploma for future Advanced study.

## The four-module learning architecture

| Module | Learning Role | Title |
|---|---|---|
| Module 1 | Problem | Understanding Money |
| Module 2 | Solution | How Bitcoin Works |
| Module 3 | Action | Using Bitcoin Safely |
| Module 4 | Connect — educator-curated and required | Bitcoin in Your Community |

### Module 1: Understanding Money

Focus:

- why money exists;
- functions and properties of money;
- scarcity, value, and exchange;
- fiat money;
- inflation and purchasing power;
- why people look for alternatives.

Learner competency:

Learners can explain the money problems Bitcoin responds to.

### Module 2: How Bitcoin Works

Focus:

- why Bitcoin was created;
- decentralization;
- finite supply;
- peer-to-peer transfer;
- users, nodes, and miners;
- proof of work at a beginner level.

Learner competency:

Learners can explain Bitcoin’s purpose and key properties in beginner language.

### Module 3: Using Bitcoin Safely

Focus:

- wallets;
- keys;
- seed phrases;
- custodial and non-custodial use;
- receiving bitcoin;
- sending bitcoin;
- verification and safety.

Learner competency:

Learners can explain beginner self-custody concepts and demonstrate safe actions under approved, supported conditions.

### Module 4: Bitcoin in Your Community

Focus:

- an educator-curated local Connect task;
- practical workshops and demonstrations;
- local Bitcoin tools and resources;
- daily-life Bitcoin use;
- circular economies;
- merchant or community use cases;
- Bitcoin as money in the local context.

Learner competency:

Learners can safely complete one educator-curated Bitcoin-as-money task and explain its purpose, trade-offs, safety considerations, and realistic next step. A well-reasoned conclusion that a path is not suitable locally is valid.

## What changes for learners

Learners should experience a clearer, shorter, more practical Diploma.

Expected improvements:

- shorter path to completion;
- clearer sequence;
- less beginner technical overload;
- more structured discussion;
- more practical activities;
- more confidence building;
- stronger local relevance;
- a required educator-curated Module 4 task that makes the learning locally relevant; and
- Advanced study outside the Diploma for learners and educators who want more depth.

| Current Challenge | Redesigned Response |
|---|---|
| Long or dense learning path | Approximately 8-hour completion pathway |
| Advanced content too early | Required Diploma learning with Advanced study kept outside the beginner pathway |
| Passive learning risk | Discussion-rich activities |
| Practical skills inconsistent | Practical learning and supported evidence in every module |
| Local adaptation unclear | Adaptable examples with fixed competencies |

---

## What changes for educators

Educators should receive clearer teaching support before, during, and after delivery.

Expected improvements:

- clearer preparation guidance;
- clearer pacing;
- better activity selection;
- misconception support;
- facilitation prompts;
- review points;
- classroom decision-making support;
- adaptation boundaries.

The Educator Guide functions as a facilitation companion, not as a duplicate of the Student Guide.

The fourth module is educator-curated because local educators understand their learners’ situations, needs, risks, opportunities, and requirements better than a centralized curriculum can.

The redesign gives educators practical options and templates so they can make the required Connect task locally meaningful while protecting shared competencies, evidence standards, and safety boundaries.

## The new learning ecosystem

The redesigned Diploma should be understood as a learning ecosystem rather than a single document.

| Component | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Student Guide | Learner understanding, reflection, and key concepts |
| Educator Guide | Facilitation, pacing, classroom decisions, discussion management, and adaptation support |
| Resources Hub activities | Participation, practice, local adaptation, review, and practical learning |
| Module 4 planning and task templates | Local workshops, demonstrations, and community-specific Bitcoin-as-money tasks |
| My First Bitcoin app | Optional learner-owned recap, self-checks, discovery, and connection to further learning; never assessment evidence |
| Educator-led assessment | Explanation, synthesis, simulations, role-plays, and direct observation of practical competence |
| Future Advanced study | Deeper study without overloading the beginner pathway |

Possible supporting components:

- website or download hub;
- slide templates;
- assessment tools;
- localization guidance;
- advanced extensions.

## Assessment and certification direction

Assessment should connect every required competency to evidence that matches it. The model has two parts:

1. A 50% theoretical component, evaluated and recorded by educators through approved competency-aligned explanations, comparisons, illustrations, or scenario responses.
2. A 50% practical component, evaluated and recorded by educators through direct observation of approved demonstrations, simulations, tests, or role-plays.

Like a driving test, learners must show what they can explain, compare, apply, decide, and perform. The optional app can help learners review, but it does not assess, record, or determine certification.

Assessment should align with:

- assessed competencies;
- observable competency criteria;
- learner competencies;
- practical activities;
- delivery context.

Possible formative evidence:

- classroom discussions;
- think-pair-share outputs;
- reflection journals;
- exit tickets;
- activity completion;
- peer feedback;
- concept checks;
- misconception checks.

Possible summative evidence:

- project;
- case study analysis;
- presentation;
- podcast;
- written explanation;
- written exam;
- essay;
- practical demonstration.

Important distinction:

Modules 1 and 2 are knowledge-dominant; Modules 3 and 4 are skills-dominant and require direct observation of approved practical competencies. The two components are equal-status and non-compensatory: a combined score cannot substitute for unmet evidence in either one.

Participation, attendance, and app progress are not certification. A learner may receive a Diploma certificate only after both components meet the required standard for all 11 assessed competencies and the evidence is recorded. This protects a shared minimum standard across educators and communities.

Certificate governance, identity verification, and public verification remain implementation decisions; they must not create a path to certification without both assessment components.

## Localization and adaptation

The redesigned Diploma should support local relevance without weakening shared standards.

Fixed:

- assessed competencies;
- required Diploma criteria;
- competency expectations;
- Bitcoin-only educational focus.

Adaptable:

- examples;
- case studies;
- activities;
- classroom structures;
- timing adjustments;
- facilitation style;
- local references.

Flexible with guardrails:

- delivery schedules that preserve the official module sequence;
- delivery mode adaptations;
- localization choices;
- practical variants in Modules 3–4 and the depth of future Advanced study.

Stakeholder message:

Local relevance should increase without weakening learning standards.

## Risks and safeguards

| Risk | Safeguard |
|---|---|
| Modularization weakens coherence | Preserve conceptual progression and spiral review |
| Shorter duration reduces depth | Keep required Diploma learning coherent and place deeper study outside the beginner pathway |
| Educator workload increases | Build a strong Educator Guide |
| Technology fails | Design for low-connectivity and offline contexts |
| Local adaptation changes competencies | Define fixed competencies and adaptation guardrails |
| Practical learning becomes inconsistent | Use a clear explain → practise → apply → reflect rhythm |

## Release path to October 2026

Suggested release path:

1. Finalize publication materials and supporting implementation records.
2. Align the Student Guide, Educator Guide, Resources Hub activities, and assessment materials.

5. Complete translation-ready, public, and publication materials.
6. Publish the offline-capable print/PDF package, online PDF, and website-ready content through Sanity toward the October 2026 target.

## Slide notes

### Slide 1: Bitcoin Diploma 2026.10

Time: 1 minute

Key message: The Diploma is evolving, not being replaced.

Speaker note: Welcome the audience and name the redesign as a continuation of the same mission: open-source Bitcoin education led by communities. Set the destination: a public release target of October 2026.

Suggested visual: Title over a simple four-module strip.

### Slide 2: The foundation we are carrying forward

Time: 2 minutes 30 seconds

Key message: The redesign begins with respect for what the Diploma has already made possible.

Speaker note: The project is not responding to a lack of purpose or valuable material. It is carrying forward a serious beginner education program shaped by communities. The work is to reorganize that value so learners and educators can use it more easily.

Suggested visual: A bridge from the existing Diploma to the 2026.10 design, labelled “same mission, clearer path.”

### Slide 3: Why evolve now?

Time: 2 minutes

Key message: The opportunity is to improve sequencing, practical learning, and educator usability.

Speaker note: Name the design tensions plainly: content can feel long or dense; beginners can encounter technical detail too early; educators need clearer pacing and activity support; and communities need local relevance without losing shared standards. Do not portray the earlier Diploma as a failure.

Suggested visual: Four concise learner-and-educator challenges.

### Slide 4: What stays non-negotiable

Time: 2 minutes

Key message: The mission and identity remain intact.

Speaker note: Reassure stakeholders that open-source access, community-led delivery, Bitcoin-only education, independent reasoning, learner autonomy, and local adaptability remain at the centre. The Diploma teaches people how to think, not what to think.

Suggested visual: Four anchors: Open-source, Community-led, Bitcoin-only, Independent reasoning.

### Slide 5: What is changing

Time: 3 minutes

Key message: The redesigned Diploma is shorter, clearer, more practical, and easier to teach.

Speaker note: Introduce the approximately eight-hour completion pathway, including teaching, practice, assessment, feedback, and recording. Explain that four modules provide structure, not a mandatory set of two-hour classes. All four modules are required. Educators adapt practical learning in Modules 3–4 and curate the required Module 4 Connect task within shared standards; future Advanced study preserves depth without overloading beginners.

Suggested visual: Before / after table.

### Slide 6: The new learning story

Time: 3 minutes

Key message: The four modules create one coherent journey.

Speaker note: Walk through the official sequence: Understanding Money, How Bitcoin Works, Using Bitcoin Safely, and Bitcoin in Your Community. Emphasize the progression from a monetary problem, to Bitcoin’s purpose and design, to personal responsibility and safety, to a locally meaningful application.

Suggested visual: Horizontal four-module journey with the labels Problem, Solution, Action, and Connect.

### Slide 7: Practice with responsibility

Time: 3 minutes

Key message: Practical learning means guided orientation and safer next steps, not pressure or overclaiming mastery.

Speaker note: Explain the practical learning rhythm: educators introduce a key idea, learners practise through guided activities and scenarios, then apply or explain their learning and reflect on a safer next step. Practical work can include wallet, address, recovery, verification, and scam-awareness concepts. It does not make this a trading course, investment program, or a claim of high-stakes custody competence.

Suggested visual: Explain → Practise → Apply → Reflect loop.

### Slide 8: Educators complete the local story

Time: 3 minutes

Key message: Module 4 is required and educator-curated because educators know their communities best.

Speaker note: Educators curate a beginner-appropriate Module 4 Connect task based on local tools, risks, opportunities, and learner needs. Examples may include a Lightning demonstration, merchant-payment scenario, remittance comparison, local resource map, or community use case. The task includes a realistic alternative, safety considerations, and a responsible next step. Required competencies, criteria, and Bitcoin-only focus remain shared.

Suggested visual: An educator selects an approved local task from a Module 4 option bank.

### Slide 9: The learning ecosystem

Time: 3 minutes

Key message: The redesigned Diploma is a coordinated learning package, not a single book or an app.

Speaker note: Give each component a distinct role. The Student Guide carries learner explanations, reflection, and key concepts. The Educator Guide supports facilitation and adaptation. Resources Hub activities supply participation and practice. The app is optional and learner-owned: it can support recap, self-checks, and discovery, but it does not assess or record certification evidence. Future Advanced study holds deeper material outside the Diploma.

Suggested visual: Ecosystem diagram with educators and learners at the centre.

### Slide 10: A certificate that means something

Time: 2 minutes 30 seconds

Key message: A certificate represents demonstrated beginner learning, not attendance, app progress, or a financial recommendation.

Speaker note: The assessment has two equal-status, non-compensatory components: 50% educator-evaluated theoretical evidence and 50% directly observed practical evidence. All 11 assessed competencies must meet the required standard and be recorded before certification. Participation, attendance, and app progress alone do not qualify a learner. Detailed certificate governance and verification remain implementation decisions, so do not promise them.

Suggested visual: Two equal evidence paths converging on “assessment-complete,” then certificate.

### Slide 11: From redesign to release

Time: 2 minutes

Key message: The project is moving from architecture into review, field learning, publication, and launch readiness.

Speaker note: Show the path: align the Student Guide, Educator Guide, Resources Hub activities, and assessment materials; review with educators and communities; pilot or field-test where feasible; complete the offline-capable print/PDF package, online PDF, website-ready Sanity content, and translation-ready source; and publish toward the October 2026 target. Explain that this is a framework-first release, not a claim that every local route is ready for certificated delivery. Present the timing as a target, not a promise that bypasses quality review.

Suggested visual: Simple release path with October 2026 as the final marker.

### Slide 12: What collaboration makes possible

Time: 2 minutes

Key message: The redesign is strongest when people who teach and use it help shape it.

Speaker note: Invite educators to test educator-curated Module 4 variants and practical tasks; communities to share local constraints and use cases; contributors to build activities, visuals, and translation-ready materials; and supporters to help publication, educator readiness, and distribution. Local examples and named services remain local choices, not global endorsements.

Suggested visual: Four collaboration roles around the Diploma.

### Slide 13: The invitation

Time: 1 minute

Key message: Help make the next chapter of the Bitcoin Diploma clear, practical, locally useful, and faithful to its mission.

Speaker note: Close with the public thesis: shorter but not shallower; more practical but not less rigorous; locally adaptable while protecting a shared standard. Restate the October 2026 public release target and invite involvement.

Suggested visual: Closing statement with the four-module strip.

## Speaker guidance

Keep the tone public, factual, and reassuring.

Say:

- The Diploma is being reorganized, not replaced.
- It is becoming shorter, but not shallower.
- Legacy content is moving into the right places: the required Diploma modules or future Advanced study.
- The fourth module is required and educator-curated within shared standards.
- Practical workshops and demonstrations are becoming more central.
- The app is optional and never certification evidence in 2026.10.

Avoid:

- sounding like the earlier Diploma failed;
- promising financial outcomes, adoption outcomes, or personal financial decisions;
- promising certificate-governance details that have not yet been decided;
- presenting a named wallet, exchange, or local service as a global recommendation;
- treating open-source status as a guarantee instead of one transparency factor in a contextual wallet decision;
- overexplaining technical Bitcoin topics;
- using internal planning terms when a plain contrast works better.
