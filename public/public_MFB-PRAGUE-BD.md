# MFB Prague: Bitcoin Diploma Redesign Brief

## 1. Executive Summary

This document explains the planned redesign of the Bitcoin Diploma for key stakeholders and provides the source structure for a Keynote presentation ahead of the October 2026 release.

The Bitcoin Diploma is being redesigned to become shorter, clearer, more practical, more modular, and easier to teach while preserving its open-source, community-driven, Bitcoin-only educational identity.

This redesign is not a replacement of the Diploma's mission. It is a refinement of its structure, delivery, learner experience, educator support, and public readiness.

Key points:

- The Diploma remains open source.
- The Diploma remains community driven.
- The Diploma remains focused on Bitcoin education.
- The redesigned learner pathway should be shorter and more coherent.
- The working model is an in-person-first, approximately 8-hour pathway.
- The current working architecture is 4-block structure of approximately 2 hours each: Problem / Fiat, Solution / Bitcoin, Action / Self-custody, and Custom / Bitcoin as Money.
- Practical learning will become more intentional.
- Educator support will become clearer and more usable.
- Advanced technical content will be separated from the beginner pathway.
- The public release target is October 2026.

## 2. The Current Moment

The Bitcoin Diploma has an opportunity to reach more learners, educators, and communities by becoming easier to deliver without becoming less serious.

The redesign responds to several needs:

- more accessible beginner learning;
- clearer sequencing;
- less unnecessary cognitive load;
- better educator support;
- stronger practical learning;
- clearer distinction between core and advanced content;
- better international and local adaptability;
- stronger alignment between objectives, activities, and assessment.

The goal is not to reduce the value of the Diploma. The goal is to help more learners complete, understand, discuss, and apply the Diploma with confidence.

## 3. What The Bitcoin Diploma Must Preserve

The redesigned Diploma must preserve the identity and purpose of the original program.

Non-negotiables:

- open-source accessibility;
- community-driven delivery;
- grassroots adaptability;
- Bitcoin-only educational focus;
- educational seriousness;
- independent reasoning;
- learner autonomy;
- educator empowerment;
- conceptual coherence;
- global accessibility;
- support for real-world implementation, not only ideal conditions.

Stakeholder message:

The redesign changes the learning experience, not the mission.

## 4. Why The Redesign Is Needed

The redesign is needed because the Diploma can become more teachable, more completeable, and more practical for beginner learners.

Current challenges to address:

- The learner path can be long or dense.
- Technical content can arrive too early for beginners.
- Educators need clearer facilitation support.
- Practical activities need stronger structure.
- Learning objectives need clearer alignment with activities and assessment.
- Communities need local flexibility without losing shared standards.
- Beginner learners need confidence, not only information.

The challenge is not lack of value. The challenge is helping more learners reach the value.

## 5. Design Direction For The New Diploma

The current working design direction is:

- in-person first;
- approximately 8-hour maximum completion pathway;
- 4-block structure of approximately 2 hours each: Problem / Fiat, Solution / Bitcoin, Action / Self-custody, and Custom / Bitcoin as Money;
- one Diploma with Custom and Advanced pathways;
- Core / Custom / Advanced content separation;
- substantially increased practical learning;
- practical skills structured using PPP: Presentation, Practice, Production;
- advanced technical content separated from the beginner pathway;
- Educator Guide treated as a facilitation engine;
- Activity Bank treated as part of the learning ecosystem;
- localization supported through adaptable examples, activities, and facilitation.

Possible public framing:

From long and dense to short, coherent, practical, and teachable.

## 6. The Learning Philosophy

The redesigned Diploma should teach learners how to think, not what to think.

Learners should be encouraged to:

- ask better questions;
- compare claims;
- reason from first principles;
- distinguish evidence from opinion;
- evaluate trade-offs;
- continue learning independently;
- participate in structured discussion.

The redesigned Diploma should not produce passive agreement. It should produce better questions, clearer reasoning, and more confident learners.

## 7. The Learner Journey

The learner journey should move from foundational concepts toward practical orientation and independent reasoning.

Possible journey:

1. Learners begin with questions about money.
2. They explore value, scarcity, trade, and monetary systems.
3. They compare fiat systems and Bitcoin.
4. They understand Bitcoin from first principles.
5. They analyze risks, trade-offs, custody, and practical pathways.
6. They leave able to discuss Bitcoin clearly and continue learning independently.

Graduate profile:

- Knowledge: understands money, Bitcoin, custody, and trade-offs.
- Competence: can explain, compare, evaluate, and discuss.
- Reasoning: can think from first principles.
- Confidence: can ask better questions and avoid obvious mistakes.
- Participation: can join local Bitcoin education and discussion.

## 8. Proposed Curriculum Architecture

The following block structure is the working architecture and should be confirmed before public release.

### Block 1: Problem / Fiat

Focus:

- why money exists;
- functions and properties of money;
- scarcity, value, and exchange;
- fiat money;
- inflation and purchasing power;
- why people look for alternatives.

Learner outcome:

Learners can explain why fiat money is the problem Bitcoin responds to.

### Block 2: Solution / Bitcoin

Focus:

- why Bitcoin was created;
- decentralization;
- finite supply;
- peer-to-peer transfer;
- users, nodes, and miners;
- proof of work at a beginner level.

Learner outcome:

Learners can explain Bitcoin as the solution being introduced and how it differs from fiat money.

### Block 3: Action / Self-custody

Focus:

- wallets;
- keys;
- seed phrases;
- custodial and non-custodial use;
- receiving bitcoin;
- sending bitcoin;
- verification and safety.

Learner outcome:

Learners can explain beginner self-custody concepts and identify basic safety practices.

### Block 4: Custom / Bitcoin as Money

Focus:

- educator-selected local modules;
- practical workshops and demonstrations;
- local Bitcoin tools and resources;
- daily-life Bitcoin use;
- circular economies;
- merchant or community use cases;
- Bitcoin as money in the local context.

Learner outcome:

Learners can identify beginner-appropriate ways Bitcoin can function as money in local daily life.

## 9. What Changes For Learners

Learners should experience a clearer, shorter, more practical Diploma.

Expected improvements:

- shorter path to completion;
- clearer sequence;
- less beginner technical overload;
- more structured discussion;
- more practical activities;
- more confidence building;
- stronger local relevance;
- Custom and Advanced pathways for learners and educators who need more depth.

| Current Challenge | Redesigned Response |
|---|---|
| Long or dense learning path | 8-hour modular pathway |
| Advanced content too early | Core / Custom / Advanced separation |
| Passive learning risk | Discussion-rich activities |
| Practical skills inconsistent | PPP practical learning |
| Local adaptation unclear | Adaptable examples with fixed outcomes |

## 10. What Changes For Educators

Educators should receive clearer teaching support before, during, and after delivery.

Expected improvements:

- clearer preparation guidance;
- clearer pacing;
- better activity selection;
- misconception support;
- facilitation prompts;
- review points;
- classroom decision-making support;
- adaptation boundaries.

The Educator Guide should function as a facilitation engine, not as a duplicate of the Student Book.

## 11. The New Learning Ecosystem

The redesigned Diploma should be understood as a learning ecosystem rather than a single document.

Core components:

1. Student-facing materials

Purpose: learner understanding, reflection, and key concepts.

2. Educator Guide

Purpose: facilitation, pacing, classroom decisions, discussion management, and adaptation support.

3. Activity Bank

Purpose: participation, practice, local adaptation, review, and practical learning.

Possible supporting components:

- website or download hub;
- slide templates;
- assessment tools;
- localization guidance;
- advanced extensions.

## 12. Assessment And Evidence Of Learning

Assessment should connect learning objectives to evidence of learning.

Assessment should align with:

- learning objectives;
- competencies;
- learner outcomes;
- practical activities;
- delivery context.

Possible formative evidence:

- classroom discussions;
- think-pair-share outputs;
- reflection journals;
- exit tickets;
- activity completion;
- peer feedback;
- concept checks;
- misconception checks.

Possible summative evidence:

- project;
- case study analysis;
- presentation;
- podcast;
- written explanation;
- written exam;
- essay;
- practical demonstration.

Important distinction:

Assessment is part of the redesign foundation. Certification design is a future decision.

## 13. Certification: What Is In Scope And Out Of Scope

The current redesign may prepare foundations for certification through:

- learning objectives;
- competencies;
- assessment framework;
- evidence models.

Certification design itself is not currently finalized.

Future certification questions:

- What evidence proves competence?
- Who verifies competence in decentralized teaching environments?
- What is the difference between participation, completion, and certification?
- What must be standardized across communities?
- What can be adapted locally?
- What assessment evidence works for in-person, online, self-paced, and hybrid delivery?

Stakeholder message:

The redesign strengthens the foundations for future certification, but certification governance and verification are separate future decisions.

## 14. Localization And Adaptation

The redesigned Diploma should support local relevance without weakening shared standards.

Fixed:

- learning objectives;
- core outcomes;
- competency expectations;
- Bitcoin-only educational focus.

Adaptable:

- examples;
- case studies;
- activities;
- classroom structures;
- timing adjustments;
- facilitation style;
- local references.

Flexible with guardrails:

- block sequencing;
- delivery mode adaptations;
- localization choices;
- depth of Custom and Advanced material.

Stakeholder message:

Local relevance should increase without weakening learning standards.

## 15. Risks And Safeguards

| Risk | Safeguard |
|---|---|
| Modularization weakens coherence | Preserve conceptual progression and spiral review |
| Shorter duration reduces depth | Separate Core, Custom, and Advanced content |
| Educator workload increases | Build a strong Educator Guide |
| Technology fails | Design for low-connectivity and offline contexts |
| Local adaptation changes outcomes | Define fixed outcomes and adaptation guardrails |
| Practical learning becomes inconsistent | Use PPP: Presentation, Practice, Production |

## 16. Release Path To October 2026

Suggested release path:

1. Confirm design direction.
2. Finalize block structure.
3. Define Core / Custom / Advanced content.
4. Rewrite student materials.
5. Build Educator Guide.
6. Build Activity Bank.
7. Align objectives and assessment.
8. Review with educators.
9. Pilot or field test.
10. Complete final edits and publication.
11. Prepare public launch materials.
12. Release in October 2026.
