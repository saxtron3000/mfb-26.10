# MFB BD Keynote: Bitcoin Diploma 2026

## Purpose

This is the single public keynote document for presenting the Bitcoin Diploma redesign ahead of the October 2026 release.

It combines the public stakeholder brief and the 10-minute keynote handoff into one source for:

- explaining what is changing and why;
- reassuring educators, learners, communities, contributors, and supporters about what stays the same;
- building a short public keynote or slide deck;
- preparing answers for likely stakeholder questions.

---

# Public Thesis

The Bitcoin Diploma is becoming shorter, clearer, more practical, and more locally adaptable while preserving its open-source, community-led, Bitcoin-only identity.

---

# Executive Summary

The Bitcoin Diploma is being redesigned to become easier to teach, easier to complete, and more useful in local communities.

This redesign is not a replacement of the Diploma's mission. It is a refinement of the structure, delivery, learner experience, educator support, and public readiness.

Key points:

- The Diploma remains open source.
- The Diploma remains community driven.
- The Diploma remains focused on Bitcoin education.
- The learner pathway becomes shorter and more coherent.
- The working model is an in-person-first, approximately 8-hour pathway.
- The current working architecture is a 4-block structure of approximately 2 hours each.
- Practical learning becomes more intentional.
- Educator support becomes clearer and more usable.
- Advanced technical content is separated from the beginner pathway.
- The public release target is October 2026.

Stakeholder message:

The redesign changes the learning experience, not the mission.

---

# Why The Redesign Is Needed

The Bitcoin Diploma has strong foundations, but the learner path needs clearer structure.

The redesign responds to several needs:

- more accessible beginner learning;
- clearer sequencing;
- less unnecessary cognitive load;
- better educator support;
- stronger practical learning;
- clearer distinction between core and advanced content;
- better international and local adaptability;
- stronger alignment between objectives, activities, and assessment.

The issue is not lack of value. The issue is helping more learners reach the value.

Public framing:

From long and dense to short, coherent, practical, and teachable.

---

# What The Diploma Must Preserve

The redesigned Diploma must preserve the identity and purpose of the original program.

Non-negotiables:

- open-source accessibility;
- community-driven delivery;
- grassroots adaptability;
- Bitcoin-only educational focus;
- educational seriousness;
- independent reasoning;
- learner autonomy;
- educator empowerment;
- conceptual coherence;
- global accessibility;
- support for real-world implementation, not only ideal conditions.

What stays the same:

- the mission;
- the community-led model;
- the Bitcoin-only focus;
- the open-source spirit;
- the goal of helping learners reason independently.

---

# Design Direction

The current working design direction is:

- in-person first;
- approximately 8-hour maximum completion pathway;
- 4 blocks of approximately 2 hours each;
- one Diploma with Core, Custom, and Advanced pathways;
- substantially increased practical learning;
- practical skills structured using PPP: Presentation, Practice, Production;
- advanced technical content separated from the beginner pathway;
- Educator Guide treated as a facilitation engine;
- Activity Bank treated as part of the learning ecosystem;
- localization supported through adaptable examples, activities, and facilitation.

The redesign should teach learners how to think, not what to think. Learners should leave with better questions, clearer reasoning, and more confidence.

Legacy content is being reorganized, not simply discarded. Foundational money and fiat material moves into Problem / Fiat; Bitcoin origins and benefits move into Solution / Bitcoin; practical wallet and verification material moves into Action / Self-custody; daily-life payments, local tools, and local money examples move into Custom / Bitcoin as Money; deeper technical, philosophical, and future-facing content moves into Advanced pathways.

# The 4-Block Learning Architecture

| Block | Learning Role | Title |
|---|---|---|
| Block 1 | Problem | Fiat |
| Block 2 | Solution | Bitcoin |
| Block 3 | Action | Self-custody |
| Block 4 | Custom | Bitcoin as Money |

## Block 1: Problem / Fiat

Focus:

- why money exists;
- functions and properties of money;
- scarcity, value, and exchange;
- fiat money;
- inflation and purchasing power;
- why people look for alternatives.

Learner outcome:

Learners can explain why fiat money is the problem Bitcoin responds to.

## Block 2: Solution / Bitcoin

Focus:

- why Bitcoin was created;
- decentralization;
- finite supply;
- peer-to-peer transfer;
- users, nodes, and miners;
- proof of work at a beginner level.

Learner outcome:

Learners can explain Bitcoin as the solution being introduced and how it differs from fiat money.

## Block 3: Action / Self-custody

Focus:

- wallets;
- keys;
- seed phrases;
- custodial and non-custodial use;
- receiving bitcoin;
- sending bitcoin;
- verification and safety.

Learner outcome:

Learners can explain beginner self-custody concepts and identify basic safety practices.

## Block 4: Custom / Bitcoin as Money

Focus:

- educator-selected local modules;
- practical workshops and demonstrations;
- local Bitcoin tools and resources;
- daily-life Bitcoin use;
- circular economies;
- merchant or community use cases;
- Bitcoin as money in the local context.

Learner outcome:

Learners can identify beginner-appropriate ways Bitcoin can function as money in local daily life.

# What Changes For Learners

Learners should experience a clearer, shorter, more practical Diploma.

Expected improvements:

- shorter path to completion;
- clearer sequence;
- less beginner technical overload;
- more structured discussion;
- more practical activities;
- more confidence building;
- stronger local relevance;
- Custom and Advanced pathways for learners and educators who need more depth.

| Current Challenge | Redesigned Response |
|---|---|
| Long or dense learning path | 8-hour modular pathway |
| Advanced content too early | Core, Custom, and Advanced separation |
| Passive learning risk | Discussion-rich activities |
| Practical skills inconsistent | PPP practical learning |
| Local adaptation unclear | Adaptable examples with fixed outcomes |

---

# What Changes For Educators

Educators should receive clearer teaching support before, during, and after delivery.

Expected improvements:

- clearer preparation guidance;
- clearer pacing;
- better activity selection;
- misconception support;
- facilitation prompts;
- review points;
- classroom decision-making support;
- adaptation boundaries.

The Educator Guide should function as a facilitation engine, not as a duplicate of the Student Book.

The fourth block is educator-selected because educators are community leaders. They understand local situations, needs, risks, opportunities, and learner requirements better than a centralized curriculum can.

The redesign should give educators a bank of modules and activity templates so they can complete the Diploma in a way that is locally meaningful while still protecting shared learning standards.

# The New Learning Ecosystem

The redesigned Diploma should be understood as a learning ecosystem rather than a single document.

| Component | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Student Book | Learner understanding, reflection, and key concepts |
| Educator Guide | Facilitation, pacing, classroom decisions, discussion management, and adaptation support |
| Activity Bank | Participation, practice, local adaptation, review, and practical learning |
| Custom templates | Local workshops, demonstrations, and community-specific Bitcoin use cases |
| My First Bitcoin app | Micro-learning, knowledge checks, feedback, progress tracking, and combined-status record |
| Educator-led assessment | Explanation, synthesis, simulations, role-plays, and direct observation of practical competence |
| Advanced pathways | Deeper study without overloading the beginner pathway |

Possible supporting components:

- website or download hub;
- slide templates;
- assessment tools;
- localization guidance;
- advanced extensions.

# Assessment And Certification Direction

Assessment should connect every learning objective to evidence that matches it. The model has two parts:

1. App-supported knowledge and applied-understanding assessment.
2. Educator-led assessment of explanations, synthesis, simulations, role-plays, and directly observed practical performance.

Like a driving test, learners must show that they understand the relevant ideas and that they can perform the required skills. The app supports the theory side and may record combined status, but it cannot assess practical performance.

Assessment should align with:

- learning objectives;
- competencies;
- learner outcomes;
- practical activities;
- delivery context.

Possible formative evidence:

- classroom discussions;
- think-pair-share outputs;
- reflection journals;
- exit tickets;
- activity completion;
- peer feedback;
- concept checks;
- misconception checks.

Possible summative evidence:

- project;
- case study analysis;
- presentation;
- podcast;
- written explanation;
- written exam;
- essay;
- practical demonstration.

Important distinction:

Blocks 1 and 2 are knowledge-dominant, but the assessment should require substantial explanation, comparison, application, and synthesis—not only recall. Blocks 3 and 4 are skills-dominant and require direct educator observation.

Participation and attendance are not certification. A learner may receive a Diploma certificate only after both the knowledge-and-understanding component and the educator-led applied and practical component are passed and recorded. This protects a shared minimum standard across educators and communities.

Certificate governance, identity verification, and public verification remain implementation decisions; they must not create a path to certification without both assessment components.

# Localization And Adaptation

The redesigned Diploma should support local relevance without weakening shared standards.

Fixed:

- learning objectives;
- core outcomes;
- competency expectations;
- Bitcoin-only educational focus.

Adaptable:

- examples;
- case studies;
- activities;
- classroom structures;
- timing adjustments;
- facilitation style;
- local references.

Flexible with guardrails:

- block sequencing;
- delivery mode adaptations;
- localization choices;
- depth of Custom and Advanced material.

Stakeholder message:

Local relevance should increase without weakening learning standards.

# Risks And Safeguards

| Risk | Safeguard |
|---|---|
| Modularization weakens coherence | Preserve conceptual progression and spiral review |
| Shorter duration reduces depth | Separate Core, Custom, and Advanced content |
| Educator workload increases | Build a strong Educator Guide |
| Technology fails | Design for low-connectivity and offline contexts |
| Local adaptation changes outcomes | Define fixed outcomes and adaptation guardrails |
| Practical learning becomes inconsistent | Use PPP: Presentation, Practice, Production |

# Speaker Guidance

Keep the tone public and reassuring.

Say:

- The Diploma is being reorganized, not replaced.
- It is becoming shorter, but not shallower.
- Legacy content is moving into the right places: Core, Custom, or Advanced.
- The fourth block gives educators local choice.
- Practical workshops and demonstrations are becoming more central.

Avoid:

- listing all Advanced module titles;
- sounding like the old Diploma failed;
- promising certificate-governance details that have not yet been decided;
- overexplaining technical Bitcoin topics;
- using internal planning terms when a plain contrast works better.
