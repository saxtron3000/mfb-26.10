# MFB BD Keynote: Bitcoin Diploma 2026

## Purpose

This is the public keynote document for a 30-minute project-launch presentation of the Bitcoin Diploma redesign ahead of the October 2026 release target.

It combines the public stakeholder brief and keynote handoff into one source for:

- explaining what is changing and why;
- reassuring educators, learners, communities, contributors, and supporters about what stays the same;
- building a 30-minute public keynote or slide deck;
- preparing answers for likely stakeholder questions.

---

# Public Thesis

The Bitcoin Diploma is becoming shorter, clearer, more practical, and more locally adaptable while preserving its open-source, community-led, Bitcoin-only identity.

---

# Executive Summary

The Bitcoin Diploma is being redesigned to become easier to teach, easier to complete, and more useful in local communities.

This redesign is not a replacement of the Diploma's mission. It is a refinement of the structure, delivery, learner experience, educator support, and public readiness.

Key points:

- The Diploma remains open source.
- The Diploma remains community driven.
- The Diploma remains focused on Bitcoin education.
- The learner pathway becomes shorter and more coherent.
- The working model is an in-person-first, approximately 8-hour pathway.
- The current working architecture is a 4-module structure within an approximately 8-hour completion pathway.
- Practical learning becomes more intentional.
- Educator support becomes clearer and more usable.
- Advanced technical content is separated from the beginner pathway.
- The public release target is October 2026.

Stakeholder message:

The redesign changes the learning experience, not the mission.

---

# Why The Redesign Is Needed

The Bitcoin Diploma has strong foundations, but the learner path needs clearer structure.

The redesign responds to several needs:

- more accessible beginner learning;
- clearer sequencing;
- less unnecessary cognitive load;
- better educator support;
- stronger practical learning;
- clearer distinction between core and advanced content;
- better international and local adaptability;
- stronger alignment between objectives, activities, and assessment.

The issue is not lack of value. The issue is helping more learners reach the value.

Public framing:

From long and dense to short, coherent, practical, and teachable.

---

# What The Diploma Must Preserve

The redesigned Diploma must preserve the identity and purpose of the original program.

Non-negotiables:

- open-source accessibility;
- community-driven delivery;
- grassroots adaptability;
- Bitcoin-only educational focus;
- educational seriousness;
- independent reasoning;
- learner autonomy;
- educator empowerment;
- conceptual coherence;
- global accessibility;
- support for real-world implementation, not only ideal conditions.

What stays the same:

- the mission;
- the community-led model;
- the Bitcoin-only focus;
- the open-source spirit;
- the goal of helping learners reason independently.

# Design Direction

The current working design direction is:

- in-person first;
- approximately 8-hour maximum completion pathway;
- 4 modules within an approximately 8-hour completion pathway, rather than fixed two-hour classes;
- one Diploma with Core, Custom, and Advanced pathways;
- substantially increased practical learning;
- practical skills structured using PPP: Presentation, Practice, Production;
- advanced technical content separated from the beginner pathway;
- Educator Guide treated as a facilitation engine;
- Activity Bank treated as part of the learning ecosystem;
- localization supported through adaptable examples, activities, and facilitation.

The redesign should teach learners how to think, not what to think. Learners should leave with better questions, clearer reasoning, and more confidence.

Legacy content is being reorganized, not simply discarded. Foundational money and fiat material moves into Understanding Money; Bitcoin origins and benefits move into How Bitcoin Works; practical wallet and verification material moves into Using Bitcoin Safely; daily-life payments, local tools, and local money examples move into Bitcoin in Your Community; deeper technical, philosophical, and future-facing content moves into Advanced pathways.

# The 4-Module Learning Architecture

| Module | Learning Role | Title |
|---|---|---|
| Module 1 | Problem | Understanding Money |
| Module 2 | Solution | How Bitcoin Works |
| Module 3 | Action | Using Bitcoin Safely |
| Module 4 | Custom | Bitcoin in Your Community |

## Module 1: Understanding Money

Focus:

- why money exists;
- functions and properties of money;
- scarcity, value, and exchange;
- fiat money;
- inflation and purchasing power;
- why people look for alternatives.

Learner outcome:

Learners can explain why fiat money is the problem Bitcoin responds to.

## Module 2: How Bitcoin Works

Focus:

- why Bitcoin was created;
- decentralization;
- finite supply;
- peer-to-peer transfer;
- users, nodes, and miners;
- proof of work at a beginner level.

Learner outcome:

Learners can explain Bitcoin as the solution being introduced and how it differs from fiat money.

## Module 3: Using Bitcoin Safely

Focus:

- wallets;
- keys;
- seed phrases;
- custodial and non-custodial use;
- receiving bitcoin;
- sending bitcoin;
- verification and safety.

Learner outcome:

Learners can explain beginner self-custody concepts and identify basic safety practices.

## Module 4: Bitcoin in Your Community

Focus:

- educator-selected local modules;
- practical workshops and demonstrations;
- local Bitcoin tools and resources;
- daily-life Bitcoin use;
- circular economies;
- merchant or community use cases;
- Bitcoin as money in the local context.

Learner outcome:

Learners can identify beginner-appropriate ways Bitcoin can function as money in local daily life.

# What Changes For Learners

Learners should experience a clearer, shorter, more practical Diploma.

Expected improvements:

- shorter path to completion;
- clearer sequence;
- less beginner technical overload;
- more structured discussion;
- more practical activities;
- more confidence building;
- stronger local relevance;
- Custom and Advanced pathways for learners and educators who need more depth.

| Current Challenge | Redesigned Response |
|---|---|
| Long or dense learning path | Approximately 8-hour completion pathway |
| Advanced content too early | Core, Custom, and Advanced separation |
| Passive learning risk | Discussion-rich activities |
| Practical skills inconsistent | PPP practical learning |
| Local adaptation unclear | Adaptable examples with fixed outcomes |

# What Changes For Educators

Educators should receive clearer teaching support before, during, and after delivery.

Expected improvements:

- clearer preparation guidance;
- clearer pacing;
- better activity selection;
- misconception support;
- facilitation prompts;
- review points;
- classroom decision-making support;
- adaptation boundaries.

The Educator Guide should function as a facilitation engine, not as a duplicate of the Student Book.

The fourth module is educator-selected because educators are community leaders. They understand local situations, needs, risks, opportunities, and learner requirements better than a centralized curriculum can.

The redesign should give educators a bank of modules and activity templates so they can complete the Diploma in a way that is locally meaningful while still protecting shared learning standards.

# The New Learning Ecosystem

The redesigned Diploma should be understood as a learning ecosystem rather than a single document.

| Component | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Student Book | Learner understanding, reflection, and key concepts |
| Educator Guide | Facilitation, pacing, classroom decisions, discussion management, and adaptation support |
| Activity Bank | Participation, practice, local adaptation, review, and practical learning |
| Custom templates | Local workshops, demonstrations, and community-specific Bitcoin use cases |
| My First Bitcoin app | Optional microlearning, learner-owned self-checks, feedback, recap, and discovery; never assessment evidence |
| Educator-led assessment | Explanation, synthesis, simulations, role-plays, and direct observation of practical competence |
| Advanced pathways | Deeper study without overloading the beginner pathway |

Possible supporting components:

- website or download hub;
- slide templates;
- assessment tools;
- localization guidance;
- advanced extensions.

# Assessment And Certification Direction

Assessment should connect every learning objective to evidence that matches it. The model has two parts:

1. A 50% theoretical component, evaluated by educators through approved, objective-aligned interactions with criteria and records.
2. A 50% practical competency component, evaluated by educators through directly observed demonstrations, simulations, tests, and role-plays.

Like a driving test, learners must show that they understand the relevant ideas and that they can perform the required skills. The optional app can help learners review, but it does not assess, record, or determine certification.

Assessment should align with:

- learning objectives;
- competencies;
- learner outcomes;
- practical activities;
- delivery context.

Possible formative evidence:

- classroom discussions;
- think-pair-share outputs;
- reflection journals;
- exit tickets;
- activity completion;
- peer feedback;
- concept checks;
- misconception checks.

Possible summative evidence:

- project;
- case study analysis;
- presentation;
- podcast;
- written explanation;
- written exam;
- essay;
- practical demonstration.

Important distinction:

Modules 1 and 2 are knowledge-dominant, while Modules 3 and 4 are skills-dominant. The complete assessment is allocated 50% to educator-led theory and 50% to educator-observed practical competence.

Participation, attendance, and app progress are not certification. A learner may receive a Diploma certificate only after both the theoretical component and the educator-led practical competency component meet the approved standard and are recorded. This protects a shared minimum standard across educators and communities.

Certificate governance, identity verification, and public verification remain implementation decisions; they must not create a path to certification without both assessment components.

# Localization And Adaptation

The redesigned Diploma should support local relevance without weakening shared standards.

Fixed:

- learning objectives;
- core outcomes;
- competency expectations;
- Bitcoin-only educational focus.

Adaptable:

- examples;
- case studies;
- activities;
- classroom structures;
- timing adjustments;
- facilitation style;
- local references.

Flexible with guardrails:

- delivery schedules that preserve the official module sequence;
- delivery mode adaptations;
- localization choices;
- depth of Custom and Advanced material.

Stakeholder message:

Local relevance should increase without weakening learning standards.

# Risks And Safeguards

| Risk | Safeguard |
|---|---|
| Modularization weakens coherence | Preserve conceptual progression and spiral review |
| Shorter duration reduces depth | Separate Core, Custom, and Advanced content |
| Educator workload increases | Build a strong Educator Guide |
| Technology fails | Design for low-connectivity and offline contexts |
| Local adaptation changes outcomes | Define fixed outcomes and adaptation guardrails |
| Practical learning becomes inconsistent | Use PPP: Presentation, Practice, Production |

# Slide Notes

## Slide 1: Bitcoin Diploma 2026.10

Time: 1 minute

Key message: The Diploma is evolving, not being replaced.

Speaker note: Welcome the audience and name the redesign as a continuation of the same mission: open-source Bitcoin education led by communities. Set the destination: a public release target of October 2026.

Suggested visual: Title over a simple four-module strip.

## Slide 2: The foundation we are carrying forward

Time: 2 minutes 30 seconds

Key message: The redesign begins with respect for what the Diploma has already made possible.

Speaker note: The project is not responding to a lack of purpose or valuable material. It is carrying forward a serious beginner education program shaped by communities. The work is to reorganize that value so learners and educators can use it more easily.

Suggested visual: A bridge from the existing Diploma to the 2026.10 design, labelled “same mission, clearer path.”

## Slide 3: Why evolve now?

Time: 2 minutes

Key message: The opportunity is to improve sequencing, practical learning, and educator usability.

Speaker note: Name the design tensions plainly: content can feel long or dense; beginners can encounter technical detail too early; educators need clearer pacing and activity support; and communities need local relevance without losing shared standards. Do not portray the earlier Diploma as a failure.

Suggested visual: Four concise learner-and-educator challenges.

## Slide 4: What stays non-negotiable

Time: 2 minutes

Key message: The mission and identity remain intact.

Speaker note: Reassure stakeholders that open-source access, community-led delivery, Bitcoin-only education, independent reasoning, learner autonomy, and local adaptability remain at the centre. The Diploma teaches people how to think, not what to think.

Suggested visual: Four anchors: Open-source, Community-led, Bitcoin-only, Independent reasoning.

## Slide 5: What is changing

Time: 3 minutes

Key message: The redesigned Diploma is shorter, clearer, more practical, and easier to teach.

Speaker note: Introduce the approximately eight-hour completion pathway, including teaching, practice, assessment, feedback, and recording. Explain that four modules provide structure, not a mandatory set of two-hour classes. Core content is shared; Custom content makes room for local practice; Advanced content preserves depth without overloading beginners.

Suggested visual: Before / after table.

## Slide 6: The new learning story

Time: 3 minutes

Key message: The four modules create one coherent journey.

Speaker note: Walk through the official sequence: Understanding Money, How Bitcoin Works, Using Bitcoin Safely, and Bitcoin in Your Community. Emphasize the progression from a monetary problem, to Bitcoin’s purpose and design, to personal responsibility and safety, to a locally meaningful application.

Suggested visual: Horizontal four-module journey with the labels Problem, Understanding, Responsibility, and Local application.

## Slide 7: Practice with responsibility

Time: 3 minutes

Key message: Practical learning means guided orientation and safer next steps, not pressure or overclaiming mastery.

Speaker note: Explain the Presentation, Practice, Production approach: educators demonstrate; learners practise through guided activities and scenarios; learners explain, apply, or reflect. Practical work can include wallet, address, recovery, verification, and scam-awareness concepts. It does not make this a trading course, investment program, or a claim of high-stakes custody competence.

Suggested visual: Presentation → Practice → Production → Reflection loop.

## Slide 8: Educators complete the local story

Time: 3 minutes

Key message: Module 4 is locally selected because educators understand their communities best.

Speaker note: Educators can select beginner-appropriate Custom practice based on local tools, risks, opportunities, and learner needs. Examples may include a Lightning demonstration, merchant-payment scenario, remittance comparison, local resource map, or community use case. The learning objectives, Core outcomes, competency expectations, and Bitcoin-only focus remain shared.

Suggested visual: An educator selects approved local examples from a Custom module bank for Module 4.

## Slide 9: The learning ecosystem

Time: 3 minutes

Key message: The redesigned Diploma is a coordinated learning package, not a single book or an app.

Speaker note: Give each component a distinct role. The Student Book carries learner understanding and reflection. The Educator Guide supports facilitation and adaptation. The Activity Bank supplies participation and practice. The app is optional and learner-owned: it can support recap, self-checks, and discovery, but it does not assess or record certification evidence. Advanced pathways hold deeper study outside the beginner Core.

Suggested visual: Ecosystem diagram with educators and learners at the centre.

## Slide 10: A certificate that means something

Time: 2 minutes 30 seconds

Key message: A certificate represents demonstrated beginner learning, not attendance, app progress, or a financial recommendation.

Speaker note: The assessment has two non-compensatory components: 50% educator-evaluated theoretical evidence and 50% directly observed practical competence. Both must meet the approved standard and be recorded before certification. Participation, attendance, and app progress alone do not qualify a learner. Detailed certificate governance and verification remain implementation decisions, so do not promise them.

Suggested visual: Two equal evidence paths converging on “assessment-complete,” then certificate.

## Slide 11: From redesign to release

Time: 2 minutes

Key message: The project is moving from architecture into review, field learning, publication, and launch readiness.

Speaker note: Show the path: confirm and refine the design; complete learner and educator materials; align activities, objectives, and assessment; review with educators; pilot or field-test; prepare translation-ready and public materials; publish for the October 2026 target. Present the timing as a target, not a promise that bypasses quality review.

Suggested visual: Simple release path with October 2026 as the final marker.

## Slide 12: What collaboration makes possible

Time: 2 minutes

Key message: The redesign is strongest when people who teach and use it help shape it.

Speaker note: Invite educators to test Custom modules and practical tasks; communities to share local constraints and use cases; contributors to build activities, visuals, and translation-ready materials; and supporters to help publication, educator readiness, and distribution. Local examples and named services remain local choices, not global endorsements.

Suggested visual: Four collaboration roles around the Diploma.

## Slide 13: The invitation

Time: 1 minute

Key message: Help make the next chapter of the Bitcoin Diploma clear, practical, locally useful, and faithful to its mission.

Speaker note: Close with the public thesis: shorter but not shallower; more practical but not less rigorous; locally adaptable while protecting a shared standard. Restate the October 2026 public release target and invite involvement.

Suggested visual: Closing statement with the four-module strip.

# Speaker Guidance

Keep the tone public, factual, and reassuring.

Say:

- The Diploma is being reorganized, not replaced.
- It is becoming shorter, but not shallower.
- Legacy content is moving into the right places: Core, Custom, or Advanced.
- The fourth module gives educators local choice within shared standards.
- Practical workshops and demonstrations are becoming more central.
- The app is optional and never certification evidence in 2026.10.

Avoid:

- sounding like the earlier Diploma failed;
- promising financial outcomes, adoption outcomes, or personal financial decisions;
- promising certificate-governance details that have not yet been decided;
- presenting a named wallet, exchange, or local service as a global recommendation;
- overexplaining technical Bitcoin topics;
- using internal planning terms when a plain contrast works better.
