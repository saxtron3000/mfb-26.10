# **Bitcoin Diploma 26.10: Quentin's review notes**

Quentin's handwritten notes on a printed copy of the Bitcoin Diploma Student Guide, Version 26.10, transcribed from 23 photos taken on 5 October 2026 (they are in this folder).  
Each note gives the page, the passage Quentin marked (quoted from the guide) and what Quentin wrote. Words in \[square brackets\] are the transcriber's, and notes Quentin crossed out on the page are included and labelled.  
The photos cover the cover page and pages 8 to 9, 20 to 47, 50 to 53, 60 to 63, 74 to 75 and 80 to 81\. Other pages were not photographed.

> **Current status note (2026-10-07):** The My First Bitcoin App launch is postponed. References in this dated feedback record describe the guide at the time of review and do not authorize a current-release app link, call to action, or QR asset.

## **Overall comments**

Notes about a whole module or the guide as a whole, in page order.

> * **Pages 22 to 23** (Module 1, top of the spread, sections 1.4 to 1.6): General feedback: I find it a bit hard to read due to a lack of logical connectors. Sentences feel disconnected sometimes.  
> * **Page 26** (Module 1, top of the page, section 1.8): Feels like the AI fed us mainstream sources. Maybe we should feed it Austrian economics books or Saif's books.  
> * **Page 27** (Module 1, top of the page, section 1.9): General feedback: I feel this is very similar to what I studied in economics at uni: a flat definition of terms with no questioning of what's behind them, like Keynesian economics BS.  
> * **Page 30** (end of Module 1, below the key takeaways): Not happy with module 1\. Feels like an edulcorated, watered down version of the BD, based on mainstream definitions that are very successful at “noyer le poisson” \[French: to muddy the waters\]. I feel like I'm reading a French economics textbook.  
> * **Pages 34 to 35** (Module 2, bottom margin, sections 2.3 to 2.6): Can education really be impartial... as in sharing no opinions? Maybe again impartiality can be sharing 0 opinions or sharing different opinions.  
> * **Page 47** (end of Module 2, below the key takeaways): Module 2 is much better, even though I think we still get (to some degree) that same flavour of flat information in the name of impartiality. But I think my major criticism is lack of ability to identify Bitcoin as a solution, because the problem / issue was not well identified in chap 1\. All technical stuff is on point.  
> * **Page 60** (end of Module 3, below the key takeaways): Module 3 is more technical and less reliant on opinions, which makes me like it much more. It presents the tradeoffs very well. That's the impartiality I like LOL  
> * **Page 75** (end of Module 4, below the key takeaways): Module 4 is very well constructed, asks the right questions for learners and educators to adapt to local context. Very good. IMO nothing to change.  
> * **Pages 80 to 81** (closing pages, top margin above “Continue with the Education Network”): Overall I feel like this is a lot of links and I would probably offload a lot of them to the app, whose job is to present all these resources in a more dynamic way.  
> * **Page 80** (closing pages, side margin beside “Learn from community case studies”): I would probably limit the CTAs to improving the BD, sharing it to other people, learning more via TRH and advanced modules.
