# Daniel’s Notes on BD26

## Overall structure and delivery

- Keep the concise, focused structure of BD 26.10.
- Keep the context of money within a single block rather than distributing it throughout the Diploma.
- Preserve the neutral starting point so educators can adapt the content to their local context.
- Keep activities out of the learner-facing Diploma; they will live in the Activity Bank as PDFs on the website.
- Use the Educator’s Guide to direct educators to relevant Activity Bank activities.
- Let educators choose the activities that suit their delivery context and learners, rather than requiring numbered activities within each Diploma block.
- Maintain a broad Activity Bank so educators can select proven activities and avoid those that do not work online, take too long, or are not useful in their context.
- Consider providing suggested activity sequencing or timing in the Educator’s Guide while preserving educator choice.
- Preserve the original four-block structure.
- Design the delivery plan with extra time where possible, rather than assuming every block can fit tightly into a two-hour class.

## Proposed time structure

- Encourage educators to ask learners to read each chapter before class, so classroom time can focus on discussion, questions, local context, and selected activities.
- In the Educator’s Guide, state that time planning should begin with the learning objectives and the delivery needed to achieve them; educators should not cut learning short merely to fit a fixed class duration.
- Conduct a thorough AI-supported review of Blocks 1 and 3 to identify material that could be removed or consolidated, while preserving the learning objectives, to reduce their delivery time.
- Review whether Block 1 and Block 3 need approximately four hours each, divided into two parts.
- Consider dividing Block 1 as follows:
  - Part 1: opening questions about money; why money exists; functions and properties of money; barter and the double coincidence of wants; scarcity, value, and exchange; and beginner monetary history from barter to modern fiat.
  - Part 2: fiat money; fractional-reserve banking at a beginner level; inflation and purchasing power; CBDCs as a beginner fiat-system example where appropriate; alternatives such as precious metals, real estate, and stocks; and a review of the problems created by fiat money.
- Keep Block 2 within the current estimated time range.
- Consider dividing Block 3 as follows:
  - Part 1: wallets; addresses; keys; seed phrases; custodial and non-custodial use; hot and cold wallets; wallet recovery and seed-phrase testing; receiving bitcoin; and sending bitcoin.
  - Part 2: verification; block explorers; irreversible transactions; a safety checklist; acquiring and exchanging bitcoin as pathway awareness rather than financial advice; beginner-level on-chain and Lightning payments; and a review of self-custody responsibility.
- Keep Block 4 within the current estimated time range.
- Recognize that educators may need to adapt their schedule to the available time, but guide them to protect the required learning objectives rather than reducing the lesson to fit a one- or two-hour class.
