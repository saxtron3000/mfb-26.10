# Community suggestions

This document sorts suggestions consolidated from `ARNOLD-SURVEY_Education Comments.md`, `ARNOLD_Comments meetings w_ Questionnaire.md`, and the Bitcoin Diploma teacher survey (`20260724_Teachers Survey (Responses) - Form Responses 1.csv`, 8 responses collected 5–12 November 2024) against the current Diploma redesign documents. Repeated feedback is merged into one actionable suggestion.

- **Done**: addressed in `workspace/04_design.md` and reflected in the current redesign direction or plan.
- **To Do**: aligned with the design and current project scope, but not yet made an explicit deliverable or action in `workspace/06_plan.md`.
- **Horizon**: useful suggestions that are not currently addressed in `workspace/03_scope.md` or `workspace/04_design.md`; retain for post-launch work.
- **Out of scope**: suggestions about other programs, ongoing community or educator services, outreach, funding, or operational infrastructure rather than this Diploma redesign.

“Done” means that the suggestion has been accounted for in the architecture, boundaries, principles, or planned deliverables. It does not necessarily mean that the final artifact has already been produced.

## Done

1. Make the Diploma shorter and easier to complete by reducing repetition, cognitive load, and unnecessary content density. — Dream Grad Academy; Bitcoin Sisonke

2. Preserve educator freedom to adapt the workbook to the audience, country, education level, language, and local history. Make local adaptation an explicit feature of the curriculum. — San Antonio Bitcoin Club; The Core; Bitcoin Roatan; Bitcoin Anambra

3. Add more local examples and context, including local currencies and price examples rather than relying heavily on U.S. or dollar-based examples. Use educator-selected local and regional adaptations. — Bitcoin Africa Story; Acepta Bitcoin; BTC Shule; Bitcoin Victoria Falls

4. Involve educators and local experts in developing and validating localized practice, especially where they understand local problems, risks, and learner needs. — Bitcoin Africa Story

5. Develop an intercultural and multilingual approach, with translation-ready English source material, simple language, terminology guidance, and culturally adaptable examples. — Mi Tierra Digital; Oui Bitcoin Haiti; Bitcoin Sisonke

6. Add optional or locally adaptable thematic pathways, including activism, careers, health, older adults, business owners, human rights, inheritance, and economic impact, where appropriate. Treat these as Custom or Advanced pathways rather than required beginner Core. — Bitkwa; Dream Grad Academy; SABTC; School of Satoshi

7. Provide current, grassroots examples and review current-data claims so outdated information is replaced, dated, cited, or made evergreen before publication. — The Core

8. Simplify the technical chapters, especially Chapters 8 and 9, while preserving only the technical detail needed for beginner understanding, safety, and reasoning. Move deeper material to Custom, Advanced, or educator background. — Bitcoin Victoria Falls; Bitcoin Campus Zambia; Michael/SowetoBTC; Bitcoin Sinonke

9. Teach technical topics in layers so learners can stop at an appropriate level of depth. Use plain explanations, clear conceptual hierarchy, and optional deeper material. — Michael/SowetoBTC; School of Satoshi

10. Add visualizations, simulations, and interactive tools for mining, UTXOs, wallets, and other technical concepts, including safe practice with test coins where appropriate. The teacher survey specifically requests a dynamic mining/hash demonstration and practical node, miner, and UTXO extensions, while the current redesign keeps deeper mechanics outside beginner Core. — Bitcoin BOMA Coalition; Acepta Bitcoin; School of Satoshi; Bitcoin Diploma teacher survey

11. Provide practical wallet instruction, including receiving, accessing, non-custodial use, recovery, verification, and safety. Adapt hardware-wallet content to the learner and local context without making global product recommendations. — Satoshi Team; Bitcoin Victoria Falls; Bitcoin Ekasi; BitJr Academy and Space

12. Add practical resources for educators teaching technical chapters, including demonstrations, emulators, software, and chapter-specific materials. — Bitcoin Africa Story; Bitcoin BOMA Coalition; Acepta Bitcoin; BTC Curacao; Bitcoin Diploma teacher survey

13. Use simpler wording, shorter sentences, less text, and accessible visual design. Favor clear ideas and images over dense presentation slides. — Bitcoin Victoria Falls; BitJr Academy and Space; BTC Curacao Institute

14. Add interactive content, including games, discussions, reflection, hands-on exercises, simulations, and production tasks across the learning pathway. — BitJr Academy and Space; Life Christian School; BTC Curacao Institute; Bitcoin Diploma teacher survey

15. Make technical sessions shorter or split them across sessions so learners are not overwhelmed or exhausted. Most teacher-survey respondents found the pace “Just Right,” but one found it too fast; preserve educator pacing choice and protect time for practice and review. — Michael/SowetoBTC; Bitcoin Ekasi; Bitcoin Diploma teacher survey

16. Make learning practical and connect lessons to immediate actions, such as receiving or accessing Bitcoin, while keeping practical work guided, supported, and safety-oriented. — Mision21; Satoshi Team; Bitcoin Edu Hub

17. Provide offline-capable delivery through print or PDF for places with limited electricity, internet, or device access. The teacher survey confirms that some classrooms lack audiovisual equipment and that video homework is not a reliable substitute. — Bitcoin BOMA Coalition; Bitcoin Diploma teacher survey

18. Add local and practical examples of mining, wallets, payments, saving, business use, and other Bitcoin activities through educator-selected Custom practice. This can include a locally appropriate entrepreneurship fair or community-use project, as requested in the teacher survey. — Bitcoin Africa Story; BTC Shule; Bitcoin School Argentina; Bitcoin Diploma teacher survey

19. Provide an instructor-focused Educator Guide with facilitation notes, explanations, activities, timing, discussion prompts, assessment guidance, and classroom-management support. The teacher survey's Guide ratings were mixed (Lesson Plan Guide average 3.25/5; Activities Guide average 3.12/5), supporting a clearer, more practical implementation guide. — Life Christian School; Bitcoin Diploma teacher survey

20. Include teaching guidance alongside new resources, including intended audience, lesson plan, lesson guide, preparation requirements, and suggested use. — Bitcoin Grove

21. Strengthen pedagogy for educators who know Bitcoin but have limited teaching experience. Include facilitation, teaching skills, learner listening, safe discussion, and non-coercive learning practices. The teacher survey also requests more activity ideas, subject-deepening courses, and training led or informed by experienced educators. — Bitcoin Grove; Dream Grad Academy; Bitcoin Diploma teacher survey

22. Share new materials through demonstrations and explain their intended audience and teaching approach. — Bitcoin Grove; BTC Curacao Institute

23. Support community-based delivery and local follow-through through educator-led Custom practice, local problem-solving, community use cases, and pathways that connect Bitcoin to local daily life. — Mision21; Bitcoin Africa Story; The Core

## To Do

24. Provide classroom-ready slide decks or presentation materials for each chapter, designed with less text and more visual support. The design supports visual and educator usability, but this is not yet an explicit `06_plan.md` deliverable. — Havo Notre Dame; BTC Curacao Institute

25. Pilot and triage the Activity Bank using educator evidence before finalizing required activities. Preserve high-value hands-on tasks—especially wallet setup, money discussions, and explorer work—and redesign, replace, or make optional activities that are repeatedly skipped or rated confusing, including the consensus-attack message exercise, transaction role-play, barter game, some time-preference discussions, and video-dependent tasks. Make survey skip questions optional so educators are not forced to report false skips. — Bitcoin Diploma teacher survey

26. Allow limited educator choice in the form of assessment evidence while keeping shared learning objectives, safety requirements, and rubrics fixed. Offer equivalent options such as spoken explanation, written response, role-play, project, or practical demonstration so assessment can account for learner accessibility and different strengths. — Bitcoin Diploma teacher survey

27. Create no-audiovisual alternatives for every video-linked learning objective, such as a short transcript, printable visual sequence, educator-led explanation, or offline activity. Treat videos as optional support or enrichment rather than a required learning dependency, because the teacher survey reports classrooms without audiovisual equipment and low completion of video homework. — Bitcoin Diploma teacher survey
