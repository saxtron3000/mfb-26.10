# 2026.10 Bitcoin Diploma Overview

## Purpose

This document is a short, standalone overview of the planned 2026.10 Bitcoin Diploma for internal staff, educators, partners, funders, and external collaborators. In this workspace, 2026.10 refers to the October 2026 version of the Diploma.

The Bitcoin Diploma is My First Bitcoin's flagship beginner education program. It is designed to help people understand what money is, why Bitcoin was created, how Bitcoin works at a beginner level, and how to approach Bitcoin use with personal responsibility and safety.

The 2026.10 version is a redesign of the existing Diploma. It is being reorganized, not replaced. The redesign preserves the Diploma's open-source, community-led, Bitcoin-only identity while making the learning path shorter, clearer, more practical, easier to teach, and easier to adapt across communities.

---

# Short Summary

The 2026.10 Bitcoin Diploma is an approximately 8-hour beginner course organized into four blocks:

1. Problem / Fiat
2. Solution / Bitcoin
3. Action / Self-custody
4. Custom / Bitcoin as Money

The first three blocks create a shared Core that every learner completes. The fourth block is selected by local educators so learners can connect Bitcoin to their own community, tools, risks, and opportunities.

The first publication mode is offline-capable, in-person print or PDF delivery. Future branches may include online PDF, online cohort, self-paced, web, app, and cross-modal formats, but these branches should preserve the same Core outcomes.

---

# Why The Diploma Exists

The Bitcoin Diploma exists to give beginners a structured path into Bitcoin education.

It is not a trading course, investment program, product recommendation system, or technical developer course. It is a foundational education program about money, fiat systems, Bitcoin, self-custody, and responsible participation.

Learners do not need technical or financial background. The Diploma is designed for students, community learners, educators, grassroots organizations, local Bitcoin communities, and partner institutions that want a serious but accessible introduction to Bitcoin.

The Diploma aims to help learners:

- ask better questions about money;
- understand the problems Bitcoin responds to;
- explain Bitcoin's core properties in plain language;
- compare fiat money and Bitcoin at a beginner level;
- understand basic custody, wallet, transaction, and safety concepts;
- identify realistic next steps without being pushed into financial decisions;
- connect Bitcoin education to their local context.

---

# What Is Changing In The 2026.10 Redesign

The redesign responds to problems found in the existing Diploma: unclear duration, broad scope, inconsistent pacing, technical overload for some beginners, limited measurable objectives, and uneven educator support.

The new version improves the Diploma by:

- shortening the beginner pathway to approximately 8 hours;
- organizing the course into four clear blocks;
- separating required Core content from Custom local practice and Advanced future learning;
- increasing practical learning, discussion, reflection, and learner explanation;
- improving educator guidance, pacing, activities, and assessment support;
- preparing English source files for translation and localization;
- reducing fragile links, QR-code clutter, and named-service dependencies;
- keeping advanced technical, economic, and philosophical material outside the beginner pathway.

The redesign does not remove the mission of the Diploma. It makes the mission easier to deliver.

# Four-Block Learning Path

## 1. Problem / Fiat

Learners begin with money itself.

This block introduces why societies use money, what makes money useful, how money has changed over time, and what problems can emerge in fiat systems. Topics include barter, the functions and properties of money, scarcity, purchasing power, inflation, fractional reserve banking, and beginner-level fiat-system roles.

Core question:

What problem does fiat money create?

Learner outcome:

Learners can explain why fiat money is the problem Bitcoin responds to.

## 2. Solution / Bitcoin

Learners then study why Bitcoin was created and what makes it different.

This block introduces the search for digital and decentralized money, the double-spend problem, Bitcoin's origin story, decentralization, peer-to-peer transfer, finite supply, satoshis, proof of work, users, nodes, miners, and verifiability at a beginner level.

Core question:

Why was Bitcoin created, and how does it respond?

Learner outcome:

Learners can explain Bitcoin's purpose and how it differs from fiat money.

## 3. Action / Self-custody

Learners then move from theory into responsibility.

This block introduces wallets, addresses, keys, seed phrases, custodial and non-custodial use, hot and cold wallets, wallet recovery, seed phrase testing, receiving, sending, verification, irreversible transactions, on-chain and Lightning distinctions, and common beginner safety risks.

The goal is not to make every learner ready for high-stakes custody. The goal is to help beginners understand the responsibility involved and identify safe next steps.

Core question:

How do I take responsibility for using and securing Bitcoin?

Learner outcome:

Learners can explain beginner self-custody concepts and identify basic safety practices.

## 4. Custom / Bitcoin as Money

The final block is educator-selected and locally grounded.

Educators choose the Custom block because local educators understand their students, tools, risks, opportunities, and community conditions better than a centralized curriculum can.

Possible Custom modules include Lightning demonstrations, merchant payments, local spending and receiving scenarios, local on-ramp and off-ramp comparisons, KYC and no-KYC trade-offs where appropriate, local resource maps, circular economies, community use cases, and practical workshops using tools that are relevant in the local context.

Spending is one possible practice, but Block 4 is broader than spending. Bitcoin as money may also include earning, saving, receiving, mapping resources, finding local communities, or solving local monetary problems.

Core question:

How can Bitcoin function as money in this community?

Learner outcome:

Learners can identify beginner-appropriate ways Bitcoin can function as money in local daily life.

# Learning Approach

The 2026.10 Diploma is designed for active learning.

The course should combine:

- short explanations;
- guided discussion;
- reflection;
- practical demonstrations;
- scenario analysis;
- safety checklists;
- local examples;
- learner explanation and review.

The Diploma should teach learners how to think, not what to think. It should help learners compare claims, understand trade-offs, ask better questions, and reason independently.

Practical learning is included in every block, but practical tasks should remain beginner-appropriate and safety-oriented. The course should never imply financial, legal, tax, or investment advice.

# Materials And Outputs

The 2026.10 Diploma package is expected to include:

- Student Book;
- offline print/PDF package;
- online PDF package;
- Educator Guide;
- Activity Bank;
- Custom / Bitcoin as Money templates;
- assessment framework;
- public stakeholder brief and keynote material;
- translation-ready Markdown source files;
- website-ready source files;
- future delivery-branch notes.

The Student Book should carry the learner path. The Educator Guide should help teachers facilitate, pace, adapt, and protect learner safety. The Activity Bank should provide modular exercises that educators can use inside the Diploma or as standalone learning experiences.

---

# Assessment And Certification

Assessment in the redesigned Diploma is lightweight and beginner-focused.

The purpose is to show whether learners can explain, discuss, and apply beginner concepts safely. Evidence may include short reflections, learner explanations, group presentations, practical demonstrations, safety checklists, or local-context outputs.

A simple final assessment may ask learners to answer:

1. What problem does fiat money create?
2. Why was Bitcoin created?
3. What does self-custody responsibility mean?
4. How might Bitcoin function as money in this local context?

Certification is separate from this redesign. The 2026.10 work may prepare better foundations for future certification, but it does not define certification governance, credential infrastructure, or on-chain verification systems.

---

# Localization And Translation

The Diploma is intended for global, community-led use.

The English source should be drafted first, then reviewed for beginner clarity, simple language, consistent terminology, tone, style, and translation readiness before translation or publication.

Local educators may adapt examples, tools, activities, and Custom modules. Core outcomes should remain consistent across languages and delivery modes.

Named wallets, exchanges, services, maps, and local resources should be treated as local examples unless explicitly approved for global publication.

---

# Delivery Model

The first implementation mode is in-person and offline-capable.

The course is designed to work as:

- one full-day intensive;
- four 2-hour sessions;
- eight 1-hour sessions;
- another local schedule that preserves the four-block sequence.

Future delivery branches may adapt pacing, media, learner support, and evidence of learning. They should not create separate outcome standards.

The Diploma benefits from collaborators who can support:

- translation and localization;

---

# Current Boundaries

The 2026.10 Diploma is a beginner pathway.

It should not promise:

- financial advice;
- trading or investment outcomes;
- independent high-stakes custody mastery;
- advanced technical mastery;
- legal or tax guidance;
- global endorsement of named tools or services;
- completed certification infrastructure.

Advanced topics should be preserved for future pathways rather than forced into the 8-hour beginner course.

---

# Working Principle

The 2026.10 Bitcoin Diploma should be clear enough for a beginner, useful enough for an educator, flexible enough for local communities, and disciplined enough to scale.

It is a shorter and stronger version of the same mission: helping people understand Bitcoin, money, responsibility, and freedom through open-source education.
