# Bitcoin Diploma Redesign Research Framework

## Purpose

This research process exists to support redesign decisions using evidence rather than intuition.

The goal is not to validate assumptions.

The goal is to identify evidence that supports, challenges, or refines current design decisions.

Every major redesign decision should be traceable:

Decision → Evidence → Confidence Level → Action

# Internal Purpose Definitions

These are internal stakeholder definitions of what the Bitcoin Diploma is for. Treat them as qualitative internal evidence for purpose, tone, and design direction, not as final public copy.

## Colleague Responses

> Arnold - bring structured and foundational concepts of bitcoin education to students from all corners of the world.

> Quentin - to arm students with the knowledge they need to see past the broken fiat system and to give them the tools to regain sovereignty and agency

> Gabe - Introduce people to Bitcoin, with a newfound knowledge about economics and monetary history. Once properly introduced, the Bitcoin Diploma exists to guide individuals through the values of Bitcoin from an objective point of view, not subjective. Goes back to "we don't teach you what to think, we teach you how to think and provide you the facts/information to make a decision for your financial future"

> John - teach not just about money, but also about sovereignty.

## Synthesis

The internal definitions converge on five purpose themes:

1. The Diploma should provide structured, foundational Bitcoin education.
2. The Diploma should be globally accessible and useful across local contexts.
3. The Diploma should teach monetary history, economics, fiat-system critique, and Bitcoin together rather than presenting Bitcoin as an isolated technology.
4. The Diploma should support sovereignty, agency, and financial responsibility without becoming investment advice.
5. The Diploma should teach learners how to think, not what to think, by presenting facts, trade-offs, and reasoning tools.

## Where This Is Addressed

This purpose language is already present in several source and planning documents:

- `ref/03_bd_202604_spec.md` states the one-sentence purpose as a structured foundation in Bitcoin, money, financial systems, and digital sovereignty. It also names the principle "teach how to think, not what to think" and describes the main outcome as helping learners evaluate Bitcoin intelligently and participate in financial sovereignty.
- `ref/02_bd_202604_educator_guide.md` directly frames the educator role around teaching how to think, sharpening questions, and helping students form their own conclusions.
- `ref/README.md` describes the Diploma as a comprehensive and structured introduction to Bitcoin, focused on understanding, sovereignty, and long-term thinking rather than speculation or trading.
- `public/MFB-BD-Keynote.md` repeats the redesign principle that learners should leave with better questions, clearer reasoning, and more confidence.
- `workspace/04_design.md` already owns this as a design principle under "Teach How To Think, Not What To Think" and as part of thematic coherence around money, economics, technology, Bitcoin, sovereignty, and responsibility.
- `workspace/07_contents.md` identifies the future student-facing location for this idea in "Our mission," "What we stand for," and "Why this Diploma exists."

## Implementation Implications

Use these internal definitions to validate and sharpen existing redesign language rather than adding a separate new mission.

Implementation locations:

1. `workspace/02_research.md`: preserve the internal definitions as stakeholder evidence.
2. `workspace/04_design.md`: use the synthesis as a purpose anchor for design decisions.
3. `workspace/07_contents.md`: later translate the synthesis into student-facing opening material about why the Diploma exists.
4. `workspace/05_objectives.md` and `workspace/12_assessment.md`: keep sovereignty, agency, and independent reasoning visible in objectives and evidence of learning without turning them into ideological agreement.

Recommended working definition:

The Bitcoin Diploma exists to provide structured, foundational, globally accessible Bitcoin education that helps learners understand money, fiat systems, Bitcoin, sovereignty, and responsibility so they can reason independently about their financial future.

# Internal Source Review: Bitcoin For Organizations

`workspace/mergefromorganizations.md` and `workspace/advancedorganizations.md` reviewed the retiring `ref/bitcoin-for-organizations/en` pathway on 2026-06-26.

Treat this as internal curriculum evidence, not as external research. The review supports the current redesign direction in three ways:

1. It confirms that most Organizations content is too advanced for the 8-hour beginner Diploma.
2. It identifies beginner-safe concepts that clarify existing Core objectives: CBDCs as digital fiat, digital scarcity, double spending, short technical lineage, credible scarcity, users/nodes/miners/developers, confirmations, block explorer verification, public-ledger privacy, on-chain versus Lightning layers, and local payment scenarios.
3. It provides educator and Activity Bank material for misconception handling, claim/evidence/trade-off discussion, local payment path comparison, community donation scenarios, remittance scenarios, and circular-economy mapping.

Research and publication caution:

- Current-data claims from Organizations require verification before public use. This includes CBDC status, policy claims, legal restrictions, tax and accounting rules, energy claims, market metrics, Lightning usage, company examples, merchant maps, and named tools or services.
- Strategic, operational, investment, treasury, legal, policy, and sector-specific Organizations material should become Advanced source material, not beginner Core content.
- Beginner use should favor guided questions and scenario prompts over institutional recommendations or predictions.

# Final Recommendations

The research package should support, test, or challenge the following redesign decisions. Until external sources are filled in, these should be treated as research priorities and internal review implications rather than completed evidence claims.

## Strong Internal Direction

1. Reduce the beginner pathway to an approximately 8-hour 4-block structure.
2. Separate beginner, Custom, and Advanced material to reduce cognitive overload.
3. Increase active learning through a dedicated Activity Bank and educator facilitation support.
4. Design source files for multilingual transfer from the beginning.
5. Preserve an offline-capable in-person PDF path while documenting future delivery branches.
6. Keep print QR codes sparse and tied to stable, high-value destinations.

---

## Evidence Needed Before Public Claims

1. Adult learning evidence for shorter, active, in-person learning experiences.
2. Curriculum design evidence for modular learning, spiral review, and competency alignment.
3. Educator support evidence showing why facilitator guides, timing support, and activity banks improve consistency.
4. Evidence or expert review for multilingual source-writing practices.
5. Evidence for online, self-paced, web, app, and cross-modal adaptations before making claims about those modes.

---

## Areas Requiring More Research

1. Completion and dropout patterns in comparable community education programs.
2. Low-connectivity and mobile-first learning constraints.
3. Cross-cultural adaptation risks for money, custody, and local Bitcoin examples.
4. Print usability and QR-code restraint.
5. Local expert-led practice models and safety boundaries.

---

## Decisions Safe To Make Now

1. Use the 4-block model as the working architecture.
2. Keep Advanced material outside the Core beginner pathway.
3. Build the Educator Guide and Activity Bank as required parts of the learning ecosystem.
4. Write in plain, consistent, translation-ready language.
5. Treat spending as one possible Block 4 practice, not the full global definition of Block 4.
6. Use QR codes sparingly in printed learner materials.

---

## Decisions That Should Wait

1. Certification design and verification governance.
2. Final Advanced module objectives.
3. Public claims that depend on external research until sources are added.
4. App implementation and cross-modal continuation design.
5. Final online cohort and self-paced evidence requirements.

## Hackfest 2023 Repository Research

### Source

Repository: https://github.com/StevenBlack/hackfest

This repository contains notes and assets from the Hackfest 2023 presentation “Bitcoin: Cool et pas à peu près,” a 45-minute French-language session designed to explain Bitcoin while highlighting technical facets.

Reviewed files from the Hackfest repository:

- README
- Hackfest btc-epistemology PDF
- `btc-epistemology.xlsx`
- Hackfest btc-blockchain-pace PDF
- Hackfest btc-halving PDF
- `btc-epistemology.nb`
- `btc-blockchain-pace.nb`
- `btc-halving.nb`
- selected diagrams and concept maps in `assets/`

### Relevant Findings

- Bitcoin has high learner “activation energy.” The presentation frames Bitcoin learning as requiring an initial cognitive push before later concepts become easier to connect.
- Bitcoin is difficult to teach linearly because key concepts are interdependent. The Hackfest README, btc-epistemology spreadsheet, and base-concepts diagrams show prerequisite relationships, graph layouts, layered layouts, and concept clusters.
- A concept map can make hidden dependencies visible. The epistemology material treats Bitcoin topics as a network rather than a simple list.
- Concepts can be prioritized by comparing prerequisite value against complexity. In the btc-epistemology spreadsheet, high-unlocking concepts include `satoshi`, `hashing`, `proof of work`, `blockchain`, `receive address`, `tx fee`, and `block time`.
- Satoshis are a useful early technical concept. The README and Hackfest eight-decimals diagram show how satoshis address unit bias and make Bitcoin amounts more concrete.
- The technical sequence `hashing -> blocks/blockchain -> proof of work -> mining` is clean and teachable when supported by demonstration rather than abstract lecture.
- Block time, mining difficulty, difficulty adjustment, halving, and issuance can be presented as Bitcoin’s “heartbeat,” using pacing and reward schedules to connect technical mechanisms to monetary issuance.
- Live tools can support observation-based learning. The README references Anders Brownworth’s blockchain demo and `mempool.space` as ways to show hashing, blocks, proof of work, mining, and live network activity.
- Some technical claims are useful for demystification. The repository argues that Bitcoin’s math can be made accessible and that educators should avoid unnecessary mystique around cryptography.

### Application to Diploma Redesign

Module sequencing:

- Use prerequisite structure to guide sequencing inside the 4-block Diploma.
- Place high-unlocking, low-complexity concepts early when they help later ideas make sense.
- Use `satoshi` before transaction amounts, fees, rewards, and issuance.
- Use `hashing` before blocks, proof of work, mining, and difficulty.
- Keep deeper mining, mempool, UTXO, issuance, and difficulty-adjustment detail outside the required beginner core unless needed for a specific local or Advanced pathway.

Concept maps:

- Create a Diploma concept map showing which ideas unlock later understanding.
- Build a prerequisite table for Core, Custom, and Advanced content.
- Use the map to identify concepts that appear simple but have high teaching value.

Learner prerequisites:

- Assume learners may not understand why technical topics are connected.
- Make dependencies visible before introducing dense explanations.
- Reduce learner overload by naming what learners do not need to master yet.

Technical explanations:

- Teach technical Bitcoin concepts without pretending they are simple.
- Avoid presenting Bitcoin as mysterious or mathematically unreachable.
- Use concrete integer-based examples such as satoshis, block rewards, and transaction amounts.
- Keep bit-shifting, detailed issuance math, mempool mechanics, and difficulty-adjustment mechanics as enrichment unless the learner pathway requires them.

Educator guide activities:

- Add demonstration scripts for hashing, blocks, proof of work, mining, and mempool observation.
- Include prompts that ask learners what changed, what stayed fixed, and what the network is trying to regulate.
- Provide “demo before abstraction” guidance so educators show a mechanism before naming the theory.

Live demonstrations:

- Consider a technical demonstration bank that includes Anders Brownworth’s blockchain demo, `mempool.space`, block-time visualizations, and halving/issuance diagrams.
- Include offline or low-connectivity alternatives for each live tool.
- Treat live network statistics as examples to observe, not as fixed curriculum facts.

Assessment design:

- Ask learners to explain simple prerequisite chains, such as satoshi to fee, hashing to proof of work, or block time to difficulty adjustment.
- Use concept-map completion as formative assessment.
- Use short observation tasks: learners inspect a block explorer, identify a block, find a fee, or explain what miners are competing to do.
- Assess beginner understanding of relationships, not memorization of technical trivia.

### Recommended Design Principles

- Teach by prerequisite structure, not topic enthusiasm.
- Start with high-unlocking, low-complexity concepts.
- Use demonstrations before abstraction.
- Avoid unnecessary technical mystique.
- Make concept dependencies visible.
- Separate core beginner concepts from Advanced technical enrichment.
- Use live tools for observation, not for current-data claims.
- Teach technical topics honestly without overloading beginners.

### Candidate Additions to Scope

- Diploma concept map
- Core prerequisite table
- Core / Custom / Advanced dependency map
- Technical demonstration bank
- Educator demo scripts for hashing, blocks, proof of work, mining, mempool observation, block pace, and halving
- Offline alternatives for live technical demonstrations
- Optional Advanced technical appendix
- Concept-map formative assessment tasks

### Notes / Warnings

- Some repository material is too technical for the Core beginner Diploma.
- Mining statistics and energy comparisons from 2023 should not be used as current data unless clearly marked as historical examples.
- The repo is best used as a curriculum design reference, not as direct Diploma content.
- The concept-scoring approach is useful, but it should be validated against actual learner needs, educator constraints, and the 8-hour Diploma scope.
- Do not copy the presentation wholesale; extract sequencing, demonstration, and curriculum-design insights.
