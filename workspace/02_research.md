# Bitcoin Diploma Redesign Research Framework

## Purpose

This research process exists to support redesign decisions using evidence rather than intuition.

The goal is not to validate assumptions.

The goal is to identify evidence that supports, challenges, or refines current design decisions.

Every major redesign decision should be traceable:

Decision → Evidence → Confidence Level → Action

# External Evidence Review Synthesis

## Review record

Date: 2026-08-12

Inputs: rapid evidence reviews and a combined decision summary, retained as reviewed inputs rather than local repository files.

These are rapid evidence reviews, not registered systematic reviews. They draw mainly from adjacent research in adult learning, cognitive science, financial education, digital skills, professional assessment, educator development, accessibility, translation, and delivery-mode design. Findings from those settings do not transfer automatically to a mixed-age, community-led Bitcoin programme.

## Executive conclusion

The three reviews agree that the redesign is moving in a defensible direction. Strong adjacent evidence supports active participation, guided beginner practice, retrieval distributed across sessions, competency–activity–assessment alignment, direct observation of practical skills, task-specific assessment criteria, educator preparation and calibration, human-reviewed localisation, and a complete offline-capable instructional path.

The evidence supports these design principles, but it does not validate the Bitcoin Diploma's exact numbers. Eight hours, four modules, four two-hour sessions, and the 50/50 theory-practice allocation are programme choices, not proven optima. Competence, access, and safety should take priority over treating a timetable or percentage as research-established.

**Programme policy update, 2026-08-19:** The Bitcoin Diploma has adopted two educator-evaluated 50% components: theoretical competency demonstrated through explanation, comparison, or application, and directly observed practical competency. The app is optional self-assessment only and cannot provide assessment evidence. This is a governance decision to implement and evaluate, not a ratio established as optimal by the research reviewed here. Low-tech and offline-capable delivery includes learning, assessment, completion, and certification.

The largest evidence gap is Bitcoin-specific. None of the reviews located a peer-reviewed or genuinely independent outcome evaluation of a beginner, community-led Bitcoin course. Public programme materials can describe curricula, delivery, and reach, but they cannot establish learning, retention, safe performance, or behaviour change. The Bitcoin Diploma should help close this gap through transparent pilot evaluation rather than effectiveness claims.

## Shared programme-design implications

- Prefer four two-hour meetings as the first spaced-delivery hypothesis where feasible, but do not make that schedule universal.
- Use a recurring module repertoire: reconnect or retrieve prior learning; explain or demonstrate a manageable step; provide guided processing or practice; move toward a scenario, explanation, or supported performance; collect evidence; give specific feedback; and close with retrieval or reflection.
- Keep learners active in every module. "Active" means purposeful processing and supported practice, not minimally guided discovery.
- Use several evidence channels. App completion, attendance, confidence, enjoyment, self-report, or a quiz alone cannot demonstrate practical competence.
- Keep theoretical and practical assessment as non-compensatory certification gates. Treat 50/50 as the approved administrative or reporting allocation, not as a research-proven optimum.
- Implement the educator-led 50/50 theory-practice allocation as programme policy while evaluating assessment validity, access, differential completion, and learner support needs. Keep app self-checks optional and separate from certification evidence.
- Publish a one-page Bitcoin Diploma/Module 4 contract so educators and translators know what they may adapt and what requires formal review.
- Treat translation and mode conversion as versioned curriculum changes, especially when assessment meaning, safety, or learner evidence may change.
- Keep print and offline instructional materials complete without scanning. Optional app self-checks must not become hidden instructional prerequisites or certification evidence.

## Claims the evidence does not support

- Eight hours is sufficient for every learner group, language, cohort size, or assessed competency.
- Four equal sessions or four two-hour sessions outperform every alternative schedule.
- Lecture should occupy one exact percentage of each session.
- The 50/50 allocation improves validity, reliability, or access.
- No delivery mode is universally superior.
- QR codes improve learning.
- Completing Bitcoin education causes Bitcoin adoption, financial improvement, ideological agreement, or durable behaviour change.
- Evidence from formal education, professional training, financial literacy, or digital skills predicts a specific Bitcoin Diploma effect size.
- Organisational reach, enrolment, completion, certificate, or translation counts establish programme effectiveness.

## Suggested eight-hour pathway: feasibility and governance risk

The Educator Guide's operational assessment protocol defines seven assessed certification competencies, mirrored for planning in `05_competencies.md`, with required `C`-ID observable criteria and two formative cross-module competencies, `F1` and `F2`, which are not certificate gates. Several required performances are observed by an educator. The minimum eight-hour pathway must account for access checks, instruction, guided practice, discussion, transitions, breaks, theoretical evidence, direct observation, feedback, accommodations, recovery, and reassessment. Educators extend the route whenever compatible criteria cannot be validly integrated into coherent scenarios or the learner-to-educator model does not provide enough observation capacity.

Before locking scope, create and field-time a competency blueprint containing:

- prerequisite knowledge
- teaching or demonstration time
- supported practice time
- the smallest valid evidence matching the competency's observable action
- opportunities to integrate compatible competencies without hiding a criterion
- learner-minutes of direct observation and available qualified educator
- accessibility and low-connectivity alternatives
- feedback, failed attempts, and reassessment time; and
- the response to device, connection, audiovisual, or live-service failure.

Estimate minimum observation burden as:

`learner count × observed minutes per learner ÷ simultaneous qualified educator`

Then add setup, rotations, evidence recording, feedback, accommodations, failed attempts, and reassessment. If the complete pathway does not fit, first remove duplication and integrate compatible evidence, then add learner-contact or separately scheduled supported assessment time. Do not convert a performance competency to a quiz or waive a safety-critical criterion to protect the clock.

The timing decision is fixed: eight hours is the minimum and shortest planned route, with no upper limit. The advertised course pathway includes teaching, practice, assessment, feedback, and recording. Required assessment is not a hidden extra. Educators extend the schedule to give learners the time needed to acquire and demonstrate the required competencies; testing should report planned and actual time so future revisions can improve pacing without weakening competencies to protect the clock.

## Minimum pilot and release evidence

The October 2026 release should include a common, proportionate, privacy-minimised evaluation:

- competency-aligned pre-course and immediate post-course checks using equivalent, not identical, forms
- a common observed practical scenario with analytic criteria
- attendance by module, delivery mode, implementation deviations, access failures, practice attempts, feedback, and reassessment data
- a delayed retrieval and safety-transfer check, ideally after 8–12 weeks
- educator preparation time, guide usability, scoring rationale, incidents, and near misses
- double-scoring of a planned sample to estimate educator agreement
- separate invitation, enrolment, start, completion, assessment, and delayed-follow-up denominators; and
- aggregate results, uncertainty, missing data, subgroup gaps, adverse and null findings, and limitations.

Do not collect seed phrases, private keys, wallet credentials, unnecessary addresses or transaction histories, balances, or unnecessary personal financial information.

Do not scale a language and delivery-mode combination until every required competency has a tested teach–practice–assess path, essential content has a low-connectivity alternative, learner testing finds no unresolved safety-critical misconception, educators have calibrated, practical observation and reassessment fit the cohort model, accessibility has been tested, translation has been reviewed, and incidents and appeals have an owner.

## Review quality and use

- Use the Codex review as the primary decision anchor because it has the clearest verification procedure, evidence labels, uncertainty statements, internal-workspace audit, feasibility analysis, and release-evaluation proposal.
- Use the Claude review as strong corroboration and practical synthesis. It marks some citations for direct verification and identifies search areas not covered.
- Use the Gemini review for directional corroboration and implementation ideas. Verify Gemini-only claims before using them in curriculum governance or public materials because its verification trail is less transparent and some claims are more specific than the cited evidence appears to support.

Confidence is highest where strong adjacent evidence converges: active participation, retrieval and spacing, novice guidance, competency-assessment alignment, direct evidence for practical skills, calibrated task-specific criteria, human-reviewed localisation, and offline-capable instruction. Confidence is lowest for Bitcoin-specific learning evidence, exact time and assessment allocations, educator-assessment reliability, QR code learning effects, self-paced completion, and long-term behaviour change.

# Research Versus Current Curriculum Comparison

## Audit scope

Audit date: 2026-08-19

This comparison applies the complete research file to the current workspace. It reviewed the active architecture, competency framework, Student Guide, activities, Educator Guide plan and draft, assessment and certification frameworks, lexicon, source register, visual index, publication file, scope, release to-do, and consistency report.

The comparison uses four status labels:

- **Implemented:** the workspace contains a usable curriculum or delivery feature, not only a statement of intent.
- **Partly implemented:** the principle is present, but one or more artifacts, procedures, owners, or tests needed to make it reliable are missing.
- **Not operational:** the workspace names the need, but an educator, editor, translator, or evaluator could not yet carry it out consistently.
- **Conflicted:** current files communicate materially different expectations.

## Overall judgement

The project is not ignoring the main research findings at the level of curriculum intent. The architecture, Student Guide, and especially the Educator Guide draft already apply active learning, beginner guidance, discussion, retrieval, practical safety, offline-capable delivery, direct observation, feedback, reassessment, localisation boundaries, and non-advisory framing.

The larger risk is the gap between **policy and operational evidence**. The workspace often says that a blueprint, rubric, equivalent variant, calibration exercise, pilot, accessibility check, translation review, or release control is required without yet providing the finished artifact or assigning it to a release gate. If the Bitcoin Diploma were delivered from the current package, educator quality and local effort could compensate for these gaps in some cohorts, but results and certificate meaning could vary substantially across educators, languages, cohort sizes, and delivery conditions.

The most important competency-changing work is therefore not adding more learner content. It is completing the minimum system that makes the existing competencies teachable, assessable, accessible, and testable within the available time.

## High-impact findings the current project is still underweighting

### 1. A finished guide is not evidence that the course works

The workspace treats feedback and iteration as a milestone, but it does not yet treat end-to-end pilot evidence as a release dependency. This matters because the largest evidence gap in the field is the absence of independent Bitcoin-education outcomes. A strong October release should include at least a usability and safety pilot, even if it cannot yet make a causal effectiveness claim.

The pilot should test the complete system rather than isolated chapters: learner comprehension, teaching time, supported practice, direct observation, educator workload, accessibility, translation friction, reassessment, delayed retention, and certificate-decision consistency.

### 2. The assessment system needs artifacts, not more policy prose

The principle of two non-compensatory components is now clear. The Educator Guide operational assessment protocol provides the crosswalk, approved individual-evidence tasks, criteria, prompt boundaries, records, calibration process, moderation, and reassessment procedure. Certificate validity now depends on piloting the protocol, creating local calibration exemplars, and establishing the outstanding record-governance controls.

### 3. The minimum eight-hour pathway is constrained primarily by observation capacity

Content density is a concern, but educator observation is the harder constraint. Every learner seeking certification must produce individually identifiable evidence across required theoretical competencies and directly observed Module 3 and Module 4 performance. A schedule that works for six learners with two qualified educators may fail for twenty learners with one educator even when the lesson content is unchanged.

The delivery model should therefore publish cohort and educator assumptions, not only session length.

### 4. Translation quality is part of assessment validity

Translation is currently treated mainly as an editorial workflow. Because learners are assessed through explanation, comparison, safety judgement, and practical instructions, translation can change the construct or difficulty of an assessment. Language approval should therefore include assessment review and learner testing, not only proofreading and subject-matter review.

### 5. Accessibility needs a production standard and tested task variants

The curriculum has a strong accessibility promise, but “offline-capable” and “accessible” are not interchangeable. A PDF may work without a connection and still fail reading order, contrast, navigation, form usability, screen-reader interpretation, large-print use, or accessible assessment. The Educator Guide operational assessment protocol now defines equivalent assessment routes; they still require representative-user testing before certificated delivery.

### 6. Source maintenance is part of educator support

The Educator Guide correctly asks local educators to verify changing local tools and rules. It should not make them re-establish shared Bitcoin Diploma economic, historical, technical, or safety claims for every cohort. A maintained shared Bitcoin Diploma claim register would reduce educator burden, prevent drift, and make translations easier to update.

## Easily achievable modifications with disproportionate value

These changes can be made before major layout or platform work:

1. **Extend the activity template.** Add prerequisites, model or worked example, likely misconceptions, minimum and extended timing, supported-practice steps, guidance fading, offline fallback, accessibility variant, permitted prompts, pilot status, and revision owner.
2. **Create the one-page four-module Bitcoin Diploma contract.** State that educators curate Module 4 within the Bitcoin Diploma using local subject-matter expertise, while the approved `C7.1` and `C7.2` evidence remains required for certification.
3. **Synchronize learner promises.** Replace weaker Module 3 and Module 4 learner-facing verbs with the exact explain-and-demonstrate standard already owned by `05_competencies.md`.
4. **Add stable schemas before adding content.** Define the row formats for the competency crosswalk, claim register, controlled lexicon, activity/task records, and pilot log so future work accumulates consistently.
5. **Extract educator run sheets.** Keep the detailed educator reference, but provide one concise preparation-and-delivery sheet per module plus a separate assessment cue card.
6. **Add one post-course retrieval touchpoint.** Provide an offline-ready short prompt set and educator instructions; do not make it a hidden certification gate.
7. **Add pilot status to every required task.** Record learner group, language, mode, educator experience, planned and actual time, access failures, safety events, completion, common errors, and revision decision.
8. **Turn evaluation into scheduled work.** Add the pilot protocol and go/no-go review to `03_scope.md` and `00_todo.md` rather than leaving evaluation only inside this research file.
9. **Replace the false publication-spec role.** Preserve the useful overview under an appropriate name and make `16_spec.md` enforce production, accessibility, rights, citation, export, QA, sign-off, release, and maintenance rules.
10. **Define minimum data protections before pilots.** A one-page approved data map and incident route is more valuable now than a complex future credential design.

## Recommended execution order

1. Resolve the eight-hour governance rule and Module 4 requirement language.
2. Pilot the competency-to-evidence-and-time crosswalk.
3. Approve the selected local `P7` variant and create calibration exemplars.
4. Field-time the complete route and test educator capacity.
5. Run independent educator usability, learner comprehension, accessibility, translation, and safety pilots.
6. Revise the Student and Educator Guides using observed failures and friction.
7. Complete source controls, data protections, and proportionate release checks without changing `16_spec.md` from its overview role.
8. Publish the evaluation protocol and limitations with the release; publish results when sufficient data exists.

## Decision implication

The research does not justify expanding the beginner syllabus. It supports finishing, simplifying, and testing the system around the existing competencies and observable criteria. The highest-quality Student and Educator Guides will come from a verified competency chain, a timed delivery route, usable educator tools, safe and equivalent assessment variants, controlled language and sources, and observed learner and educator performance—not from adding more explanatory prose.

## Synthesis

The internal definitions converge on five purpose themes:

1. The Bitcoin Diploma should provide structured, foundational Bitcoin education.
2. The Bitcoin Diploma should be globally accessible and useful across local contexts.

## Internal Evidence: 2024 Teacher Survey

Source: `../input/20260724_Teachers Survey (Responses) - Form Responses 1.csv`.

This file contains eight Bitcoin Diploma educator responses collected in November 2024. It is a small, self-selected internal sample, and some activity-selection answers are unreliable because the form required respondents to mark an activity even when they used all of them. Treat it as directional qualitative evidence, not as a representative evaluation or a basis for numerical targets.

### Signals

- All eight respondents had used the Lesson Plan Guide. Its mean learning rating was 3.25 out of 5; the Activities Guide mean was 3.38 out of 5; onboarding mean was 3.88 out of 5. This supports the existing direction to make the Educator Guide a practical facilitation and educator tool, and to strengthen educator preparation and calibration.
- Seven respondents said activity time and pace were “Just Right”; one said “Too Fast.” Keep eight hours as the minimum route, protect discussion, practice, review, and assessment time, and extend delivery through multi-session options whenever learners need it.
- The clearest positive activity signals were the wallet-creation exercise (six mentions), the money discussion using Monopoly bills (five), and block-explorer work (five). These are useful candidates for pilot priority, subject to competency alignment, safety, and low-connectivity alternatives.
- Consensus, barter, video, hashing, relay-race, and transaction role-play activities were often skipped or rated among the least effective. These results do not establish that an activity should be removed: the survey does not distinguish availability, educator confidence, audience, or delivery conditions. They do establish a need to test the activity’s purpose, instructions, timing, materials, and fallback form before retaining it as a recommended item.
- One educator reported no audiovisual equipment and that assigned videos were not watched. Video may be an optional enhancement, never a required dependency; each activity needs a print, spoken, diagram, or facilitator-led alternative.
- Requests included simpler explanations of technical content, additional educator training and peer exchange, a graduate follow-on community, and deeper nodes, mining, hashing, UTXOs, Lightning, and Nostr content. The first two inform educator-support and pathway planning. The technical requests support the existing Advanced boundary; they do not expand the beginner Bitcoin Diploma.

# Final Recommendations

The external reviews now support several design principles while leaving the exact programme parameters as pilot hypotheses. The following statuses replace the earlier assumption that all listed decisions were equally safe.

## Evidence-Informed Direction

1. Use the minimum 8-hour, 4-module pathway as the working pilot baseline, with no upper limit and additional time or support whenever learners need it.
2. Separate the complete four-module Bitcoin Diploma, including educator-curated Module 4, from Advanced material to reduce cognitive overload.
3. Require active learning, guided practice, retrieval, feedback, and competency-aligned evidence throughout the programme.
4. Build the Educator Guide, activities on the [Resources Hub](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/resources/), educator calibration, and reassessment system as required implementation infrastructure.
5. Design source files for multilingual transfer, human review, versioning, and local comprehension testing from the beginning.
6. Preserve a complete offline-capable instructional and educator-assessment path; optional app self-checks must never become a certification requirement.
7. Keep print QR codes sparse, optional, accessible, and tied to controlled, maintained destinations.
8. Make release evaluation part of the redesign rather than adding it after publication.

---

## Evidence Limits Before Public Claims

1. No located Bitcoin-specific independent evaluation establishes the Bitcoin Diploma's effectiveness.
2. No located evidence establishes that eight hours is universally sufficient or that four equal sessions are optimal.
3. No located evidence validates the 50/50 theory-practice allocation.
4. Delivery-mode claims require testing of the actual materials, supports, interaction, and evidence route in that mode.
5. Translation equivalence requires local human review and learner testing; source-language quality alone is insufficient.
6. Practical-assessment reliability requires measured educator agreement, not the existence of a rubric alone.
7. Learning, reach, completion, satisfaction, certification, adoption, and financial outcomes are different constructs and must not be reported as interchangeable.
8. Behavioural or long-term claims require delayed follow-up and, for causal claims, a credible comparison design.

---

1. Independent evaluation of beginner Bitcoin education.
2. Completion, attrition, retention, and transfer in comparable community programmes.
3. Whether every required competency can be taught, practised, assessed, and reassessed safely within the intended pathway.
4. Educator agreement and moderation feasibility across distributed educators.
5. Low-connectivity, mobile-first, self-paced, and remote practical-assessment constraints.
6. Cross-cultural and cross-language interpretation of money, custody, privacy, risk, and local Bitcoin examples.
7. Accessibility and comprehension across teenagers, adults, older adults, varied literacy, and varied digital confidence.
8. Safe local-practice models that do not require real secrets, meaningful-value transactions, or named-service recommendations.

### Relevant Findings

- Bitcoin has high learner “activation energy.” The presentation frames Bitcoin learning as requiring an initial cognitive push before later concepts become easier to connect.
- Bitcoin is difficult to teach linearly because key concepts are interdependent. The Hackfest README, btc-epistemology spreadsheet, and base-concepts diagrams show prerequisite relationships, graph layouts, layered layouts, and concept clusters.
- A concept map can make hidden dependencies visible. The epistemology material treats Bitcoin topics as a network rather than a simple list.
- Concepts can be prioritized by comparing prerequisite value against complexity. In the btc-epistemology spreadsheet, high-unlocking concepts include `satoshi`, `hashing`, `proof of work`, `blockchain`, `receive address`, `tx fee`, and `block time`.
- Satoshis are a useful early technical concept. The README and Hackfest eight-decimals diagram show how satoshis address unit bias and make Bitcoin amounts more concrete.
- The technical sequence `hashing -> blocks/blockchain -> proof of work -> mining` is clean and teachable when supported by demonstration rather than abstract lecture.
- Block time, mining difficulty, difficulty adjustment, halving, and issuance can be presented as Bitcoin’s “heartbeat,” using pacing and reward schedules to connect technical mechanisms to monetary issuance.
- Live tools can support observation-based learning. The README references Anders Brownworth’s blockchain demo and `mempool.space` as ways to show hashing, blocks, proof of work, mining, and live network activity.
- Some technical claims are useful for demystification. The repository argues that Bitcoin’s math can be made accessible and that educators should avoid unnecessary mystique around cryptography.

### Application to Bitcoin Diploma Redesign

Module sequencing:

- Use prerequisite structure to guide sequencing inside the 4-module Bitcoin Diploma.
- Place high-unlocking, low-complexity concepts early when they help later ideas make sense.
- Use `satoshi` before transaction amounts, fees, rewards, and issuance.
- Use `hashing` before blocks, proof of work, mining, and difficulty.
- Keep deeper mining, mempool, UTXO, issuance, and difficulty-adjustment detail outside the required beginner Bitcoin Diploma unless needed for a specific local or Advanced pathway.

Concept maps:

- Create a Bitcoin Diploma concept map showing which ideas enable later explanation or application.
- Build a prerequisite table for the four-module Bitcoin Diploma, educator-curated Module 4 within it, and Advanced content.
- Use the map to identify concepts that appear simple but have high teaching value.

Learner prerequisites:

- Assume learners may not yet see why technical topics are connected.
- Make dependencies visible before introducing dense explanations.
- Reduce learner overload by naming what learners do not need to master yet.

Technical explanations:

- Teach technical Bitcoin concepts without pretending they are simple.
- Avoid presenting Bitcoin as mysterious or mathematically unreachable.
- Use concrete integer-based examples such as satoshis, block rewards, and transaction amounts.

Assessment design:

- Ask learners to explain simple prerequisite chains, such as satoshi to fee, hashing to proof of work, or block time to difficulty adjustment.
- Use concept-map completion as formative assessment.
- Use short observation tasks: learners inspect a block explorer, identify a block, find a fee, or explain what miners are competing to do.
- Assess a beginner's ability to explain relationships, not memorization of technical trivia.

### Recommended Design Principles

- Teach by prerequisite structure, not topic enthusiasm.
- Start with high-unlocking, low-complexity concepts.
- Use demonstrations before abstraction.
- Avoid unnecessary technical mystique.
- Make concept dependencies visible.
- Separate required beginner Bitcoin Diploma concepts from Advanced technical enrichment.
- Use live tools for observation, not for current-data claims.
- Teach technical topics honestly without overloading beginners.
