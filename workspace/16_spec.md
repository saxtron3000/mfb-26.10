# 2026.10 Bitcoin Diploma Overview

## Purpose

This document is a short, standalone overview of the planned 2026.10 Bitcoin Diploma for internal staff, educators, partners, funders, and external collaborators. In this workspace, 2026.10 refers to the October 2026 version of the Diploma.

The Bitcoin Diploma is My First Bitcoin's flagship beginner education program. It is designed to help people explain what money is, why Bitcoin was created, how Bitcoin works in plain language, and how to approach Bitcoin use with personal responsibility and safety.

The 2026.10 version is a redesign of the existing Diploma. It is being reorganized, not replaced. The redesign preserves the Diploma's open-source, community-led, Bitcoin-only identity while making the learning path shorter, clearer, more practical, easier to teach, and easier to adapt across communities.

---

# Short Summary

The 2026.10 Bitcoin Diploma is a beginner course organized into four modules, with a minimum 8-hour delivery pathway that includes teaching, practice, assessment, feedback, and recording. Eight hours is the shortest planned route, with no upper limit when learners need more time:

1. **Problem:** Understanding Money
2. **Solution:** How Bitcoin Works
3. **Action:** Using Bitcoin Safely
4. **Connect:** Bitcoin in Your Community

The Diploma follows the **Problem → Solution → Action → Connect** learning arc. Modules 1 and 2 develop the Problem and Solution through explanation, comparison, and reasoning. Module 3 develops safe Action. Module 4 is the educator-curated Connect stage, required for Diploma completion: it links the shared learning to a local need or opportunity, a realistic alternative, safe practice, and a responsible next step.

Teaching may be facilitated in person or online. The October 2026 publication package includes low-tech, offline-capable print/PDF materials, an online PDF version, and a web version. All October public outputs are published through Sanity. The My First Bitcoin app is optional in 2026.10 and may support recap, self-checks, discovery, and connection to future My First Bitcoin learning. Future delivery branches may include online cohort, self-paced, optional app, and cross-modal formats, but every branch must preserve the required Diploma competency standards and educator-led assessment standard.

# Why The Diploma Exists

The Bitcoin Diploma exists to give beginners a structured path into Bitcoin education.

It is not a trading course, investment program, product recommendation system, or technical developer course. It is a foundational education program about money, fiat systems, Bitcoin, self-custody, and responsible participation.

Learners do not need technical or financial background. The Diploma is designed for students, community learners, educators, grassroots organizations, local Bitcoin communities, and partner institutions that want a serious but accessible introduction to Bitcoin.

The Diploma aims to help learners:

- ask better questions about money;
- explain the problems Bitcoin responds to;
- explain Bitcoin's key properties in plain language;
- compare fiat money and Bitcoin through essential ideas;
- explain basic custody, wallet, transaction, and safety concepts;
- identify realistic next steps without being pushed into financial decisions;
- connect Bitcoin education to their local context.

---

# What Is Changing In The 2026.10 Redesign

The redesign responds to problems found in the existing Diploma: unclear duration, broad scope, inconsistent pacing, technical overload for some beginners, limited measurable competencies, and uneven educator support.

The new version improves the Diploma by:

- establishing a minimum 8-hour delivery pathway that includes the required learning and assessment work and expands whenever learner needs require more time;
- organizing the course into four clear modules;
- keeping all four Diploma modules together, while keeping Advanced future learning outside the Diploma and allowing educators to adapt Modules 3–4 within it;
- increasing practical learning, discussion, reflection, and learner explanation;
- using selected Bitcoin for Organizations material only where it clarifies beginner concepts, educator misconception support, activity scenarios, or educator-curated Module 3–4 practice;
- improving educator guidance, pacing, activities, and assessment support;
- preparing English source files for translation and localization;
- reducing fragile links, QR code clutter, and named-service dependencies;
- keeping advanced technical, economic, and philosophical material outside the beginner pathway.

The redesign does not remove the mission of the Diploma. It makes the mission easier to deliver.

# Four-Module Learning Path

## Module 1: Understanding Money

Learners begin with money itself.

This module introduces why societies use money, what makes money useful, how money has changed over time, and what problems can emerge in modern money systems. Topics include barter, the functions and properties of money, scarcity, purchasing power, inflation, fractional reserve banking, the main roles of banks and governments, and CBDCs where appropriate.

Guiding question:

What problem does fiat money create?

Observable learner competency:

Learners can explain the money problems Bitcoin responds to.

## Module 2: How Bitcoin Works

Learners then study why Bitcoin was created and what makes it different.

This module introduces the search for digital and decentralized money, the double-spend problem, digital scarcity, Bitcoin's origin story, decentralization, peer-to-peer transfer, credible finite supply, satoshis, proof of work, users, nodes, miners, developers, and verifiability in an accessible overview.

Guiding question:

Why was Bitcoin created, and how does it respond?

Observable learner competency:

Learners can explain Bitcoin's purpose and compare it with fiat money using guided criteria.

## Module 3: Using Bitcoin Safely

Learners then move from theory into responsibility.

This module introduces wallets, addresses, keys, seed phrases, custodial and non-custodial use, hot and cold wallets, wallet recovery, seed phrase testing, receiving, sending, confirmations, block explorer orientation, verification, public-ledger privacy, irreversible transactions, on-chain and Lightning distinctions, and common beginner safety risks.

The goal is not to make every learner ready for high-stakes custody. The goal is to help beginners identify the responsibility involved and safe next steps.

Guiding question:

How do I take responsibility for using and securing Bitcoin?

Observable learner competency:

Learners can explain beginner self-custody concepts and demonstrate safe first-access, verification, recovery-readiness, and risk-response actions under approved conditions.

## Module 4: Bitcoin in Your Community

The final module is educator-curated and locally grounded.

It is the **Connect** stage of the Diploma's Problem → Solution → Action → Connect learning arc. Learners connect the money problem, Bitcoin response, and safe-use responsibility from Modules 1–3 to a local need or opportunity, a realistic alternative, a safe task, and a responsible next step. The module does not require a learner to adopt Bitcoin or endorse a local tool.

Educators choose the Module 4 because local educators know their students, tools, risks, opportunities, and community conditions better than a centralized curriculum can.

Possible educator-curated Module 4 options include Lightning demonstrations, merchant payments, remittance comparisons, nonprofit or community donation scenarios, local spending and receiving scenarios, local on-ramp and off-ramp comparisons, KYC and no-KYC trade-offs where appropriate, local resource maps, circular economies, community use cases, and practical workshops using tools that are relevant in the local context.

Spending is one possible practice, but Module 4 is broader than spending. Bitcoin as money may also include earning, saving, receiving, mapping resources, finding local communities, or solving local monetary problems.

Guiding question:

How can Bitcoin function as money in this community?

Observable learner competency:

Learners can safely complete one educator-curated Bitcoin-as-money task and explain its purpose, trade-offs, safety considerations, and realistic next step.

# Learning Approach

The 2026.10 Diploma is designed for active learning.

The course should combine:

- short explanations;
- guided discussion;
- reflection;
- practical demonstrations;
- scenario analysis;
- safety checklists;
- local examples;
- learner explanation and review.

The Diploma should teach learners how to think, not what to think. It should help learners compare claims, identify trade-offs, ask better questions, and reason independently.

Practical learning is included in every module, but practical tasks should remain beginner-appropriate and safety-oriented. The course should never imply financial, legal, tax, or investment advice.

# Assessment And Certification

Assessment in the redesigned Diploma is beginner-focused, competency-aligned, and required. It has two non-compensatory parts:

1. Theoretical assessment: a learner explanation, comparison, illustration, or scenario response, evaluated and recorded through educator-led, competency-aligned interactions. This represents 50% of the complete assessment.
2. Practical competency assessment: directly evaluated by an educator through demonstrations, simulations, tests, and role-plays. This represents the remaining 50%.

Modules 1 and 2 are knowledge-dominant. Modules 3 and 4 are skills-dominant and require direct observation of approved practical competencies. Educator-led theory activities are formative unless they are explicitly approved, criteria-based, individually evidenced, and recorded.

The percentages describe two equal-status, non-compensatory components; they do not create a combined numerical grade.

The optional app cannot determine whether a learner can perform a practical skill. Its self-checks and recap do not contribute to assessment in 2026.10.

No learner may receive a Bitcoin Diploma certificate until both assessment components meet the 11 assessed competencies at the required standard. Educators may adapt practical teaching in Modules 3–4 and curate Module 4 tasks, but required competencies, observable criteria, equivalent evidence, and passing standards may not be waived. Certificate governance, identity verification, credential infrastructure, and any on-chain verification remain implementation decisions.

The Educator Guide's operational assessment protocol supplies the common competency-to-evidence crosswalk, approved theory interactions, practical tasks, rubrics, permitted support, safety-critical errors, equivalent routes, reassessment, calibration, and moderation. Every certificated cohort must pass its capacity check before delivery.

# Localization And Translation

The Diploma is intended for global, community-led use.

The English source should be drafted first, then reviewed for beginner clarity, simple language, consistent terminology, tone, style, and translation readiness before translation or publication.

Local educators may adapt examples, tools, activities, and practical variants in Modules 3–4, including the educator-curated Module 4 Connect experience. Required Diploma competency standards should remain consistent across languages and delivery modes.

Named wallets, exchanges, services, maps, and local resources should be treated as local examples unless explicitly approved for global publication.

---

# Delivery Model

Teaching may be in person or online and should remain low-tech and offline-capable wherever possible. No device, app, or connectivity requirement applies to certification in 2026.10.

The course is designed to work as:

- one full-day intensive;
- four 2-hour sessions;
- eight 1-hour sessions;
- another local schedule that preserves the four-module sequence.

The October online PDF and web versions are delivery outputs, not future-branch preparation. Future delivery branches may adapt pacing, media, learner support, and evidence of learning. They should not create separate competency standards.

# Current Boundaries

The 2026.10 Diploma is a beginner pathway.

It should not promise:

- financial advice;
- trading or investment competence;
- independent high-stakes custody mastery;
- advanced technical mastery;
- organizational treasury, policy, compliance, accounting, tax, legal, investment, or institutional custody competence;
- legal or tax guidance;
- global endorsement of named tools or services;
- certification without documented educator-led theoretical and practical evidence.

Advanced topics should be preserved for future pathways rather than forced into the beginner Diploma.

---

# Working Principle

The 2026.10 Bitcoin Diploma should be clear enough for a beginner, useful enough for an educator, flexible enough for local communities, and disciplined enough to scale.

It is a shorter and stronger version of the same mission: helping people explain Bitcoin, money, responsibility, and freedom through open-source education.
