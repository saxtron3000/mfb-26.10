## Program Overview

**Target Audience:** Learners aged 8–12

**Format:** Structured educational guide for educators, parents, and facilitators

**Delivery Mode:** In-person facilitation (classrooms, study groups, home learning)

**Duration:** Flexible, activity-based curriculum (typically 6–10 sessions depending on pacing and group)

**Core Philosophy:** Build foundational thinking skills and conceptual understanding before introducing Bitcoin; focus on HOW to think, not WHAT to think.


---

## Learning Outcomes

By completing Bitcoin for Juniors, learners should be able to:

* Describe value, exchange, and price in everyday terms
* Connect time, energy, and personal choices in simple economic stories
* Compare analog and digital worlds and imagine how technology changes options
* Recognize basic ideas about privacy, security, and who controls information
* Explain what money does for people and communities
* Name core Bitcoin ideas at a high level (where it came from, why it exists, and what "yours" can mean in a digital world)

## Curriculum Structure

### Module 1: Exchange, Value, and Price

Explores how we think about worth, trade, and fairness through games and discussion.

**Lessons:**

* 1.1 Ship Rekt (simulation game)
* 1.2 Going, Going, Gone (auction activity)
* 1.3 What Is Value? (guided discussion)
* 1.4 Worth It? (personal decision-making)

**Key Ideas:** Value is subjective. Price reflects exchange. Fairness depends on perspective.


---

### Module 2: Time, Energy, and Reward

Examines work, rest, choice, and fairness through experiential learning and storytelling.

**Lessons:**

* 2.1 Work, Rest, and Play (energy inventory)
* 2.2 Energy Economy (classroom simulation)
* 2.3 That's Not Fair (guided discussion)
* 2.4 That's Not Fair: Story Time (narrative exploration)
* 2.5 Time Preference (decision-making activity)
* 2.6 Smart Choices (cooperative challenge)

**Key Ideas:** Energy is finite. Time has a cost. Choices matter. Fairness is complex.


---

### Module 3: Analog vs. Digital

Investigates how technology shapes communication, connection, and possibility.

**Lessons:**

* 3.1 Evolution of Technology (timeline activity)
* 3.2 Vote with Your Feet (choice and consequence simulation)
* 3.3 Invent the Future (creative problem-solving)

**Key Ideas:** Technology changes what's possible. Tools have trade-offs. Innovation shapes society.


---

### Module 4: Safety, Security, and Privacy

Builds understanding of protection, control, and trust in digital spaces.

**Lessons:**

* 4.1 Gatekeepers (role-play and discussion)
* 4.2 Wire Tap (simulation game)
* 4.3 Cypherpunks Write Code (introduction to cryptography concepts)
* 4.4 Nostr (decentralized communication introduction)

**Key Ideas:** Control matters. Privacy is protection. Decentralization changes trust.


---

### Module 5: Money Matters

Explains what money is, what it does, and why it matters to individuals and communities.

**Lessons:**

* 5.1 Money Matters (foundational discussion and exploration)

**Key Ideas:** Money is a tool. Money solves problems. Money can fail. Money shapes societies.


---

### Module 6: Bitcoin Basics

Introduces Bitcoin's origins, purpose, and core concept of personal ownership in a digital world.

**Lessons:**

* 6.1 From Problem to Protocol (problem-solving through Bitcoin's lens)
* 6.2 Satoshi, Where Are You? (history and origins)
* 6.3 It's Mine (digital ownership and responsibility)

**Key Ideas:** Bitcoin solves a specific problem. Bitcoin is decentralized. Bitcoin means ownership.

## Pedagogical Approach

### Activity-Based, Not Lecture-Based

Learning happens through games, simulations, discussions, and hands-on activities. Direct instruction is minimal and purposeful.

### No Devices Required

All activities function in low-tech environments. Flexibility for educators in different settings.

### Adaptable to Context

Teachers are expected to modify, simplify, expand, or replace activities based on:

* Student age and developmental level
* Energy and group dynamics
* Time constraints
* Cultural context
* Local examples and languages

### Focus on Thinking, Not Belief

The curriculum teaches **how to think critically**, not what to think about money or Bitcoin. Students draw their own conclusions.

### Accessibility & Inclusion

Each lesson includes differentiation strategies for:

* Emerging readers
* English language learners
* Diverse learning styles
* Physical and sensory considerations
* Mixed-age groups

## Teacher Requirements

### Prior Knowledge

* No Bitcoin or economics background required
* Willingness to facilitate rather than lecture
* Comfort with guiding group discussion and experiential learning

### Training

* Self-paced review of curriculum structure and individual lessons (\~1–2 hours)

### Support Resources

* Lesson-by-lesson instructions and scripts

* FAQ for common questions

## Assessment

### Learning Verification

Assessment is formative and embedded in activities:

* **Observation:** Teacher notes during activities
* **Discussion:** Student contributions in reflection
* **Artifact:** Completed worksheets, responses, group notes

### No High-Stakes Testing

Bitcoin for Juniors is not a tested curriculum. The goal is conceptual understanding and critical thinking, not standardized evaluation.

### Reflection Checkpoints

Each lesson includes a reflection phase where students articulate what they learned and why it matters.

## Content Principles

### Age-Appropriate Language

Complex ideas (value, fairness, privacy, ownership) are explained through:

* Real-world examples (family, school, local community)
* Games and storytelling
* Hands-on simulation
* Guided discussion

### No Speculation or Trading Language

Bitcoin for Juniors explicitly avoids:

* Investment advice
* Price speculation
* Get-rich schemes
* Technical jargon without context

### Culturally Responsive

Activities encourage students to:

* Use local examples
* Connect to their own experience
* Explore fairness through different cultural lenses
* See themselves in the material


---

## Scope & Limitations

### Not a Technical Course

Students do not learn:

* How to code or mine
* Cryptographic algorithms
* Blockchain technical details
* How to transact Bitcoin

### Not a Financial Course

Students do not receive:

* Investment advice
* Trading strategies
* Portfolio guidance
* Speculation tools

### Prepares for Later Learning

Bitcoin for Juniors lays the foundation for:

* Introduction to Bitcoin (self-paced, free course)
* Bitcoin Diploma (structured, in-depth program)
* Advanced technical learning (for interested students)

Activity worksheets and resource sheets (can be printed or projected)

* Classroom-sized space for whole group
* Flexibility to rearrange seating

## Certification & Credentials

### Student Completion

There is no formal certification for students completing Bitcoin for Juniors. A completion certificate may be offered at the educator's discretion as recognition and encouragement.

### Educator Training

Educators who complete the My First Bitcoin Educator Training Program (focused on the Bitcoin Diploma) gain skills applicable to teaching Bitcoin for Juniors.


---

## Disclaimer

This curriculum is **educational in nature**. It is intended to support understanding, discussion, and critical thinking.

It should **not** be interpreted as:

* Financial advice
* Investment guidance
* Legal counsel
* Tax information

Adults should adapt pacing, vocabulary, and activities to the age and developmental context of learners.
