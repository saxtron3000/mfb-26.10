# Bitcoin Diploma Learning Objectives

## Purpose

This document defines the learning objectives for the redesigned Bitcoin Diploma.

It answers:

- What should every learner be able to explain after the core Diploma?
- What should learners be introduced to but not expected to master?
- What practical competence is realistic in an 8-hour beginner program?
- Which outcomes belong in Core, Custom, or Advanced pathways?
- What graduate profile is realistic for the redesigned Diploma?

This document is scoped against the current plan in `07_plan.md`:

- 4-block structure: Problem / Fiat, Solution / Bitcoin, Action / Self-custody, and Custom / Bitcoin as Money;
- approximately 2 hours per block;
- approximately 8 hours total;
- beginner-facing;
- in-person first;
- practical in every block;
- activities separated into a dedicated Activity Bank;
- advanced material removed from the beginner pathway.

This document does not define project workstreams, milestones, assessment methods, certification requirements, or detailed activity instructions.

---

# 1. Status

These objectives are a working framework.

They should be refined after:

- final block structure is confirmed;
- Core / Custom / Advanced boundaries are agreed;
- activity designs are drafted;
- assessment expectations are clarified;
- educator delivery constraints are tested.

Strict planning rule:

If an objective cannot be taught, practiced, and assessed inside the 8-hour scope, it is not a Core objective.

---

# 2. Objective Design Principles

Learning objectives should be:

- precise;
- measurable;
- beginner-appropriate;
- realistic within 8 hours;
- aligned to the 4-block structure: Problem / Fiat, Solution / Bitcoin, Action / Self-custody, and Custom / Bitcoin as Money;
- aligned to activities;
- suitable for educator delivery;
- clear about Core, Custom, and Advanced expectations;
- supportive of critical thinking without overpromising mastery;
- practical where skill acquisition is required.

The redesigned Diploma should distinguish between:

- outcomes every learner must reach;
- outcomes learners may practice with educator support;
- outcomes that belong in Custom / Bitcoin as Money community modules;
- outcomes that belong in advanced follow-on courses.

---

# 3. Realistic Graduate Profile

The graduate profile should describe what an 8-hour beginner Diploma can reasonably produce.

## Knowledge

A graduate has a beginner-level understanding of:

- why money exists;
- what makes money useful;
- basic weaknesses of fiat money;
- why Bitcoin was created;
- Bitcoin's core monetary properties;
- beginner custody concepts;
- basic wallet, key, and seed phrase concepts;
- basic risks and safety considerations.

## Competence

A graduate can:

- explain core ideas in plain language;
- compare fiat money and Bitcoin at a beginner level;
- identify basic custody trade-offs;
- identify safe next steps for learning or practical use;
- participate in guided discussions about Bitcoin.

## Reasoning

A graduate has practiced reasoning from first principles, but should not be expected to have mastered independent economic, technical, or philosophical analysis.

## Confidence

A graduate should leave with enough confidence to:

- ask better questions;
- avoid obvious beginner mistakes;
- continue learning;
- know when a topic is beyond beginner level.

## Participation

A graduate can participate meaningfully in beginner-level Bitcoin education, community discussion, and follow-on learning.

---

# 4. Program-Level Learning Aim

Learners should develop the beginner-level knowledge, confidence, and practical orientation needed to understand why Bitcoin exists, explain its basic purpose, identify core custody and safety considerations, and take informed next steps in continued Bitcoin learning or use.

The Diploma should introduce critical reasoning and local application, but it should not claim to fully develop those abilities in 8 hours.

---

# 5. Outcome Levels

## 5.1 Core Outcomes

Core outcomes are required for the redesigned Bitcoin Diploma.

Every learner should be able to:

1. Explain what money is and why societies use it.
2. Identify the functions and properties of money.
3. Describe basic problems in fiat monetary systems, including inflation and purchasing power loss.
4. Explain why Bitcoin was created at a beginner level.
5. Identify Bitcoin's core properties, including decentralization, finite supply, peer-to-peer transfer, and verifiability.
6. Describe the roles of users, nodes, and miners at a beginner level.
7. Explain the 21 million supply limit and halving at a beginner level.
8. Explain what wallets, keys, seed phrases, and custody mean at a beginner level.
9. Compare custodial and non-custodial approaches at a beginner level.
10. Identify basic safety practices and common risks.
11. Distinguish beginner practical Bitcoin use from advanced technical theory.
12. Participate in guided discussion using clear explanations and basic reasoning.

## 5.2 Custom Outcomes

Custom / Bitcoin as Money outcomes are selected by educators based on local context, student needs, available tools, community requirements, and realistic opportunities for Bitcoin use as money.

Educators are community leaders and are best positioned to choose appropriate teaching for their students. The curriculum should therefore provide a bank of Custom modules that educators can choose from to complete the Diploma.

The Custom / Bitcoin as Money block should focus on practical ways students can use Bitcoin as money in daily life. It should introduce tools, resources, workshops, and demonstrations that local bitcoiners actually use.

Learners may be able to:

- discuss local Bitcoin use cases;
- explore circular economies;
- analyze a Bitcoin Beach-style case study;
- discuss CBDCs and future monetary systems;
- compare KYC and no-KYC pathways at a beginner level;
- practice buying and selling bitcoin scenarios;
- complete app-specific wallet demonstrations;
- identify tools and resources used by local bitcoiners;
- participate in practical workshops or demonstrations;
- identify opportunities where Bitcoin may empower their community;
- connect Bitcoin concepts to local economic conditions;
- reflect on sovereignty, responsibility, and personal agency.

Custom / Bitcoin as Money outcomes should not be required for every learner unless they are explicitly added to a local delivery plan.

Custom / Bitcoin as Money module objectives should be designed using template units that help educators define:

- the local money problem or Bitcoin-as-money opportunity;
- the practical tools being taught;
- the workshop or demonstration format;
- learner safety considerations;
- evidence of learning;
- realistic next steps.

## 5.3 Advanced Outcomes

Advanced outcomes do not belong in the core 8-hour Diploma.

Status: ongoing.

Advanced outcomes have not yet been decided. They should be designed on a module-by-module basis after the core Diploma structure is finalized.

The candidate advanced module titles are maintained in `07_plan.md`. They should guide future advanced-pathway design without becoming Core Diploma objectives.

Advanced topics are valuable, but they create cognitive overload for beginner learners if treated as core. Final objectives should be written separately for each advanced module after its scope, audience, and teaching format are defined.

# 6. Block-To-Objective Map

This map connects the 4-block structure to realistic beginner outcomes.

| Block | Public Learner Promise | Core Objective Emphasis |
|---|---|---|
| Problem / Fiat | Learners understand the money problem Bitcoin responds to. | Define money, identify functions and properties, describe beginner-level fiat weaknesses. |
| Solution / Bitcoin | Learners understand why Bitcoin was created and what makes it different. | Explain Bitcoin's purpose, finite supply, peer-to-peer transfer, decentralization, and verification. |
| Action / Self-custody | Learners understand what responsibility looks like in practice. | Explain wallets, keys, seed phrases, custody trade-offs, receiving, sending, and basic safety. |
| Custom / Bitcoin as Money | Learners see how Bitcoin can function as money in their local context. | Educator-selected local outcomes using template units, workshops, and demonstrations. |

This map should guide assessment, Activity Bank design, Educator Guide pacing, and public communication.

# 7. Learning Domains

## 6.1 Knowledge

Learners should know beginner-appropriate concepts related to:

- money;
- value;
- scarcity;
- time preference;
- opportunity cost;
- functions of money;
- properties of money;
- barter limitations;
- fiat systems;
- inflation;
- purchasing power;
- Bitcoin;
- decentralization;
- finite supply;
- proof of work at a beginner level;
- basic custody concepts;
- wallets;
- keys;
- seed phrases;
- on-chain and Lightning distinctions at a beginner level;
- risks and safety considerations.

## 6.2 Understanding

Learners should understand:

- why money exists;
- why monetary systems shape behavior;
- why some money works better than other money;
- why people looked for alternatives to fiat money;
- why Bitcoin matters to different people and communities;
- why self-custody matters;
- why practical Bitcoin use differs from advanced technical theory;
- why security and responsibility matter when using Bitcoin.

## 6.3 Skills

Learners should be able to:

- explain key ideas in their own words;
- compare fiat money and Bitcoin at a beginner level;
- identify basic trade-offs;
- discuss Bitcoin using simple evidence and reasoning;
- participate in structured dialogue;
- complete guided practical activities where included;
- identify safe next steps.

Learners should not be expected to independently perform advanced analysis, technical troubleshooting, or high-stakes custody decisions after only the core Diploma.

## 6.4 Attitudes and Dispositions

The Diploma should encourage:

- curiosity;
- caution;
- independent learning;
- willingness to ask questions;
- respect for local context;
- seriousness about money, technology, and responsibility.

These are educational aims, not hard certification outcomes unless assessment evidence is later defined.

# 8. Revised Program-Level Objectives

By the end of the redesigned Bitcoin Diploma, learners should be able to:

1. Define foundational monetary concepts using clear beginner language.
2. Explain why money exists and what problems it helps solve.
3. Identify the functions and properties of money.
4. Describe basic weaknesses of fiat money.
5. Explain why Bitcoin was created.
6. Identify Bitcoin's core monetary properties.
7. Describe users, nodes, miners, proof of work, finite supply, and halving at a beginner level.
8. Explain basic wallet, key, seed phrase, and custody concepts.
9. Compare custodial and non-custodial use at a beginner level.
10. Identify basic Bitcoin safety practices and beginner risks.
11. Distinguish core beginner knowledge from advanced technical topics.
12. Participate in structured discussion and explain their reasoning in simple terms.

Where practical activities are included, learners should also be able to complete guided beginner tasks such as:

- identifying wallet types;
- reading a receive screen;
- explaining the purpose of a seed phrase;
- describing how to receive bitcoin safely;
- describing how to verify a transaction or payment at a beginner level.

The core Diploma should not promise that every learner can independently and securely manage bitcoin after 8 hours.

# 9. Revised Competency Areas

## 8.1 Monetary Foundations

Learners can explain what money is, why it exists, and what makes money useful.

## 8.2 Beginner Economic Reasoning

Learners can use simple concepts such as scarcity, time preference, opportunity cost, incentives, and trade-offs to discuss basic monetary situations.

## 8.3 Bitcoin Fundamentals

Learners can explain Bitcoin's basic purpose, core properties, and role as a monetary technology.

## 8.4 Practical Bitcoin Orientation

Learners can identify realistic beginner actions, basic safety practices, and custody trade-offs.

## 8.5 Guided Critical Discussion

Learners can discuss simple claims about Bitcoin, including benefits, risks, and limitations, using guided prompts.

## 8.6 Social Learning

Learners can participate in structured discussion, listen to others, explain their thinking, and ask clarifying questions.

## 8.7 Local Relevance

Learners can reflect on how Bitcoin concepts may relate to local economic or community conditions when this is included in the delivery context.

Local relevance is important, but it should be treated as a guided reflection outcome unless the local module explicitly assesses it.

# 10. Bloom's Taxonomy Scope

The core Diploma should concentrate on the lower and middle levels of Bloom's taxonomy.

## Core

- Remember: define key concepts.
- Understand: explain functions, systems, and relationships.
- Apply: use concepts in guided scenarios.

## Limited Core Use

- Analyze: compare simple trade-offs with educator support.
- Evaluate: assess simple claims using guided prompts.

## Advanced Or Custom

- Create: design new systems, solutions, custody plans, community projects, or technical explanations.

The Diploma can introduce analysis and evaluation, but it should not depend on learners mastering them in 8 hours.
