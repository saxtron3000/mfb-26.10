# Bitcoin Diploma Certification Framework

## Purpose

This document defines the non-negotiable relationship between assessment and certification for the Bitcoin Diploma.

The Bitcoin Diploma consists of the **Problem → Solution → Action → Connect** learning arc. Modules 1 and 2 develop theoretical competencies through observable explanation, comparison, reasoning, and application. Module 3 develops safe practical action. Module 4 is the educator-curated Connect stage: learners connect the shared learning to a local need or opportunity, an appropriate comparison, safe performance, and a realistic next step. It is required for Bitcoin Diploma completion.

Nobody may receive a Bitcoin Diploma certificate without successfully completing both:

1. the theoretical competency component, representing 50% of the complete assessment and evaluated through observable learner explanations, comparisons, scenario responses, and other educator-led evidence; and
2. the practical competency assessment component, representing the remaining 50% and evaluated through direct observation by an educator.

Only documented educator-led interactions with clear competency criteria and records contribute to the theoretical result; participation, self-description, and unrecorded discussion do not. Other questions, discussions, games, scenarios, simulations, and role-plays are formative. Restricted-pilot app use is not assessment evidence. Both components are non-compensatory pass gates.

The percentages describe the balance of two required components. The Bitcoin Diploma does not calculate or report a combined numerical grade unless a separate reporting rule is approved.

The quality and reputation of the certificate depend on the quality and consistency of both forms of assessment.

---

# 1. Certification Rule

A certificate may be issued only when the approved completion record confirms **assessment-complete** status under `13_assessment.md`.

Assessment-complete status requires:

- passing educator-evaluated theoretical interaction evidence
- successful direct observation by an educator of both performances within `C6` and the selected local task `C7`, including their required safety-focused criteria; and
- a **Showing competency: Yes** result for every required assessed competency, with its required decimal-code criteria evidenced as observable look-fors.

The theoretical and practical components each carry 50% of the programme's assessment balance. Both must meet the approved standard; a result in one component cannot compensate for an unmet requirement in the other.

Attendance, participation, restricted-pilot app activity, an educator's general impression, self-reported ability, or completion of printed/PDF materials alone are not sufficient.

This rule applies to every cohort, educator, delivery format, language, and location. Equivalent accessibility accommodations are permitted, but neither assessment component nor the minimum competency standard may be waived.

---

# 2. What A Certificate Represents

A Bitcoin Diploma certificate represents the named educator's judgment that the learner has demonstrated the approved beginner competencies through both theoretical and practical evidence. Certification is decentralized and educator-issued. My First Bitcoin does not centrally assess the learner, issue the certificate, verify it through a central service, or guarantee the learner's competency.

The certificate confirms a shared beginner standard in:

- monetary foundations
- applied monetary and Bitcoin explanation, comparison, and reasoning
- Bitcoin explanation and verification reasoning
- practical wallet and transaction capability
- verification and safety behavior
- guided critical reasoning; and
- locally relevant Bitcoin-as-money application.

It does not represent attendance alone, advanced mastery, professional competence, or endorsement of a particular financial decision.

Certification for the beginner Bitcoin Diploma must not imply competence in high-stakes custody, organizational treasury, institutional custody, legal, tax, accounting, compliance, investment, policy, or business implementation. Those areas belong to [Advanced Modules](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/programs/advanced-modules/) or separate professional review.

# 3. Public-Safe Distinctions

Public communication must distinguish these terms:

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Participation | A learner attended or joined the learning experience. |
| Restricted-pilot app progress | A pilot participant completed some app learning units, self-evaluations, or quizzes. |
| Theoretical assessment complete | The learner passed educator-evaluated theoretical evidence for the 50% theoretical component. |
| Practical-competence complete | The learner passed direct educator observation for the 50% practical component. |
| Assessment-complete | Both assessment components are complete and recorded. |
| Certification | The named educator issues a certificate only after confirming assessment-complete status. It records that educator's judgment, not a central My First Bitcoin evaluation or guarantee. |

Neither restricted-pilot app progress nor either assessment component by itself may unlock certification.

Results are non-compensatory: strong theoretical evidence cannot offset an unmet practical or safety competency, and strong practical performance cannot replace required theoretical evidence.

# 4. Roles And Responsibilities

## Restricted pilot and later public release

My First Bitcoin App microlearning is available only to a defined pilot group. It is not part of the Student Guide, Educator Guide, regular Bitcoin Diploma delivery, or certification route. A later public release must not evaluate or record certification evidence.

## The educator

The educator evaluates the full theoretical competency component through competency-aligned activities, workshops, discussions, and learner explanations, using criteria and concise records; other educator-led theory work is formative. An educator evaluates the full practical component through direct observation. An explanation may also be scored within a practical task when it is an observable criterion of that performance.

The educator must use the [Educator Guide's operational assessment protocol](../publish/mfb-bd-26.10/mfb-bd-eg-26.10.md#operational-assessment-protocol), record evidence-based results in the Assessment Grid, provide remediation where needed, and reassess fairly. The educator may not replace required evidence with attendance, effort, familiarity, a learner's self-description, or personal discretion and may not set or override the theoretical pass result except through an explicitly governed correction process.

The educator is the certificate issuer. The educator assesses the learner, accredits the result, distributes the certificate, and remains responsible for any issuer-specific record, correction, replacement, or verification process they offer. The certificate must identify the educator or issuing education organization clearly and must not imply that My First Bitcoin directly evaluated the learner. After confirming the result, the educator may use the [Certificate Tool](https://tools.myfirstbitcoin.org/certificate) to prepare participation or completion certificates in a batch. The tool does not assess learners, decide eligibility, or change who issues the certificate.

## The certification process

The educator confirms that both educator-led component statuses are complete before issuing a certificate. The approved completion record must distinguish theoretical and practical evidence. No central My First Bitcoin approval or verification step is part of the current model.

# 5. Standardization Across Delivery And Educator

The minimum standard is created by the combination of:

- shared assessed competencies and observable criteria
- the common crosswalk, theory interactions, practical tasks, equivalent variants, rubrics, support rules, safety-critical errors, calibration, moderation, and reassessment process in the Educator Guide's operational assessment protocol; and
- evidence records in the Assessment Grid.

Educators may adapt examples, language, resources, tools, and approved practical routes in Modules 3–4; they curate Module 4 using their local subject-matter expertise. They may not remove required competencies or criteria, substitute participation for evidence, lower the passing standard, or issue a certificate outside the approved assessment process.

Standardization does not mean every learner must use the same wallet or local scenario. It means that different approved tasks must provide equivalent evidence of the same competency and its required criteria.

---

# 6. Certificate Issuance Workflow

1. The learner completes the relevant teaching and supported practice.
2. The educator gathers and records approved theoretical evidence; other explanations, discussions, scenarios, simulations, or role-plays remain formative.
3. An educator directly observes and records the required Module 3 and selected Module 4 practical competencies.
4. Any unmet competency is recorded as **Showing competency: Not yet**, followed by feedback, further practice, and reassessment while the learner builds competency.
5. The educator-led theoretical and practical results are recorded in the approved completion record.
6. The educator confirms assessment-complete status only when both components meet the standard.
7. After confirming the result and certificate type, the educator may use the [Certificate Tool](https://tools.myfirstbitcoin.org/certificate) to prepare certificates in a batch. The educator checks learner names, certificate types, and issuing details before distribution.
8. The named educator or educator's organization issues and distributes the certificate after that confirmation.

---

# 7. Protecting Certificate Reputation

The certificate's credibility rests on what it reliably communicates to learners, educators, partners, and communities.

To protect that credibility:

- every certificated learner must be assessed against the required competencies
- practical capability must be directly observed
- observable theoretical competency and practical capability must each meet the 50% component standard
- educators must use common criteria rather than personal impressions
- unsuccessful attempts must lead to feedback and reassessment, not automatic passing
- results must be recorded accurately
- local adaptation must preserve equivalent evidence; and
- public claims must match the beginner competencies actually assessed.

A strong assessment system makes the certificate meaningful. Weak, inconsistent, or bypassed assessment damages the reputation of every certificate, including those earned through robust evaluation.

---

# 8. Current Governance And Future Options

The current model is settled: certification is decentralized and educator-issued. Any educator using the Bitcoin Diploma may issue a participation or completion certificate under the published assessment requirements. My First Bitcoin does not centrally approve the result, issue the certificate, operate a public verification service, or guarantee learner competency.

Before certificated delivery, the educator establishes proportionate local arrangements for:

- assessor preparation and any useful calibration or moderation
- assessment integrity and learner identification appropriate to the context
- accessibility and equivalent access, including language parity
- minimum certificate fields and clear identification of the educator or issuing organization
- privacy, consent, permitted records, retention, deletion, and secure recovery where records are kept
- result correction, appeals, accommodations, and exceptional cases; and
- certificate correction, replacement, or withdrawal where the educator offers those processes.

The [Certificate Tool](https://tools.myfirstbitcoin.org/certificate) is available to help educators prepare certificates in batches after they have confirmed eligibility. It is not a central assessment, issuance, record, or verification service. Future tools may support issuer-specific or shared verification, educator permissions, record reconciliation, audit trails, revocation, replacement, or privacy-preserving timestamps. They are optional future infrastructure, not part of the 2026.10 certificate promise, and must not convert an educator-issued certificate into a claim of central My First Bitcoin evaluation. Any future verification method must minimize personal data and must never replace either assessment component.

# 9. Framework-First Release Boundary

The 2026.10 curriculum is released as a complete teaching package and assessment framework first. Publication does not itself declare every educator, local task variant, cohort schedule, accessibility route, or record process ready for certificated delivery.

An educator may begin certificated delivery only when the selected assessment tasks and local variants, capacity, accessibility routes, Assessment Grid, and proportionate local governance are ready. Until then, the materials may be used as a teaching package without making assessment-complete or certificate claims.
