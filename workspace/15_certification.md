# Bitcoin Diploma Certification Framework

## Purpose

This document defines the non-negotiable relationship between assessment and certification for the Bitcoin Diploma.

Nobody may receive a Bitcoin Diploma certificate without successfully completing both:

1. the theoretical assessment component, representing 50% of the complete assessment and divided between 80% app evaluation and 20% educator-evaluated objective-aligned interactions; and
2. the practical competency assessment component, representing the remaining 50% and divided between 80% direct observation by an educator and 20% app follow-up linked to the practical task.

Only approved educator-led theory interactions with criteria and records contribute to the theoretical result; other educator-led questions, explanations, discussions, games, scenarios, simulations, and role-plays are formative. App practical follow-up cannot replace direct observation. Both components and their required contributions are non-compensatory pass gates.

The quality and reputation of the certificate depend on the quality and consistency of both forms of assessment.

---

# 1. Certification Rule

A certificate may be issued only when the approved completion record confirms **assessment-complete** status under `13_assessment.md`.

Assessment-complete status requires:

- a passing app theoretical result and passing educator-evaluated theoretical interaction evidence;
- successful direct observation by an educator of required Block 3 practical and safety competencies and selected Block 4 objectives; and
- a passing app follow-up result linked to the practical tasks.

The theoretical and practical components each carry 50% of the total assessment. Both must meet the approved standard; a result in one component cannot compensate for an unmet requirement in the other.

Attendance, participation, app activity without a passing result, the app theoretical pass alone, an educator's general impression, self-reported ability, or completion of printed/PDF materials alone are not sufficient.

This rule applies to every cohort, educator, delivery format, language, and location. Equivalent accessibility accommodations are permitted, but neither assessment component nor the minimum competency standard may be waived.

---

# 2. What A Certificate Represents

A Bitcoin Diploma certificate represents that the learner has demonstrated the approved beginner learning objectives through both knowledge-based and performance-based evidence.

The certificate confirms a shared beginner standard in:

- monetary foundations;
- applied economic and Bitcoin understanding;
- Bitcoin fundamentals;
- practical wallet and transaction capability;
- verification and safety behavior;
- guided critical reasoning; and
- locally relevant Bitcoin-as-money application.

It does not represent attendance alone, advanced mastery, professional competence, or endorsement of a particular financial decision.

Certification for the beginner Diploma must not imply competence in high-stakes custody, organizational treasury, institutional custody, legal, tax, accounting, compliance, investment, policy, or business implementation. Those areas belong to Advanced pathways or separate professional review.

---

# 3. Public-Safe Distinctions

Public communication must distinguish these terms:

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Participation | A learner attended or joined the learning experience. |
| App progress | A learner completed some app learning units, self-evaluations, or quizzes. |
| Theoretical assessment complete | The learner passed the app-evaluated 80% and educator-evaluated 20% contributions to the 50% theoretical component. |
| Practical-competence complete | The learner passed the directly observed 80% and app-follow-up 20% contributions to the 50% practical component. |
| Assessment-complete | Both assessment components are complete and recorded. |
| Certification | A certificate issued only after assessment-complete status is verified. |

Neither app progress nor either assessment component by itself may unlock certification.

Results are non-compensatory: a high theoretical score cannot offset an unmet practical or safety competency, and strong practical performance cannot replace required theoretical evidence.

---

# 4. Roles And Responsibilities

## The App

The My First Bitcoin app delivers and evaluates 80% of scored theoretical evidence through objective-aligned microlearning and evaluation, and evaluates the 20% practical-task follow-up. It records both app contributions.

The app may store an educator theory result and an educator's practical result or attestation. It does not directly evaluate real-world practical capability.

## The educator

The educator evaluates 20% of the theoretical component through approved objective-aligned activities, workshops, discussions, and learner explanations; other educator-led theory work is formative. An educator evaluates 80% of the practical component through direct observation. An explanation may also be scored within a practical task when it is an observable criterion of that performance.

The educator must use approved objective-aligned tasks and rubrics, record evidence-based results, provide remediation where needed, and reassess fairly. The educator may not replace required evidence with attendance, effort, familiarity, or personal discretion and may not set or override the theoretical pass result except through an explicitly governed correction process.

## The Certification Process

The certification process verifies that both component statuses and all four required contributions are complete before issuing a certificate. Whether the combined record is held in the app or another approved system is an implementation decision; the record must distinguish app theory, educator theory, educator practical, and app practical-follow-up results.

---

# 5. Standardization Across Delivery And Educator

The minimum standard is created by the combination of:

- shared learning objectives;
- a common assessment blueprint;
- required app theoretical quizzes, interactive learning, and practical-task follow-up items;
- approved educator-evaluated theoretical interactions;
- approved Activity Bank practical assessment tasks and app follow-up items;
- common practical tasks or equivalent approved variants;
- observable rubrics;
- educator preparation, training, and calibration for practical assessment;
- evidence records;
- remediation and reassessment rules; and
- moderation or audit where required.

Educators may adapt examples, language, resources, tools, and approved Custom practice. They may not remove required objectives, substitute participation for evidence, lower the passing standard, or issue a certificate outside the approved assessment process.

Standardization does not mean every learner must use the same wallet or local scenario. It means that different approved tasks must provide equivalent evidence of the same objective and competency.

---

# 6. Certificate Issuance Workflow

1. The learner completes the relevant teaching and supported practice.
2. The learner completes the required app pathway and earns the app portion of the theoretical result under the approved scoring rules.
3. The educator gathers and records the approved theory-interaction evidence; other explanations, discussions, scenarios, simulations, or role-plays remain formative.
4. An educator directly observes the required Block 3 and selected Block 4 practical competencies, then the learner completes the linked app follow-up.
5. Any unmet competency is marked **Not yet**, followed by feedback, further practice, and reassessment.
6. The app theoretical result, educator theoretical result, educator practical result, and app practical-follow-up result are recorded separately in the approved completion record.
7. The system confirms assessment-complete status only when both components meet the standard.
8. The certificate is issued only after that confirmation.

If a learner passes an app contribution independently, they remain ineligible for certification until all remaining educator and educator contributions are passed.

---

# 7. Protecting Certificate Reputation

The certificate's credibility rests on what it reliably communicates to learners, educators, partners, and communities.

To protect that credibility:

- every certificated learner must be assessed against the learning objectives;
- app quizzes and interactive learning must assess the required theoretical objectives and must not reward rapid guessing;
- practical capability must be directly observed;
- the 40% app-theory, 10% educator-theory, 40% educator-observed-practical, and 10% app-practical-follow-up allocation must be applied consistently as two non-compensatory gates with all four contributions required;
- educators must use common criteria rather than personal impressions;
- unsuccessful attempts must lead to feedback and reassessment, not automatic passing;
- results must be recorded accurately;
- local adaptation must preserve equivalent evidence; and
- public claims must match the beginner competencies actually assessed.

A strong assessment system makes the certificate meaningful. Weak, inconsistent, or bypassed assessment damages the reputation of every certificate, including those earned through robust evaluation.

---

# 8. Governance And Verification Still To Define

The two-part assessment prerequisite is a current design requirement. The following implementation and governance details still require definition:

- certificate issuer and approval authority;
- educator authorization and renewal;
- educator calibration, moderation, and audit process;
- app availability, supported devices and operating systems, learner versus shared or loan-device accounts, and assisted use;
- weak-connectivity and offline app behavior, synchronization, interrupted attempts, outages, and account recovery;
- assessment integrity and identity and learner-account verification appropriate to the context;
- app pass, retry, feedback, item-version, and result-correction rules;
- accessibility and equivalent access, including language parity;
- certificate format, data fields, privacy, consent, retention, and recovery rules;
- appeals, corrections, accessibility accommodations, and exceptional-case workflow;
- educator permissions, practical-result submission, record reconciliation, and evidence-record audit trail;
- certificate revocation and replacement process;
- public certificate-verification process; and
- whether certificate records or hashes should ever be timestamped on-chain.

An on-chain timestamp, if adopted, must not expose learner personal information and must not replace either required assessment component.
