# Bitcoin Diploma Optional Self-Assessment: October Question Data

## Use

This file is the developer handoff for the 12-question optional My First Bitcoin app self-test. It is not formal assessment and must not be used for educator tracking, certification, or a pass/fail decision.

Each item has one correct answer and four options. Show the correct response after the learner selects the correct option. Show the incorrect response after any other selection. The review reference points to the learner’s next reading in `08_student.md`.

| ID | Module and topic | Prompt | A | B | C | D | Correct option | Correct response | Incorrect response and review reference |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `BD-ML-01` | Module 1: Why money exists | What problem does money help solve? | It makes every good free. | It helps people exchange when direct barter is difficult. | It guarantees that prices never change. | It removes the need for trust or rules. | B | Correct. Money makes exchange easier when two people do not want each other’s goods at the same time. Keep learning. | Not quite. Money helps coordinate exchange; it does not make goods free or remove every risk. Review Student Guide, Module 1, “Why money exists.” |
| `BD-ML-02` | Module 1: Functions of money | Which is an example of money acting as a unit of account? | Comparing the prices of two items in the same currency. | Carrying cash from one place to another. | Checking whether a note is genuine. | Saving a paper receipt. | A | Correct. A unit of account gives people a shared way to express and compare prices. Continue. | Not quite. A unit of account is the shared unit used to state and compare prices. Review Student Guide, Module 1, “Functions and properties of money.” |
| `BD-ML-03` | Module 1: Barter | Why can barter become difficult? | Goods can never be exchanged directly. | People must find someone who wants what they offer and offers what they want. | Barter always requires a bank. | Barter makes every good scarce. | B | Correct. Direct barter depends on a double coincidence of wants, which can be hard to find. Keep going. | Not quite. Barter can work, but it becomes difficult when people’s wants do not match. Review Student Guide, Module 1, “Barter and the double coincidence of wants.” |
| `BD-ML-04` | Module 1: Inflation and purchasing power | If the price of a basket of goods rises while your income stays the same, what usually happens to your purchasing power? | It increases. | It stays exactly the same. | It decreases. | It becomes impossible to measure. | C | Correct. The same income buys less when prices rise. Continue learning. | Not quite. Purchasing power is what an amount of money can buy. Review Student Guide, Module 1, “Inflation and purchasing power.” |
| `BD-ML-05` | Module 2: Double spending | Why is preventing double spending important for digital money? | It stops a person from copying the same digital unit and spending it twice. | It makes every payment private. | It guarantees that bitcoin will rise in price. | It removes all transaction fees. | A | Correct. A usable digital money system needs a way to reject a second attempt to spend the same unit. Keep learning. | Not quite. Digital information can be copied, so a money system must prevent the same unit from being spent twice. Review Student Guide, Module 2, “Early digital-money attempts and the double-spend problem.” |
| `BD-ML-06` | Module 2: Decentralization | Which statement best describes Bitcoin decentralization? | One company approves every transaction. | Many independent participants check and enforce the network’s rules. | Miners can change any rule whenever they choose. | Every participant has exactly the same influence. | B | Correct. Bitcoin distributes verification and rule enforcement across independent participants. Continue. | Not quite. Decentralization does not mean one company controls the network or that everyone has equal influence. Review Student Guide, Module 2, “Decentralization.” |
| `BD-ML-07` | Module 2: Users, nodes, and miners | What does a Bitcoin node do? | It independently checks transactions and blocks against Bitcoin’s rules. | It creates a new bitcoin whenever a user asks. | It stores bitcoin inside a phone. | It guarantees that a payment will never be wrong. | A | Correct. Nodes independently verify the rules and reject invalid data. Keep learning. | Not quite. Nodes check Bitcoin’s rules; they do not create bitcoin on request or store bitcoin in a phone. Review Student Guide, Module 2, “Users, nodes, and miners.” |
| `BD-ML-08` | Module 2: Supply and units | How many satoshis equal one bitcoin? | 100 | 1,000 | 1,000,000 | 100,000,000 | D | Correct. One bitcoin is divisible into 100,000,000 satoshis. Continue. | Not quite. A satoshi is the smallest named unit of bitcoin. Review Student Guide, Module 2, “Satoshis and unit bias.” |
| `BD-ML-09` | Module 3: Wallets | Which statement about a Bitcoin wallet is most accurate? | It stores bitcoin inside your phone. | It manages keys and helps you receive, sign, and track Bitcoin transactions. | It removes the need to protect secret information. | It guarantees that every transaction can be reversed. | B | Correct. Bitcoin is recorded on the shared ledger; a wallet manages the information used to access and use it. Keep learning. | Not quite. A wallet manages keys and transaction information. It does not literally contain bitcoin or remove responsibility. Review Student Guide, Module 3, “Wallets.” |
| `BD-ML-10` | Module 3: Addresses and secrets | Which information is generally safe to share when you want to receive an on-chain Bitcoin payment? | Your seed phrase. | Your private key. | Your Bitcoin receiving address. | Your wallet password. | C | Correct. A receiving address is public payment information. Your seed phrase and private key must remain secret. Continue. | Not quite. A receiving address can be shared for payment. Seed phrases, private keys, and passwords must remain secret. Review Student Guide, Module 3, “Addresses” and “Keys.” |
| `BD-ML-11` | Module 3: Seed phrases | A person claiming to be wallet support asks for your seed phrase to fix a problem. What is the safest response? | Share it if the person sounds professional. | Share only the first few words. | Take a photo and send it later. | Do not share it; stop and verify support independently. | D | Correct. No legitimate helper needs your seed phrase. Stop, protect your secrets, and seek trusted support independently. Keep learning. | Not quite. A seed phrase can give full access to a wallet. Never share, photograph, or enter it through an unexpected request. Review Student Guide, Module 3, “Seed phrases” and “Safety checklist: seed phrases, address checks, scams, and starting small.” |
| `BD-ML-12` | Module 3: Confirmations and verification | What does a first confirmation mean for an on-chain Bitcoin transaction? | The transaction has been included in a valid block. | The recipient has promised a refund. | The address can no longer be checked. | The transaction is private. | A | Correct. A first confirmation means a miner included the transaction in a valid block. Continue learning. | Not quite. Confirmations increase confidence that a transaction is included in Bitcoin’s history; they do not reverse mistakes or make a transaction private. Review Student Guide, Module 3, “Verification, block explorers, and irreversible transactions.” |

## Optional final-score messages

| Score out of 12 | Learner message |
|---|---|
| 10–12 | Great work. You have a strong foundation. Keep building your Bitcoin knowledge. |
| 6–9 | Good progress. Review the topics highlighted in your answers, then try again. |
| 0–5 | Every question is a chance to learn. Revisit the suggested Diploma sections and try the quiz again. |

## Developer notes

- Display only one answer as correct for each item.
- Do not store or share a learner’s score with an educator.
- Do not label a score pass or fail.
- Do not request seed phrases, private keys, wallet passwords, holdings, or other personal financial information.
