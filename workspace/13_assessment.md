# Bitcoin Diploma Competency Assessment Framework

## Purpose

This document defines how the Bitcoin Diploma establishes and records learner competency.

It is the assessment source of truth for the 2026.10 edition. The Assessment Grid is the minimum learner record, and the Educator Guide provides examples and support that must remain consistent with this framework.

The Diploma does not assess private knowledge or a learner's self-description. It assesses what a learner can observably explain, compare, apply, decide, and do. Knowledge matters because it enables those actions; it is not, by itself, a certification result.

The framework uses one simple competency code system:

- `C1` through `C11` are the 11 required assessed competencies. Each receives one final result: **Meets standard** or **Not yet**.
- Decimal codes such as `C1.1` and `C1.2` are required observable criteria within the related competency. They are educator look-fors, not separate certification rows.
- `C12` and `C13` are formative competencies. They support learning and feedback but are not certificate gates.

Every required criterion must have sufficient evidence before its parent competency can be recorded as **Meets standard**. A strength in one competency or criterion cannot compensate for an unmet, inaccurate, or unsafe requirement elsewhere.

The 2026.10 Diploma uses two required, non-compensatory completion gates:

1. **Theory (`C1`–`C8`)** — learners show what they can explain, compare, reason through, or apply in activities and conversations.
2. **Practice (`C9`–`C11`)** — learners show what they can safely perform, verify, and explain through an educator-observed practical activity.

These are not numerical grades. Both gates must be complete, and every required competency must meet its standard before a completion certificate is issued.

Assessment can happen through well-designed activities, discussions, demonstrations, and practice. A separate exam is not required for every competency. Activity completion may provide evidence when the activity is designed to reveal the relevant understanding or action; attendance, participation, confidence, self-report, or completion of materials alone cannot establish competency. The [My First Bitcoin App](https://myfirstbitcoin.app/) is optional and cannot provide formal assessment evidence.

The [Educator Guide's assessment guidance](../publish/mfb-bd-26.10/mfb-bd-eg-26.10.md#operational-assessment-protocol) provides examples, prompts, safety guidance, accessibility ideas, flexible routes, reassessment support, and optional quality support. Use it as a structure, not a script. The Assessment Grid is the minimum cohort record: one final `MS` or `NY` result per learner and competency.

# 1. Competency-led assessment

## Observable theory

Theoretical competency is not a recall test. An educator must be able to see or hear the learner use knowledge through an observable response. Suitable evidence includes:

- an explanation in the learner's own words;
- a comparison using stated criteria;
- a concept illustration or relationship map;
- a reasoned response to a guided scenario;
- an identified risk and safe decision; or
- a justified next step or limitation.

A multiple-choice answer may show recognition or recall. It cannot, by itself, establish that a learner can explain, compare, reason, apply, or make a safe decision.

## Observable practice

Practical competency requires direct observation by an educator. The educator must see the learner complete the relevant activity safely, make important checks, protect private information, explain key decisions, and recognize when to stop or seek help.

Use prepared wallets, simulations, testnet, physical tokens, or appropriately low-stakes activity where needed. Personal funds are not required. If learners choose to use a small amount of their own funds, make the risk of loss clear and never make this the only route. No assessment may require a learner to expose a seed phrase, private key, wallet credential, balance, or unnecessary personal information.

## Common standard

The same required competencies, observable criteria, and minimum standard apply across cohorts, educators, languages, and delivery modes. Accessibility or local adaptation may change how evidence is collected, but it may not remove or weaken the competency being assessed.

Low connectivity, lack of a device, or lack of app access must not prevent learning, assessment, completion, or certification.

# 2. Competency codes and records

| Code range | Meaning | Record rule |
|---|---|---|
| `C1`–`C8` | Theoretical competencies | Record one educator-evaluated result for each competency. |
| `C9`–`C11` | Practical competencies | Record one directly observed result for each competency. |
| Decimal `C` codes | Required observable criteria | Check as part of the parent competency; do not create separate certificate rows. |
| `C12`, `C13` | Formative competencies | Use for feedback and reflection only. |

An activity may collect evidence for several criteria within one competency. It may not combine different assessed competencies into one result unless each competency remains individually visible and recorded.

# 3. Assessment components

## Theory gate: `C1`–`C8`

Educators collect evidence through activities available on the [Resources Hub](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/resources/) or suitable local activities, such as learner explanations, guided comparisons, scenario analysis, concept illustrations, claim/evidence/trade-off responses, simulations, and role-plays. These interactions can be part of normal teaching; they do not need to be a separate written exam.

The evidence must match the competency verb. For example:

- **Explain** requires the learner to express the relevant idea in their own words.
- **Compare** requires relevant similarities, differences, or trade-offs using stated criteria.
- **Apply** requires the learner to use knowledge in a new, guided, local, or realistic scenario.
- **Identify and decide** requires the learner to recognize a relevant risk and state an appropriate action.

Participation, agreement with a slogan, or an educator's impression is not enough. The educator uses the competency look-fors and a confident professional judgement to establish the learner's demonstrated response. Brief notes are helpful but optional.

## Practice gate: `C9`–`C11`

Practical competency is evaluated through educator observation of demonstrations, simulations, role-plays, scenario-based evaluations, prepared-wallet activity, physical tokens, testnet, or carefully structured low-stakes activity. A spoken explanation may be part of a practical activity, but it does not replace the required performance.

The optional [My First Bitcoin App](https://myfirstbitcoin.app/) may prepare learners, provide checklists, or offer self-check questions. It is not practical evidence. Watching a demonstration or reporting that an action was completed is not sufficient; the educator needs an opportunity to observe the learner's relevant action or decision.

| Module | Required competencies | Assessment emphasis | Required evidence pattern |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Understanding Money | `C1`, `C2` | Theoretical competency | Observable explanation, comparison, and guided scenario response. |

| 3. Using Bitcoin Safely | `C6`, `C7`, `C8`, `C9`, `C10` | Theory and practical competency | Observable explanation and safety reasoning for `C6`–`C8`; direct observation for `C9`–`C10`. |
| 4. Connect: Bitcoin in Your Community | `C11` | Local practical competency | Direct observation of an educator-curated activity and its explanation. |

The assessment follows the Diploma's **Problem → Solution → Action → Connect** arc. Modules 1 and 2 develop the Problem and Solution through observable learner responses. Educators may adapt the practical route in Module 3 while preserving its required competency and safety purpose. Module 4 is the educator-curated Connect stage within the Diploma: it requires the learner to connect that foundation to a local need or opportunity, a realistic alternative, safe performance, and a responsible next step. Theory and practice are two completion gates across the complete Diploma, not numerical scores within each module.

# 6. Assessment workflow

For each assessed competency:

1. **Teach:** Introduce the relevant knowledge, action, and safety boundary.
2. **Practise:** Give supported practice and feedback before making a final judgement.
3. **Notice evidence:** Use an activity, conversation, demonstration, or practical route that lets the learner demonstrate the competency.
4. **Ask when unsure:** Use a friendly probing question or invite the learner to show the step again.
5. **Record:** Mark the competency **MS** (**Meets standard**) or **NY** (**Not yet**) on the Assessment Grid. Brief notes are helpful but optional.
6. **Support and reassess:** Give focused practice and a fair opportunity to demonstrate the same competency again during class, a break, an additional call, or a follow-up session.

Assessment should not surprise learners. Before the educator makes a final judgement, the learner should understand the activity, expected action or response, relevant safety boundaries, and what they are trying to show.

# 7. Completion standard

A learner is **assessment-complete** only when both component statuses are complete:

## A. Theoretical competency complete

- the educator has recorded **Meets standard** for `C1` through `C8`; and
- every required criterion within `C1` through `C8` has sufficient observable evidence.

## B. Practical competency complete

- the educator has directly observed and recorded **Meets standard** for `C9`, `C10`, and `C11`;
- every required criterion within those competencies has been observed safely; and
- `C9.3` includes the supported first-access pathway and receipt of bitcoin, or a safe simulated receipt.

Passing one component cannot compensate for an unmet requirement in the other. Passing one criterion cannot compensate for incomplete, inaccurate, unsafe, or overly prompted evidence for another required criterion.

# 8. Certification meaning

Certification is educator-issued and decentralized. Any educator using the Diploma may issue a certificate based on their assessment judgement. My First Bitcoin does not centrally issue, centrally verify, or guarantee the learner's competency.

Use two certificate types:

- **Certificate of participation:** confirms that the learner attended or took part in the Diploma. It does not confirm that the learner passed the assessment.
- **Certificate of completion:** confirms that the educator assessed the learner and judged that the learner passed all required competencies.

The educator must use the Assessment Grid as the minimum assessment process. Keeping brief assessment traces is advisable when useful for feedback, reassessment, or educator memory, but it is not a separate certification requirement. Learners may be assessed through activities, discussions, demonstrations, simulations, online observation, low-tech routes, testnet, physical tokens, or carefully structured low-stakes activity when the route allows the educator to judge the competency safely.

An educator who has completed the Educator Training Program may show the ETP badge beside their name on the certificate. The ETP is recommended support for educators who want more confidence; it is not required to issue a certificate.

# 9. Formative competencies

`C12` and `C13` support learning across the Diploma. They may guide educator feedback but do not create certification rows or alter the `C1`–`C11` completion rule.

| Formative competency | Suggested evidence |
|---|---|
| `C12` Participate in guided discussion using clear explanations, relevant evidence, respectful listening, and basic reasoning. | Educator observation and feedback. |
| `C13` Articulate a reason to continue learning about Bitcoin, or a reason for remaining uncertain, using course concepts and an observed, local, or realistic money experience without making an investment claim. | Reflection or guided conversation. |

# 10. Educator standard

Educators assess both theoretical and practical competencies using suitable activities, observable criteria, professional judgement, feedback, and fair reassessment.

The [Educator Guide's assessment guidance](../publish/mfb-bd-26.10/mfb-bd-eg-26.10.md#operational-assessment-protocol) and activities available on the [Resources Hub](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/resources/) provide:

- competency-to-activity mappings;
- required observable criteria for each competency;
- example theoretical prompts and practical routes;
- practical test, simulation, role-play, and safety guidance;
- clear definitions of **Meets standard** and **Not yet**;
- prompting and permitted-support rules;
- accessibility variants that preserve the competency;
- optional educator calibration examples; and
- optional second-review or moderation support where useful.

An educator must not award a result because a learner attended, participated confidently, completed an app self-check, or appears familiar with the topic. The educator should be able to explain what the learner showed for the competency and its relevant criteria, even when no detailed written record is kept.

---

# 11. Delivery and accessibility

All certificated delivery modes require both assessment components.

- In-person, online, and cohort delivery integrate educator-evaluated activities, formative support, and direct practical observation into competency-led module activities.
- Self-paced learning may support preparation, but certification requires educator-led theoretical and live or remote practical assessment.
- Low-connectivity delivery may use offline-capable print/PDF materials and educator-led assessment without a later app requirement.
- Remote practical assessment is permitted only when the educator can directly observe the required performance and protect learner privacy and safety.
- Accessibility accommodations may change how a learner communicates or performs, but must preserve the assessed competency, its required criteria, and the required level of performance.

---

# 12. Governance and future implementation

- Do not calculate or report a combined numerical grade unless a separate policy is approved.
- Consider delivery time, cohort size, available educators, accommodations, feedback, troubleshooting, and reassessment when planning a cohort. No fixed educator-to-learner ratio or formal pilot is required.
- Define proportionate local arrangements for privacy, consent, minors' access, records, result correction, appeals, suspected misconduct, outages, and emergencies where they are relevant to the delivery context.
- Keep future certificate-tool, central-record, and public-verification ideas separate from the current educator-issued certification model unless and until they are approved.
