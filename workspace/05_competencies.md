# Bitcoin Diploma Competency Framework

## Purpose

This document defines the demonstrated competencies required for the redesigned Bitcoin Diploma.

It answers:

- what every learner must visibly explain, compare, justify, respond to, or demonstrate after the four-module Diploma;
- which detailed observable criteria sit within each assessed competency;
- what every learner must demonstrate through an educator-curated Module 4 task;
- what belongs outside the eight-hour beginner pathway;
- how competencies connect to the four-module structure; and
- how the same competency requirement can be used across language and delivery-mode adaptations.

This document does not define activities, task instructions, rubrics, certification administration, or project milestones.

## Status and scope guardrails

This is a working planning framework for a beginner Diploma.

**Eight-hour rule:** If a competency cannot be taught, practised, and evidenced safely inside the eight-hour scope, it is not a required Diploma competency.

**Observable-evidence rule:** No competency is awarded for private knowledge or self-description alone. A theoretical competency requires an educator to observe an individual learner explanation, comparison, justification, scenario response, illustration, or another approved learner action. A practical competency requires direct educator observation of the stated performance.

**Whole-competency rule:** Each assessed competency is met only when the learner meets every required observable criterion within it through approved evidence. A strong result in one competency cannot compensate for an unmet safety-critical or practical competency.

**Translation rule:** Wording may be localized, but the required learner action, support level, safety boundary, and evidence standard must remain equivalent.

Detailed facts and terms belong in the relevant required criteria or supporting teaching materials. They should not become additional certification gates merely because they were discussed in class.

## Graduate capability profile

A realistic graduate of the eight-hour beginner Diploma can:

| Area | Realistic capability |
|---|---|
| Money foundations | Explain why money exists, identify useful monetary characteristics, and use them in a simple comparison. |
| Bitcoin foundations | Explain Bitcoin's purpose, key properties, and basic differences from selected non-Bitcoin digital assets. |
| Guided reasoning | Give clear, evidence-based explanations and comparisons in response to a prompt. |
| Safe practical capability | Complete required beginner wallet, first-access, receiving or sending, verification, recovery-readiness, and safety actions under approved, supported conditions. |
| Boundaries and confidence | Recognize obvious beginner risks, know when to pause or seek help, and continue learning without making an investment claim. |
| Language transfer | Meet the same demonstrated requirements whether the course is taught in the source language or through a localized translation. |

The Diploma does not claim that every graduate can independently manage high-stakes custody, perform advanced technical troubleshooting, or master economic, technical, or philosophical analysis.

## Competency architecture

The four-module Diploma has 11 assessed competencies. `C1` through `C11` are the recorded competency codes. Decimal codes such as `C1.1` and `C1.2` are required observable criteria within the relevant competency, not separate certification rows.

| Assessed competency | Competency and required action | Assessment component |
|---|---|---|
| `C1` | Explain why people use money and compare forms of money using beginner monetary characteristics. | Theoretical |
| `C2` | Explain an introductory modern-money trade-off and connect it to a guided, observed, local, or realistic example. | Theoretical |
| `C3` | Explain why Bitcoin was created, including the digital-copy and double-spend problem. | Theoretical |
| `C4` | Explain Bitcoin's key properties and make a guided sound-money judgment. | Theoretical |
| `C5` | Compare Bitcoin with non-Bitcoin digital assets and distinguish responsible learning and use from speculation. | Theoretical |
| `C6` | Explain wallet, custody, and hardware-wallet basics through essential terms and examples. | Theoretical |
| `C7` | Recognize common beginner risks and identify safe boundaries and responses. | Theoretical |
| `C8` | Compare beginner Bitcoin payment and access pathways in guided scenarios. | Theoretical |
| `C9` | Safely complete and verify a supported first-access wallet activity, including receipt of bitcoin or a safe simulated receipt, in an approved low-stakes or simulated setting. | Practical: direct observation |
| `C10` | Demonstrate recovery readiness and protection of recovery information. | Practical: direct observation |
| `C11` | Complete one approved locally relevant Bitcoin-as-money task and connect it to its local purpose, realistic alternative, trade-offs, and next step. | Practical: direct observation with embedded explanation |

The theoretical component (50%) and practical component (50%) are separately required and non-compensatory. The theoretical component is evidenced through approved educator-evaluated learner actions. The practical component is evidenced through direct educator observation. Every assessed competency must meet its approved standard. Optional app self-checks may support practice and review, but cannot supply either component.

Use these stable competency and criterion codes in activities, practical tasks, rubrics, optional self-checks, and completion records.

## Safety and assessment boundaries

- Use testnet, prepared wallets, simulations, or appropriately low-stakes transactions where needed to protect learners.
- Never require a learner to expose a seed phrase, private key, wallet credential, or unnecessary personal financial information.
- Do not use attendance, enthusiasm, confidence, app completion, learner self-report, or watching a demonstration as evidence that a competency is met.
- A local adaptation may change examples, language, tools, and task setup, but may not remove a required learner action, weaken a safety boundary, or turn a pathway into financial, legal, tax, accounting, compliance, or investment advice.

## Assessed competencies and required observable criteria

### Module 1: Understanding Money

#### C1 — Explain why people use money and compare forms of money

**Competency statement:** The learner gives a beginner-appropriate explanation of money and applies shared monetary characteristics in a comparison.

**Evidence form:** An individual explanation, classification, illustration, or guided comparison observed and evaluated by an educator.

| Required observable criterion | The educator must be able to observe the learner… |
|---|---|
| C1.1 | Explain what money is and why societies use it in their own words. |
| C1.2 | Describe a beginner history and evolution of money, including barter and the limits that led societies toward other forms of money. |
| C1.3 | Identify the functions and properties of money through a classification, example, or explanation. |
| C1.4 | Compare different forms of money using beginner monetary characteristics. |

#### C2 — Explain a modern-money trade-off through an example

**Competency statement:** The learner uses a guided, observed, local, or realistic situation to explain an introductory modern-money problem or trade-off without making universal claims or requiring personal financial disclosure.

**Evidence form:** An individual explanation, scenario response, or guided reflection observed and evaluated by an educator.

| Required observable criterion | The educator must be able to observe the learner… |
|---|---|
| C2.1 | Explain at an introductory scope how inflation, purchasing-power loss, fractional-reserve banking, and CBDCs can create problems in modern money systems. |
| C2.2 | Relate at least one observed, local, or realistic money experience to a monetary property or modern-money problem without personal financial disclosure. |

### Module 2: How Bitcoin Works

#### C3 — Explain why Bitcoin was created

**Competency statement:** The learner gives an introductory, own-words explanation of the digital-money problem Bitcoin was designed to address.

**Evidence form:** An individual explanation, illustration, or guided scenario response observed and evaluated by an educator.

| Required observable criterion | The educator must be able to observe the learner… |
|---|---|
| C3.1 | Explain why Bitcoin was created, including the digital-copy and double-spend problem. |

#### C4 — Explain Bitcoin's key properties and make a guided sound-money judgment

**Competency statement:** The learner explains beginner Bitcoin properties and roles, then applies beginner monetary characteristics to a qualified sound-money judgment.

**Evidence form:** An individual explanation, role map, concept illustration, or guided comparison observed and evaluated by an educator.

| Required observable criterion | The educator must be able to observe the learner… |
|---|---|
| C4.1 | Explain Bitcoin's key properties: decentralization, credible finite supply, peer-to-peer transfer, and verifiability, including the beginner meaning of “don't trust, verify.” |
| C4.2 | Describe the beginner roles of users, nodes, miners, and developers and the beginner relevance of proof of work, the 21 million limit, halving, and satoshis. |
| C4.3 | Assess whether Bitcoin can be described as sound money by applying beginner monetary characteristics and explaining the limits of that judgment. |

#### C5 — Compare Bitcoin with non-Bitcoin digital assets and distinguish responsible use from speculation

**Competency statement:** The learner makes a guided Bitcoin-only comparison and explains the Diploma's responsible-learning and responsible-use boundary.

**Evidence form:** An individual guided comparison, scenario response, or explanation observed and evaluated by an educator.

| Required observable criterion | The educator must be able to observe the learner… |
|---|---|
| C5.1 | Compare Bitcoin with non-Bitcoin crypto-assets, including stablecoins, using issuer or control, supply or issuance, verification, custody, and counterparty considerations in a guided scenario. |
| C5.2 | Explain why the Diploma treats Bitcoin learning and responsible use as different from short-term trading, token promotion, or speculation, including the beginner risks of each. |

### Module 3: Using Bitcoin Safely

#### C6 — Explain wallet, custody, and hardware-wallet basics

**Competency statement:** The learner uses essential wallet and custody language accurately in a guided explanation or comparison.

**Evidence form:** An individual explanation, comparison, or scenario response observed and evaluated by an educator.

| Required observable criterion | The educator must be able to observe the learner… |
|---|---|
| C6.1 | Explain wallets, addresses, keys, seed phrases, and custody through essential terms and examples. |
| C6.2 | Compare custodial and non-custodial approaches in guided scenarios. |
| C6.3 | Identify what hardware wallets are, why people use them, and how they differ from hot wallets in a practical overview. |

#### C7 — Recognize common beginner risks and identify safe boundaries and responses

**Competency statement:** The learner recognizes a beginner risk or high-stakes boundary and gives a safe response, including when to stop or seek help.

**Evidence form:** An individual safety scenario response, explanation, or guided discussion observed and evaluated by an educator.

| Required observable criterion | The educator must be able to observe the learner… |
|---|---|
| C7.1 | Identify common beginner risks and explain the safe response to seed phrase exposure, address mistakes, copy/paste attacks, impersonation, urgency scams, and too-good-to-be-true offers. |
| C7.2 | Distinguish beginner practical Bitcoin use from advanced technical or high-stakes activity and identify when to stop or seek help. |

#### C8 — Compare beginner Bitcoin payment and access pathways

**Competency statement:** The learner compares common beginner pathways in guided scenarios without treating any pathway as a recommendation or financial advice.

**Evidence form:** An individual guided scenario response or comparison observed and evaluated by an educator.

| Required observable criterion | The educator must be able to observe the learner… |
|---|---|
| C8.1 | Distinguish on-chain and Lightning payments and select an appropriate beginner pathway in a guided scenario. |
| C8.2 | Compare beginner pathways for acquiring or exchanging bitcoin, including relevant risks, without treating any pathway as a recommendation or financial advice. |

#### C9 — Safely complete and verify a supported first-access wallet activity

**Competency statement:** In an approved low-stakes or simulated environment, the learner safely completes the required wallet actions, receives bitcoin or safely simulates receipt, and checks the information that makes the action reliable.

**Evidence form:** Direct educator observation. A spoken answer, self-report, app completion, or watching a demonstration cannot replace the observed performance.

| Required observable criterion | The educator must directly observe the learner… |
|---|---|
| C9.1 | Demonstrate safe setup or opening and navigation of an approved or prepared wallet environment, including locating and checking receive information. |
| C9.2 | Complete or safely simulate a beginner receive or send flow and verify the relevant address, invoice, amount, confirmation, or transaction status. |
| C9.3 | Gain access to bitcoin in an approved low-stakes or simulated environment by completing a supported first-access pathway, receiving the bitcoin, and checking the relevant wallet and transaction information. |

#### C10 — Demonstrate recovery readiness and protection of recovery information

**Competency statement:** The learner demonstrates a safe recovery-readiness process with prepared or simulated materials while protecting all recovery information.

**Evidence form:** Direct educator observation. Never use or expose a real seed phrase, private key, credential, or unnecessary personal financial information.

| Required observable criterion | The educator must directly observe the learner… |
|---|---|
| C10.1 | Demonstrate recovery readiness and protection of recovery information using prepared or simulated materials without exposing real secrets. |

### Module 4: Bitcoin in Your Community

Module 4 is the **Connect** stage of the Diploma's Problem → Solution → Action → Connect arc. It requires learners to connect the shared learning from Modules 1–3 to a local need or opportunity, an appropriate comparison, safe performance, and a realistic next step.

#### C11 — Complete and explain one approved locally relevant Bitcoin-as-money task

**Competency statement:** The learner completes one approved local task safely and connects the Module 1 problem, Module 2 response, and Module 3 safe action to its local purpose, realistic alternative, and next step.

**Evidence form:** Direct educator observation of an approved variant, with an individual explanation during or immediately after the performance.

| Required observable criterion | The educator must directly observe or evaluate the learner… |
|---|---|
| C11.1 | Demonstrate at least one approved, locally relevant Bitcoin-as-money task using the required safe sequence and verification behaviors. |
| C11.2 | Explain the local purpose, realistic alternative, relevant trade-offs, failure points, safety considerations, and realistic next steps as part of the approved practical performance. |

Local tasks may differ, but every learner seeking certification must meet the same requirements for safe performance, verification, explanation, and trade-off awareness. A local task must have an approved variant code and use equivalent observable criteria.

Possible local tasks include practical workshops, demonstrations, local tools, local expert guidance, and community-specific examples. Depending on local context, a selected task may involve:

- discussing local Bitcoin use cases;
- comparing local money tools and trade-offs;
- exploring circular economies;
- completing tool- or wallet-specific demonstrations;
- practising guided testnet or low-stakes self-custody demonstrations when educator preparation and learner safety allow;
- demonstrating a local payment or receiving scenario;
- demonstrating a Lightning or on-chain payment scenario, including invoices or Lightning addresses where local tools, connectivity, and safety conditions make them appropriate;
- comparing local on-ramp and off-ramp options without endorsing a specific provider;
- comparing KYC and no-KYC trade-offs where the educator can frame local legal, privacy, and safety context responsibly;
- comparing local payment, remittance, merchant, or community donation scenarios using fees, timing, custody, privacy, reversibility, and failure points;
- identifying resources used by local bitcoiners;
- identifying local resource maps, merchants, circular economies, and community groups where relevant;
- identifying locally available low-cost hardware wallet options where relevant;
- connecting Bitcoin concepts to local economic conditions; or
- identifying realistic next steps for the community.

Other local learning remains optional unless it is included in the approved task variant.

#### Selectable local variant: Dual-rail payment practice

Where local tools, connectivity, educator preparation, and learner safety conditions allow, educators may select a Dual-rail payment practice variant. In an approved low-stakes or simulated environment, the learner must:

- complete one on-chain receive or send flow and one Lightning receive or send flow;
- verify the relevant address or invoice, amount, and transaction or payment status for each flow; and
- explain at least one practical difference between the two flows, such as the payment information used, settlement expectations, fees, or failure points.

This is an approved local variant for C11.1 and C11.2. It does not make transacting on both rails a required Diploma competency.

## Formative competencies

The following competencies guide feedback and reflection. They are not certification gates, are not part of the 11 assessed competencies, and do not change the two-component completion requirement.

| Formative competency | Educator observation or learner action |
|---|---|
| C12 — Guided discussion | Participate in guided discussion using clear explanations, relevant evidence, respectful listening, and basic reasoning. |
| C13 — Continued learning reflection | Articulate a personal reason to continue learning about Bitcoin, or a reason for remaining uncertain, using course concepts and an observed, local, or realistic money experience without making an investment claim. |

These may be supported through feedback, not awarded through a certification record.

## Advanced competencies

Advanced competencies are outside the four-module Diploma.

They should be designed module by module after the redesigned beginner Diploma is published. Candidate Advanced module titles are maintained in 17_advanced.md and should not become required Diploma competencies.

Advanced material may include detailed Bretton Woods and geopolitical monetary history, exact node mechanics, connecting a wallet or application to a Bitcoin node, exact mining mechanics, hash functions, mempool mechanics, transaction lifecycle details, Lightning node operations, channel management, deep no-KYC acquisition trade-offs, deeper hardware-wallet security models such as open-source design, Bitcoin-only firmware, and airgapped signing, and Organizations-derived topics such as treasury, institutional custody, organizational risk, policy, sector impacts, and on-chain analytics.

## Module-to-competency map

| Module | Learner promise | Assessed competency emphasis |
|---|---|---|
| Understanding Money | Learners can explain and apply the money problems Bitcoin responds to. | C1 and C2: explain why money exists, describe barter and its limits, identify and compare monetary characteristics, explain introductory modern-money trade-offs, and connect a scenario to a money concept. |
| How Bitcoin Works | Learners can explain and illustrate why Bitcoin was created and what makes it different. | C3, C4, and C5: explain Bitcoin's purpose, digital scarcity, double spending, credible finite supply, satoshis, peer-to-peer transfer, decentralization, verification, sound-money reasoning, and its distinction from stablecoins and other non-Bitcoin crypto-assets. |
| Using Bitcoin Safely | Learners can explain responsibility and demonstrate required beginner actions safely under supported conditions. | C6–C10: explain relevant concepts, compare pathways, demonstrate wallet navigation, a supported first-access pathway, receiving or sending, verification, recovery readiness, risk response, and safe limits. |
| Connect: Bitcoin in Your Community | Learners can connect the Problem, Solution, and Action learning to an approved locally relevant Bitcoin-as-money capability. | C11: educator-curated and directly assessed local task using tools, simulations, testnet or low-stakes practice, payment or receiving scenarios, local resources, workshops, or case examples, plus a realistic alternative and responsible next step. |

This map guides assessment design, activity design, Educator Guide pacing, and public communication.

## Cognitive demand

The Diploma focuses on:

- Remember: define key concepts.
- Explain: articulate functions, systems, and relationships in the learner's own words.
- Apply: use concepts in guided scenarios.

The Diploma may introduce:

- Analyze: compare simple trade-offs with educator support.
- Evaluate: assess simple claims using guided prompts.

Create-level tasks belong in educator-curated Module 4 learning within the Diploma or Advanced material unless an approved local task explicitly includes them.

## Competency statement pattern

Each module should define:

- stable competency and criterion codes;
- key idea and guiding question;
- required assessed competencies and observable criteria;
- educator-curated Module 4 application, where relevant;
- Advanced or out-of-scope boundaries;
- practical orientation;
- discussion competence;
- review point;
- required assessment type, evidence, and evaluator;
- translation or localization notes where terminology may not transfer cleanly; and
- delivery-mode notes where evidence of learning changes.

Use this wording pattern:

Learners demonstrate that they can [observable verb] [beginner-appropriate concept or action] [condition or support level].

Example:

Learners demonstrate that they can compare custodial and non-custodial wallets using beginner language and guided examples.
