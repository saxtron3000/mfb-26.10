# Bitcoin Diploma competency framework

## Purpose and authority

The [Educator Guide's operational assessment protocol](../publish/mfb-bd-26.10/mfb-bd-eg-26.10.md#operational-assessment-protocol) is the publication source of truth for the seven assessed competencies, required criteria, evidence, and educator judgments. This planning map shows how they fit the four-module Diploma. The [Assessment Grid](../publish/mfb-bd-26.10/mfb-bd-assessment-grid-26.10.md) records one result per assessed competency.

Eight hours is the shortest planned route, with no upper limit. Educators extend teaching, practice, observation, and reassessment as needed. A certificate depends on demonstrated competency, never time attended. Educators may adapt examples, language, access methods, tools, and local scenarios while preserving the required learner action, safety boundary, and standard.

The two non-compensatory components are theory (`C1`–`C5`) and directly observed practice (`C6`–`C7`). Both must meet standard. Decimal identifiers are required look-fors within the parent competency, not additional result rows. `F1` (reasoned discussion) and `F2` (course reflection) are formative and are not certification gates.

## Seven assessed competencies

| Module | Code and competency | Required look-fors | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Understanding Money | `C1` Money and modern-money trade-offs | `C1.1` Explain why people use money, barter limits, the beginner evolution of money, and its functions and properties.<br>`C1.2` Compare two forms of money using relevant characteristics.<br>`C1.3` Explain one modern-money trade-off through a local, observed, or realistic example without personal financial disclosure. | Individual educator-evaluated explanation, comparison, and scenario response. |
| 2. How Bitcoin Works | `C2` Bitcoin foundations and rules | `C2.1` Explain digital copying or double spending and how shared verification helps.<br>`C2.2` Explain beginner Bitcoin rules and roles, including users, nodes, miners, developers, proof of work, the 21 million supply limit, halving, and satoshis at beginner scope.<br>`C2.3` Relate decentralization, finite supply, peer-to-peer transfer, and verifiability to a reasoned money-property benefit or limitation, including “don't trust, verify.” | Individual educator-evaluated explanation, role map, and qualified judgment. |
| 2. How Bitcoin Works | `C3` Bitcoin and digital-asset comparison | Compare Bitcoin with another digital asset, including a generic stablecoin where useful, using relevant criteria such as control, issuance, verification, custody, or counterparty risk; state a trade-off and distinguish education and responsible use from speculation or promotion. | One individual guided comparison; no numbered sub-points. |
| 3. Using Bitcoin Safely | `C4` Wallets, custody, and safety | `C4.1` Explain wallets, addresses, keys, seed phrases, public versus secret information, and a custodial versus non-custodial trade-off.<br>`C4.2` Distinguish hot and hardware wallets, including why people use the latter, at beginner scope.<br>`C4.3` Recognize a scam, error, or high-risk request, including secret exposure, address mistakes, impersonation, or urgency, and explain when to stop, verify, or seek help. | Individual educator-evaluated explanation and safety scenario. |
| 3. Using Bitcoin Safely | `C5` Payment and access pathways | `C5.1` Compare on-chain and Lightning payments and choose a suitable beginner pathway in a guided example.<br>`C5.2` Compare access or exchange pathways and their risks without recommending a provider or financial action. | Individual educator-evaluated pathway comparison. |
| 3. Using Bitcoin Safely | `C6` Safe access and recovery readiness | `C6.1` In an approved low-stakes or simulated setting, navigate a prepared wallet, complete or safely simulate a receive-or-send flow and supported first access or receipt, and verify the relevant destination, amount, network, and status.<br>`C6.2` Demonstrate recovery readiness using prepared or simulated materials while protecting all real secrets. | Direct educator observation of both distinct practical performances; one final result. |
| 4. Connect: Bitcoin in Your Community | `C7` Local Bitcoin-as-money task | `C7.1` Safely perform one educator-selected locally relevant task or simulation, including the required checks.<br>`C7.2` Explain its local purpose, realistic alternative, trade-offs, possible failure points, safety considerations, and realistic next step. | Direct educator observation of individual performance and explanation. |

For `C3`, the comparison statement itself is the required criterion; it is deliberately not divided into numbered sub-points. Each other parent has at most three required look-fors. A single activity may reveal several look-fors, but each parent competency has one `MS` (Meets standard) or `NY` (Not yet) result. An unmet or unsafe required look-for means `NY`, followed by practice and reassessment.

## Safety and local scope

Use prepared wallets, simulations, testnet, physical tokens, or appropriately low-stakes routes. Never require personal funds, disclosure of a real seed phrase or key, or a particular app. Watching, attendance, confidence, self-report, and optional [My First Bitcoin App](https://myfirstbitcoin.app/) results do not establish competency. Practical evidence requires direct educator observation.

**Module 4** is required and educator-curated. A local task may concern a payment, receiving, a merchant, remittance, donation, or community scenario if the educator can preserve `C7.1` and `C7.2`. A dual-rail payment exercise may be selected locally; it does not become a global requirement. Local tasks may not be used to recommend a product or give financial, legal, tax, accounting, compliance, or investment advice.

## Graduate capability profile

A beginner Diploma graduate can explain why money is used, compare money forms and Bitcoin with another digital asset, reason about Bitcoin's beginner rules and properties, recognize practical risks, and safely complete supported wallet, recovery-readiness, and local tasks. The Diploma does not claim independent high-stakes custody, advanced technical troubleshooting, professional expertise, or endorsement of a financial decision. Equivalent localized or accessible routes must show the same individual understanding and action.

## Module-to-competency map

| Module | Learner promise | Assessed emphasis |
|---|---|---|
| Understanding Money | Explain and apply the money problems Bitcoin responds to. | `C1`: money's purpose, barter limits, monetary comparison, and one modern-money trade-off. |
| How Bitcoin Works | Explain why Bitcoin was created, its beginner rules and roles, and a qualified property judgment. | `C2` and `C3`: digital copying, shared verification, network roles, sound-money reasoning with limits, and a guided comparison with another digital asset. |
| Using Bitcoin Safely | Explain responsibility and demonstrate safe beginner actions under supported conditions. | `C4`–`C6`: wallets, custody, risk response, payment and access pathways, first access or simulated receipt, verification, and recovery readiness. |
| Connect: Bitcoin in Your Community | Connect the earlier learning to a locally relevant Bitcoin-as-money task. | `C7`: direct individual observation of a safe local performance, realistic alternative, trade-offs, failure points, and next step. |

## Locally selected practice

The selected `C7` task must preserve safe performance, verification, individual explanation, and trade-off awareness. Depending on context, it may use a payment or receiving scenario, merchant or refund flow, remittance or community-donation simulation, circular-economy resource map, local tool demonstration, or another approved equivalent. An educator must check the relevant local rules, tool suitability, connectivity, accessibility, privacy, and available expertise before selecting a variant. Discussion or a team presentation alone cannot establish the directly observed practical result.

Where suitable, an educator may choose a dual-rail payment variant: the learner completes or simulates one on-chain and one Lightning receive or send flow, checks the relevant payment information and status for each, and explains a practical difference. The variant may support `C7.1` and `C7.2`; transacting on both rails is not required for every Diploma learner.

## Formative learning and advanced boundaries

`F1` guided discussion uses clear explanation, relevant evidence, respectful listening, and basic reasoning. `F2` continued-learning reflection invites a learner to explain a reason to continue studying Bitcoin or to remain uncertain, using course concepts and a local, observed, or realistic money experience without an investment claim. These support feedback and do not create result rows.

Advanced learning remains outside the beginner Diploma: exact node and mining mechanics, hash functions and mempool details, Lightning channel operations, advanced hardware-wallet security, organizational treasury and custody, legal, tax, accounting, compliance, investment, and policy implementation. See `17_advanced.md` for the parked pathway and source trail.

The beginner Diploma emphasizes explaining and applying concepts in guided situations. Simple comparison and evaluation are appropriate with educator support. More demanding creation belongs in an educator-selected local task only when its approved variant includes it, or in Advanced learning.

## Implementation questions

Before local certificated delivery, check the selected cohort size and assessor capacity, actual time for individual evidence and reassessment, privacy and correction arrangements, accessible routes, and online observation safeguards. `15_certification.md` tracks decentralized educator-issued certificate governance. These questions do not change the seven competency standards.

## Historical code migration

The former 11-code system is retained here only to support migration and checking. Old `C1` and `C2` map to new `C1`; old `C3` and `C4` to new `C2`; old `C5` to new `C3`; old `C6` and `C7` to new `C4`; old `C8` to new `C5`; old `C9` and `C10` to new `C6`; and old `C11` to new `C7`. Former formative `C12` and `C13` are now `F1` and `F2`. Old protocol tasks `P9` and `P10` are two performances for new `C6`; old `P11` is the new `C7` local task.
