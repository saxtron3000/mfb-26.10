# Bitcoin Diploma Draft Scaffold

## Purpose

This document provides the draft content scaffold for the redesigned Bitcoin Diploma. It is not the final Student Book. It gives writers a shared structure for drafting learner-facing content after the planning documents are approved.

---

# Drafting Rules

- Use the 4-block structure from `06_plan.md`.
- Keep learner-facing explanations short, clear, and beginner-appropriate.
- Move full activity instructions to `10_activities.md`.
- Move facilitation notes to `11_guide.md`.
- Keep Advanced topics out of the Core beginner pathway.
- Use local examples only when they can be adapted or replaced.
- Verify current-data examples or rewrite them as evergreen explanations before publication.

---

# Block 1: Problem / Fiat

## Learner Promise

Learners understand the money problem Bitcoin responds to.

## Draft Content Needs

- Opening question about money
- Plain-language explanation of money
- Functions and properties of money
- Barter and the double coincidence of wants
- Scarcity, value, exchange, and purchasing power
- Beginner psychology of money: scarcity, time preference, and opportunity cost
- Brief path from barter to modern money and digital money, including the Nixon Shock only as a short historical marker
- Fiat money, inflation, fractional reserve banking, and bank runs at beginner level
- Who participates in the fiat system: central banks, commercial banks, governments, and citizens
- CBDCs as a beginner fiat-system example where appropriate
- Reflection on why people look for alternatives
- Block review: learners explain the fiat problem in their own words

## Activity References

- Good forms of money
- Barter scenario
- Purchasing power discussion
- Fractional reserve banking demonstration
- CBDC scenario discussion where appropriate
- Fiat problem reflection

# Block 2: Solution / Bitcoin

## Learner Promise

Learners understand why Bitcoin was created and how it differs from fiat money.

## Draft Content Needs

- Search for digital and decentralized money
- Cypherpunk context
- Early digital-money attempts and why centralized designs failed
- The double-spend problem at beginner level
- Bitcoin origin story at beginner level
- Bitcoin origin timeline: whitepaper, Genesis Block, first transaction, and Pizza Day
- Decentralization
- Users, nodes, and miners
- Proof of work at a beginner level
- 21 million supply and halving
- Satoshis and unit bias
- Peer-to-peer transfer and verification
- Bitcoin as sound digital money
- Personal responsibility as transition into self-custody
- Beginner-level benefits of Bitcoin and the trade-offs learners should still consider
- Block review: learners explain how Bitcoin responds to fiat problems

## Activity References

- Build a blockchain
- Bitcoin properties comparison
- Sats / unit-bias concept check
- Bitcoin origin timeline activity
- Benefits and trade-offs map
- Claim / evidence / reasoning discussion

# Block 3: Action / Self-custody

## Learner Promise

Learners understand what responsibility looks like when using and securing Bitcoin.

## Draft Content Needs

- Wallets as interfaces
- Addresses, keys, and seed phrases
- Custodial and non-custodial use
- Hot and cold wallet distinction
- Acquiring and exchanging bitcoin as a concept, not financial advice
- Receiving bitcoin
- Sending bitcoin
- Wallet recovery and seed phrase testing
- Verification, block explorer orientation, and irreversible transactions
- Safety practices: seed phrase protection, address verification, copy/paste risks, impersonation, urgency scams, too-good-to-be-true offers, open-source wallet preference, and starting small
- On-chain and Lightning payment distinction at beginner level
- Light transaction orientation only where it helps learners understand responsibility
- Block review: learners explain what self-custody responsibility means

## Activity References

- Wallet type identification
- Address identification
- Receive-screen walkthrough
- Seed phrase responsibility scenario
- Wallet recovery scenario
- Guided send/receive demonstration where appropriate
- Verification checklist
- Acquiring/exchanging pathway risk map
- Scam pattern scenario
- On-chain vs Lightning comparison

# Block 4: Custom / Bitcoin as Money

## Learner Promise

Learners see how Bitcoin can function as money in their local context.

## Draft Content Needs

This block is educator-selected. The Student Book should explain the purpose of the block and point educators to Custom templates.

Possible learner-facing framing:

Bitcoin becomes real when learners can connect it to their own community, tools, risks, and opportunities.

Custom content may draw from daily-life Bitcoin use, Lightning payment demonstrations, local merchant scenarios, local on-ramp and off-ramp pathways, local resource maps, circular economies, and community discovery. Named tools and services should appear only where they can be localized, replaced, and framed without endorsement.

## Activity References

- Local wallet demonstration
- Local types-of-money comparison
- Lightning wallet setup and payment demonstration where locally appropriate
- Lightning invoice or Lightning address walkthrough where locally appropriate
- Merchant payment scenario
- Everyday purchase scenario such as coffee, groceries, or local equivalent
- Local on-ramp / off-ramp comparison
- KYC / no-KYC local trade-off discussion where appropriate
- Local debt or inequality case discussion where appropriate
- Circular economy mapping
- Local Bitcoin resource map
- Community problem / Bitcoin-as-money opportunity analysis
