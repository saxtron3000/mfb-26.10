Use locally familiar goods and services. The exact item does not matter; the payment reasoning does.

**Scenario 1: A small purchase**

A customer wants to buy an everyday item. The merchant accepts Lightning and on-chain Bitcoin.

- Which path is likely to suit a small, immediate payment?
- Who creates the payment request?
- How does the customer check the amount and unit?
- How does the merchant know the payment succeeded?
- What happens if the customer's wallet shows “sent” but the merchant does not see “received”?

**Scenario 2: Payment for work**

A worker agrees to receive part of a payment in bitcoin.

- Is the price fixed in local currency, bitcoin, or sats?
- At what time is the exchange rate determined?
- Who pays any fee?
- Is the receiver using self-custody?
- What income or tax record is required?
- Can the worker safely manage the amount?

**Scenario 3: A transfer between family members**

One person wants to send value to a family member in another place.

- What are the costs and delays of the current method?
- How will each person obtain or use bitcoin?
- Does the recipient want bitcoin or local currency?
- Which exchange and custody risks appear at each step?
- Does the full route save time or money after all fees are included?

**Scenario 4: A refund**

A merchant needs to return value after an on-chain payment.

- Why should the merchant request a fresh refund address?
- How can the merchant verify that the request really came from the customer?
- Who pays the return transaction fee?
- What record should both parties keep?

**Scenario 5: A community donation**

A local organization wants to receive support in bitcoin.

- How will donors verify the real payment request?
- Who controls the receiving wallet and backup?
- Will donations be received on-chain, through Lightning, or both?
- What information should be public for transparency, and what should remain private for donor safety?
- What records are needed for local accounting, tax, or reporting rules?

For a live practice, use a low-value item or a simulation. The learner should be able to explain the complete flow before approving payment.

BINDING VISUAL STYLE
****# My First Bitcoin illustration style guide

Apply a consistent My First Bitcoin illustration style.

The illustration should look like it belongs to one unified educational series created by the same human illustrator. Prioritize brand consistency, clarity, warmth, and simplicity over visual complexity.

## Visual identity

Use a clean, minimal, modern educational illustration style inspired by My First Bitcoin’s brand system. The image should feel approachable, trustworthy, open-source, community-based, and beginner-friendly. It should support learning without feeling childish, corporate, futuristic, or decorative.

## Line work

Use simple vector-style line art with consistent line weight throughout the image. Lines should feel intentional, smooth, and hand-guided, with rounded corners and soft joins. Avoid sketchiness, scratchy outlines, hyper-detailed rendering, or inconsistent AI-generated line weights.

## Shapes

Use simplified geometric forms, soft curves, rounded rectangles, circles, and clean silhouettes. Keep objects readable at small sizes. Avoid excessive detail, visual noise, tiny decorations, or unnecessary background elements.

## Color

Use the My First Bitcoin brand palette as the foundation, especially purple, white, soft violet, and restrained warm orange accents. Purple should carry the main identity. White or off-white should provide breathing room. Orange should be used sparingly for emphasis, especially for Bitcoin-related focus points. Do not overuse orange.

## Composition

Keep the image calm, spacious, and balanced. Use one clear focal point. Maintain generous negative space. The illustration should be easy to understand quickly and should work in a student guide, educator guide, slide deck, or worksheet.

## Human tone

If people are included, make them simple, friendly, diverse, and grounded. Use posture and gesture more than facial detail. Avoid exaggerated cartoon expressions, corporate avatar clichés, or overly polished stock characters.

## Bitcoin tone

Treat Bitcoin as education, empowerment, savings, self-custody, community, and open-source learning. Avoid price hype, trading culture, luxury imagery, rockets, cyberpunk visuals, speculative finance, memes, or aggressive tech aesthetics.

## AI avoidance

Avoid common generic AI illustration traits: glowing circuits, random floating icons, fake interface text, excessive gradients, shiny 3D objects, unrealistic hands, cluttered symbolism, meaningless futuristic backgrounds, and overproduced lighting. The image should feel intentionally designed, not automatically generated.

## Overall result

A minimal, friendly, purple-led My First Bitcoin educational illustration that looks consistent with the rest of the series and suitable for beginner Bitcoin education.
