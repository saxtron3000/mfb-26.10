# Bitcoin Transactions in Action
## Keywords
**Nodes** **Miners**

## Activity Type
Role play

## Participation
Participants act as senders, receivers, nodes, or miners.
## Duration

## Objective
- Participants practice the roles of senders, receivers, nodes, and miners in validating and adding a simulated Bitcoin transaction.
# Description
Participants role-play senders, receivers, nodes, and miners to create, verify, validate, and add [peer-to-peer](../mfb-lx-26.10.md#term-peer-to-peer-network) Bitcoin transactions to a classroom blockchain. Transaction notes, coins, approval tables, and dice make the flow of a payment visible, including checks for valid addresses, sufficient funds, and [double spending](../mfb-lx-26.10.md#term-double-spending).
# Preparation

## Materials
- Transaction notes
- Coins
- Approval tables
- Dice

Assign participants the roles of sender, receiver, node, or miner.

## Setup
Explain each role: senders create and [broadcast](../mfb-lx-26.10.md#term-broadcast); receivers receive and verify; nodes validate; miners add transactions to the blockchain.
# Procedure
## Step 1
Senders write the amount and receiver on a transaction note, sign it, and pass the note and coins to the receiver.
## Step 2
Receivers check the note and coin count, then approve or reject. Nodes validate [addresses](../mfb-lx-26.10.md#term-bitcoin-address), sufficient funds, and absence of double spending.
## Step 3
Miners review approved transactions, roll dice, and the miner with the smallest number adds the transaction to the blockchain. Participants keep track of their coin balances.
# Review
## Key Points
Once added to the blockchain, a transaction cannot be changed or reversed.
