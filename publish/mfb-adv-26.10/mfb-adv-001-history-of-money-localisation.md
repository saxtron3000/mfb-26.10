# Advanced Module 1: History of Money (localisation)

## TL;DR

Study how money changed over time, then investigate the specific history, institutions, and experiences that shape money where learners live. This module uses local evidence and multiple perspectives; it does not assume one country’s history represents everyone’s.

## Purpose

Money has developed through social relationships, technology, law, trade, conflict, and institutions. Learners trace a broad path from barter and commodity money to bank money and digital payments, then test that story against their own local context.

## Learners will be able to

- distinguish historical examples of barter, commodity money, coin, paper money, bank deposits, and digital payments
- describe important local monetary events with dated, credible sources
- compare how a change in money affected different people and groups; and
- separate historical evidence from simplified stories about money.

## Suggested learning sequence

1. Map a broad history of money and identify what it leaves out.
2. Research a local monetary timeline with community sources, archives, and reliable reporting.
3. Compare perspectives: workers, savers, businesses, banks, and public institutions.
4. Present a sourced local case study and name remaining uncertainties.

## Boundary

> **Callout – Verify local history and keep the boundary clear**
>
> Local history must be researched and dated before publication or teaching. This module explains history; it does not provide financial, legal, tax, or investment advice.
