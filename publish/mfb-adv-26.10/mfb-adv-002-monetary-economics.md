# Advanced Module 2: Monetary Economics

## TL;DR

Examine how money, credit, banking, incentives, prices, and time preference interact. Learners use models and fictional cases to reason about trade-offs, rather than receiving predictions or investment recommendations.

## Purpose

This module develops careful economic reasoning beyond the beginner Diploma. It considers purchasing power, money and credit creation, bank balance sheets, liquidity and solvency, debt, monetary policy, and the distributional questions raised by changing money and credit conditions.

## Learners will be able to

- explain key concepts such as opportunity cost, time preference, liquidity, solvency, and purchasing power;
- read a simplified bank balance sheet and distinguish assets from liabilities;
- compare competing explanations for a change in prices or economic conditions; and
- identify assumptions, evidence limits, and missing information in an economic claim.

## Suggested learning sequence

1. Review scarcity, choice, and time preference.
2. Explore money, credit, and bank balance sheets through fictional cases.
3. Compare monetary and fiscal-policy tools, incentives, and constraints.
4. Examine distribution, timing, and uncertainty in a sourced local or historical case.

## Boundary

> **Callout – Models explain trade-offs; they do not make personal decisions**
>
> Use fictional scenarios rather than learners’ finances. The module must not recommend bitcoin, a portfolio allocation, a provider, or a timing decision.
