# Advanced Module 7: Privacy, Security, and Online Safety

## TL;DR

Learn to recognize digital threats and make proportionate safety choices when using Bitcoin online. Privacy and security are not single settings: they depend on a person’s situation, tools, habits, and potential adversaries.

## Purpose

This module introduces threat modelling, phishing and impersonation, device and account security, transaction privacy, metadata, public-address sharing, and careful online communication. Learners practise identifying risks before choosing a response.

## Learners will be able to

- build a basic threat model for a fictional situation;
- identify common scams, social-engineering tactics, and unsafe requests;
- explain trade-offs among convenience, privacy, verification, and recovery; and
- create a personal online-safety checklist that contains no sensitive information.

## Suggested learning sequence

1. Identify assets, adversaries, likely harms, and realistic protections.
2. Analyse examples of phishing, impersonation, and scam pressure.
3. Explore transaction privacy and the consequences of sharing information.
4. Practise an incident-response discussion: pause, verify, seek support, and document safely.

## Boundary

> **Callout – Privacy is not a guarantee or a route around lawful obligations**
>
> Educators should use fictional examples and direct learners facing abuse, theft, or urgent danger to appropriate local support.
