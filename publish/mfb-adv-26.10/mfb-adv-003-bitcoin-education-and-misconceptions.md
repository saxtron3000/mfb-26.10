# Advanced Module 3: Bitcoin Education and Misconceptions

## TL;DR

Learn to explain Bitcoin accurately, identify common misunderstandings, and design learning that welcomes questions instead of demanding agreement. Good Bitcoin education makes evidence, trade-offs, and uncertainty visible.

## Purpose

Bitcoin is often presented through slogans, fear, hype, or technical jargon. This module builds practical communication and teaching skills so learners can distinguish claims from evidence and support others without becoming promoters.

## Learners will be able to

- identify misconceptions about money, Bitcoin, mining, privacy, and custody
- explain a Bitcoin concept at beginner and advanced levels without oversimplifying it
- distinguish facts, opinions, forecasts, and marketing claims; and
- design an inclusive, locally relevant learning activity with clear safety boundaries.

## Suggested learning sequence

1. Collect common questions and claims from local conversations.
2. Check each claim against primary sources or reproducible evidence.
3. Practise plain-language explanations and respectful disagreement.
4. Test a short teaching activity, gather feedback, and revise it.

## Boundary

> **Callout – Do not pressure learners or promote products**
>
> Do not promise outcomes, pressure learners to acquire bitcoin, or use education to promote named products or services. Treat local experience as valuable evidence, not as universal proof.
