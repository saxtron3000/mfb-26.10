# Advanced Module 5: Mining and Consensus

## TL;DR

Understand how independently operated nodes and miners apply Bitcoin’s shared rules. The module covers transaction relay, proof of work, difficulty adjustment, block propagation, and the limits of what miners can decide.

## Purpose

Bitcoin’s network works through distributed verification and economic incentives, not through a central operator. Learners explore how transactions move toward settlement, how blocks are constructed, and why full nodes enforce the rules they choose to run.

## Learners will be able to

- distinguish the roles of users, nodes, miners, developers, and economic nodes
- describe the path from transaction relay to block confirmation
- explain proof of work and difficulty adjustment in plain language; and
- evaluate claims about mining power, rule changes, and decentralization.

## Suggested learning sequence

1. Model relay and independent verification.
2. Explore candidate blocks, hashing, and proof of work.
3. Examine difficulty, propagation, forks, and consensus rules.
4. Discuss incentives, trade-offs, and the role of open-source review.

## Boundary

> **Callout – Do not present mining as an income opportunity**
>
> Mining economics, energy use, regulations, and hardware conditions vary by place and time. Use dated, sourced evidence for current claims.
