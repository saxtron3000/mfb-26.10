## Welcome To The Bitcoin Diploma

### About Us

My First Bitcoin is a nonprofit education organization founded by John Dennehy in 2021. We publish free and open-source Bitcoin education for learners and educators around the world.

The project began with small in-person classes in El Salvador in 2021, after the country passed a bill making bitcoin legal tender. In 2022, the first Bitcoin Diploma cohort began in one public school in San Marcos. The pilot developed into an open-source curriculum that independent educators could translate, adapt, and teach through the [Bitcoin Education Network](https://myfirstbitcoin.org/about/network). My First Bitcoin's [public history](https://myfirstbitcoin.org/about) records the project's milestones and organizational accountability.

Our work is independent, community-driven, and Bitcoin-focused. We focus on financial literacy, careful thinking, and practical safety - not trading, speculation, or promoting other tokens. We do not ask you to accept a belief. Instead, we provide concepts, questions, and practical knowledge so you can form your own conclusions.

### Our mission: independent, open-source Bitcoin education

Our mission is to empower people through independent, impartial, and open-source Bitcoin education.

Independent education is not controlled by a government, company, or financial service. Impartial education explains evidence and [trade-offs](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#trade-off) without requiring a learner to reach a predetermined conclusion. Open-source education can be read, shared, reviewed, translated, and improved by anyone under its license.

This approach matters because money affects every person. People should be able to study the systems they use without needing permission. They should also be able to inspect the educational material, question its claims, and help improve it.

### What we stand for: accessibility, critical thinking, community, and self-sovereignty

**Accessibility** means that Bitcoin Diploma content can be taught in person, online, or through print/PDF delivery. Limited technology should not prevent participation in learning or certification. Local examples may change, but the required competencies and competency criteria remain the same.

**Curiosity** means being willing to ask why, admit what you do not know, and investigate an unfamiliar answer before accepting or rejecting it.

**Critical thinking** means asking clear questions, checking evidence, and comparing [trade-offs](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#trade-off). Bitcoin makes strong claims about money and ownership. These claims deserve careful study. During this course, distinguish facts from opinions, and possibilities from guarantees.

**Community** means that education becomes more useful when people discuss ideas and connect them to local life. Your educator and classmates may have experiences that no global book can contain. Listen carefully, ask questions, and explain your reasoning with respect.

**Self-sovereignty** means having meaningful control over your own choices and resources. With Bitcoin, greater control can reduce dependence on institutions. It also creates greater personal responsibility. Control without knowledge can be dangerous, so practical safety is a central part of the Bitcoin Diploma.

### Why the Bitcoin Diploma exists

Most people use money before they learn how money works. They may earn it, save it, borrow it, and spend it without studying who issues it, how its supply changes, or why its [purchasing power](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#purchasing-power) changes.

Bitcoin introduces a different system. It has no central [issuer](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#issuer), it follows public rules, and it allows direct digital ownership. These ideas can be difficult to explain clearly without first examining money and the problems that can arise in modern money systems.

The Bitcoin Diploma therefore follows a simple path:

1. Explain the problem Bitcoin was designed to address.
2. Explain Bitcoin's main response.
3. Explain and practice the responsibility of holding and using bitcoin.
4. Explore how Bitcoin may function as money in your community.

The aim is not to make you an expert in eight hours. The aim is to give you a reliable foundation, help you avoid common beginner mistakes, and prepare you to continue learning.

### How you can use this course as a starting point

Treat the Bitcoin Diploma as the beginning of a longer learning process. By the end, you should know enough to ask better questions and to recognize which topics require more study.

You can use the course to:

- explain common terms about money and Bitcoin
- compare modern money systems and Bitcoin in plain language
- discuss claims using evidence and [trade-offs](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#trade-off)
- identify wallet and [custody](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#custody) choices
- recognize common safety risks
- choose a safe next learning step
- connect with a local learning community
- prepare for more advanced study

> **Callout – Knowledge comes before action**
>
> You do not need to buy or otherwise acquire bitcoin to complete the Bitcoin Diploma. You also do not need to install a [Bitcoin wallet](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#bitcoin-wallet) or use personal funds. Required practical skills may be assessed with a prepared training wallet, simulation, test environment, physical model, or an educator-approved low-stakes activity. Knowledge comes before action.

If you have limited time or would like a first introduction to Bitcoin, begin with [Introduction to Bitcoin](https://myfirstbitcoin.org/learn/programs#intro). It is a shorter course that introduces the basics. After the Bitcoin Diploma, you may continue to the [Educator Training Program](https://etp.myfirstbitcoin.org/) if you want to develop teaching practice. These are separate, optional learning paths: Introduction to Bitcoin is a short primer, the Bitcoin Diploma builds a broader beginner foundation, and the Educator Training Program develops teaching practice. None is required to complete the Bitcoin Diploma. For the Bitcoin Diploma, you need this Student Guide, an educator, and the materials and resources your educator specifies. Explore the [student journey](https://journey.myfirstbitcoin.org/) to see how these opportunities connect and choose your next step.

## How the Bitcoin Diploma works

### What learners will be able to explain and demonstrate by the end

After completing the Bitcoin Diploma, you should be able to:

- explain why people use money, compare two forms, and explain one realistic modern-money [trade-off](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#trade-off) (`C1`)
- explain Bitcoin foundations and rules at a beginner level, including digital copying, shared [verification](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#verification), what users, [nodes](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#node), [miners](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#miner), and developers do, how Bitcoin's supply is limited, and how a Bitcoin property could help or limit its use as money (`C2`)
- compare Bitcoin with another [digital asset](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#digital-asset) using relevant criteria and distinguish learning from speculation or promotion (`C3`)
- explain wallets, [custody](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#custody) choices, public and secret information, wallet types, and a safe response to a scam or error (`C4`)
- compare on-chain and Lightning payments and explain the risks of different access or exchange pathways (`C5`)
- safely complete supported wallet access and receipt with checks, then show recovery readiness using prepared or simulated materials (`C6`); and
- safely complete one educator-selected local Bitcoin-as-money task and explain its purpose, alternative, [trade-offs](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#trade-off), possible failure, and next step (`C7`).

These competencies are a foundation, not proof of expert skill. Completing the Diploma does not mean you are ready to manage large amounts of bitcoin, advise other people, or solve advanced technical problems without support.

### Flexible learning

Bitcoin Diploma content may be taught in person, online, through a blended course, or with print/PDF materials. The book provides the shared knowledge path. Your educator adds discussion, guided activities, demonstrations, local context, and any required assessment.

Each module follows the same rhythm:

1. Learn a key idea.
2. Show what you can explain or apply.
3. Apply the idea to a comparison, question, scenario, or demonstration.
4. Explain the idea in your own words.
5. Review how it connects to the next module.

You will learn more by participating than by reading silently. Some questions do not have one correct answer. What matters is whether you can explain your reasoning, examine evidence, and revise your view when you learn something new.

### Discussion and privacy expectations

Use discussion to test ideas, ask questions, listen carefully, and explain your reasoning. You do not need to agree with every claim or every person in your cohort.

During discussion:

- describe ideas in your own words
- ask for clarification when a term is unclear
- separate personal experience from general evidence
- challenge ideas without attacking people
- state uncertainty when you are unsure
- remember that financial access, security, and opportunity differ; do not assume that everyone shares your circumstances
- protect other people's private financial information
- treat personal stories shared in the cohort as private unless the person gives permission to repeat them
- do not photograph, record, or share another person's screen, work, or identifying information without permission
- do not pressure anyone to buy, sell, or reveal how much money they hold

Your educator will explain any required assessment activities. Theory is mainly developed in **Modules 1 and 2**, where your educator observes you explain, compare, justify, or apply an idea. A statement of confidence is not evidence by itself. Practical competency is mainly developed in **Modules 3 and 4**, where an educator observes the relevant actions under safe, supported conditions.

Your educator uses an assessment grid to record one result for each of the seven competencies above. `C1`–`C5` are assessed through your own explanations and decisions; `C6`–`C7` require direct observation. Both the wallet/receipt task and the recovery-readiness task are required parts of `C6`.

### Required prior knowledge: none

No prior knowledge is required. The course introduces each required term before using it in greater depth.

Basic reading and everyday arithmetic are useful. The most important calculation is that one bitcoin (BTC) can be divided into 100,000,000 smaller units called [satoshis](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#satoshi-sat), usually shortened to [sats](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#satoshi-sat). **BTC** is the common abbreviation used for amounts of bitcoin. A calculator may help, but advanced mathematics is not required.

If you already use Bitcoin, support your educator, share your experience, and explain ideas to peers in plain language. Teaching others is one way to consolidate your own learning. If you enjoy supporting learners, [contribute feedback](https://forms.myfirstbitcoin.org/feedback) or local community support, and explore the [Educator Training Program](https://etp.myfirstbitcoin.org/) to develop teaching practice.

### Required materials and technology expectations

For the Diploma, you need:

- this book in print or PDF form
- a notebook and pen, or another way to record answers
- an educator or study group where available
- time to discuss and reflect

A personal phone and continuous internet connection are not required for learning, practical preparation, assessment, or certification. Do not install a [Bitcoin wallet](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#bitcoin-wallet) or create a [seed phrase](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#seed-phrase-recovery-phrase) only because another learner tells you to. Use practical tools only under clear guidance and only when the purpose and risk are clear.

When it is time to practice with a [Bitcoin wallet](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#bitcoin-wallet), your educator will guide you step by step. You can learn without using your own money. These simple habits will help:

- use a device you control, and ask your educator if you need help choosing one
- ask your educator to help you check the download source before installing a wallet
- practice with a test environment, simulation, or other prepared training material; if your educator plans a low-stakes live activity, understand the purpose and risks before choosing whether to take part
- use sample [recovery words](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#seed-phrase-recovery-phrase) provided for practice, rather than words from someone else's real wallet
- keep real [recovery words](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#seed-phrase-recovery-phrase) (also called a [seed phrase](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#seed-phrase-recovery-phrase)) private: they restore access to a wallet, so do not show them to the class, photograph them, or enter them into a website or message

### Safety, responsibility, and no financial advice

> **Callout – Learn first; this is not a recommendation**
>
> The Bitcoin Diploma is for educational purposes only. It does not provide financial, investment, legal, or tax advice. It does not promise that bitcoin will increase in [price](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#price) or recommend that you buy, sell, borrow against, or hold any amount of bitcoin.
>
> The Diploma is Bitcoin-only. Other tokens, trading products, and broader cryptocurrency promotion are outside its scope. This boundary defines the subject of the course; it is not a guarantee about Bitcoin or a recommendation to acquire it.
>
> Bitcoin's market [price](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#price) can change quickly. [Transactions](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction-tx) can be difficult or impossible to reverse. A lost [seed phrase](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#seed-phrase-recovery-phrase), exposed [private key](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#private-key), dishonest [custodian](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#custodian), software error, or scam can cause permanent loss.

Use three rules throughout the course:

1. **Learn first.** Do not act only because of excitement, fear, or pressure.
2. **Start small.** Test unfamiliar tools and processes with an amount you can afford to lose.
3. **Don't Trust, Verify.** Check addresses, amounts, fees, sources, and people before you approve an action.

Responsibility also includes knowing when to ask for help. Seek help before moving meaningful funds, but never reveal a [seed phrase](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#seed-phrase-recovery-phrase) or [private key](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#private-key) to the person helping you.

### The four-module Diploma

The Diploma consists of a four-stage learning arc: **Problem → Solution → Action → Connect**. All four modules are part of the Diploma. **Modules 1 and 2** develop the shared Problem and Solution through explanation, comparison, and reasoning. Educators can adapt practical examples, tools, resources, and delivery in **Modules 3 and 4** while preserving the required safety and competency standards. **Module 4** is the educator-curated Connect stage, where learners link the shared learning to a local need or opportunity, a realistic alternative, safe practice, and a responsible next step.

**Module 4** is a required part of the Diploma, curated by the educator. Educators are the subject-matter experts on their students' local constraints, solutions, tools, resources, laws, language, infrastructure, and needs. The educator-curated Connect experience may include earning, saving, receiving, spending, [Layer 2](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#layer-2) solutions, local exchange pathways, merchants, or circular economies.

[Advanced Learning](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/programs/advanced-modules/) is outside the Diploma. It may examine deeper economics, [cryptography](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#cryptography), [mining](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#proof-of-work), [privacy](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#privacy), [transaction](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction-tx) structure, Lightning operations, business use, or security. Advanced material is kept separate so the beginner Diploma remains clear and safe.

A topic is not unimportant because it is outside the Diploma. It may simply require more time or prior knowledge.

## Module 1: Understanding Money

### Opening questions about money

**Opening discussion | Suggested time: 10 minutes**

Money shapes everyday choices about food, transport, work, saving, and helping people we care about. Discuss these questions with your cohort.

1. Why do people need money?
2. What makes something money?
3. Who can create or control the money you use?
4. Why does the money you use have [value](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#value)?
5. Does your money buy the same amount today as it did in the past?

As you discuss these questions, notice the difference between a fact and a opinion. “This note is accepted for taxes” is a fact that can be checked. “This is the best form of money” is a conclusion that needs criteria and evidence.

### Why money exists

People have different skills, resources, needs, and plans. Exchange allows them to specialize. A farmer can grow food, a builder can build homes, and a nurse can provide care. Each person can trade the [value](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#value) of their work for the work of others.

Direct exchange is often difficult. The person who has what you need may not want what you offer. Money solves this coordination problem.

> **Definition - Money**
>
> Money is an [asset](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#asset) that people accept because they expect other people to accept it later. It helps strangers coordinate exchange, record [prices](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#price) and [debts](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#debt), and move [value](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#value) across time and distance.

Money helps strangers cooperate across time and distance, and at scale. For example, a worker can earn money today and spend it next year on a bicycle made in another country, paying for the work of many people they have never met. Money also creates a common way to record [prices](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#price), [debts](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#debt), income, costs, and savings.

Money is not the food, home, time, or skill that people [value](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#value). It is a tool for measuring and exchanging claims on those things. Its usefulness depends on trust, rules, social acceptance, and its monetary properties.

**Spot question:** Why did money come into existence? Explain the exchange or coordination problem it helps people solve.

### Functions and properties of money

Money usually performs three main functions.

**[Medium of exchange](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#medium-of-exchange):** People accept it in payment for goods and services. This reduces the need for direct [barter](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#barter-bartering).

**[Unit of account](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#unit-of-account):** [Prices](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#price) are expressed in a shared unit. This allows people to compare the cost of different goods and keep financial records.

**[Store of value](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#store-of-value):** [Value](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#value) can be moved from the present into the future. No money stores [value](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#value) perfectly, but some forms lose [purchasing power](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#purchasing-power) more slowly than others.

An item can perform one function better than another. A national [currency](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#currency) may be widely accepted for daily payments while another [asset](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#asset) may be chosen for long-term saving.

Useful money also tends to have several properties:

| Property | Question to ask |
| --- | --- |
| [Durability](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#durability) | Can it remain usable over time? |
| [Portability](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#portability) | Can people move or transfer it without excessive difficulty? |
| [Divisibility](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#divisibility) | Can it represent both small and large amounts? |
| [Fungibility](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#fungibility) | Are equal units normally interchangeable? |
| [Scarcity](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#scarcity) | Can its supply increase quickly, and who can change it? |
| [Acceptability](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#acceptability) | Will other people receive it in exchange? |
| [Verifiability](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#verifiability) | Can people check that it is genuine? |

No form of money scores perfectly.

[Gold](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#gold) is durable and scarce but difficult to divide and transport for small payments. [Paper cash](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#paper-cash) is portable and accepted locally, but it can be damaged and its supply can expand. The best comparison depends on the purpose, place, and time.

**Think critically:** Name a time when you chose one type of money or [asset](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#asset) over another. What was the purpose? Compare the properties of the options you could have used. Which properties mattered most to you, and why?

### [Barter](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#barter-bartering) and the [double coincidence of wants](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#double-coincidence-of-wants)

> **Definition - [Barter](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#barter-bartering)**
>
> [Barter](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#barter-bartering) is the direct exchange of one good or service for another. It can work well in a small exchange, but becomes difficult when many people and products are involved.

Imagine that Amina has rice and wants shoes. Luis has shoes but wants cooking oil, not rice. Mei has cooking oil and wants repair work. Amina may need several exchanges before she can obtain the shoes. Each exchange takes time and requires agreement about [value](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#value).

For [barter](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#barter-bartering) to work directly, each person must want what the other person offers at the same time and in suitable amounts. Economists call this the **[double coincidence of wants](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#double-coincidence-of-wants)**.

[Barter](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#barter-bartering) also creates other problems:

- some goods cannot be divided without destroying their [value](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#value)
- some goods spoil before they can be exchanged
- there is no common unit for comparing [prices](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#price)
- saving large amounts can be difficult
- delayed payment and accounting become complex

A commonly accepted money separates selling from buying. Amina can sell rice to one person for money and later use that money to buy shoes from someone else.

### [Scarcity](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#scarcity), [value](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#value), and exchange

Resources are limited, while human wants are not.

> **Definition - [Scarcity](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#scarcity)**
>
> [Scarcity](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#scarcity) means that choosing one use of a resource prevents another use. Time, land, energy, good health, tools, and sound money are all scarce.

The [value](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#value) of a good is not contained inside the object. People [value](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#value) goods according to their needs, preferences, information, and circumstances. Clean water may be inexpensive near a safe public source and extremely valuable during a shortage.

<!-- VISUAL: BD-V-011
ALT: The same clean water shown as more available near a safe source and more valuable during a shortage.
ASSET: assets/briefs/bd-v-011-brief.md
-->

Exchange happens when both sides prefer what they receive to what they give up. They do not need to agree that the two items have the same personal [value](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#value). They only need to agree to the terms.

Three ideas help explain everyday choices:

- **[Opportunity cost](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#opportunity-cost)** is the best alternative you give up when you make a choice. Money spent on transport cannot also be spent on food.
- **[Time preference](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#time-preference)** describes how strongly a person prefers [value](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#value) now instead of later. Urgent needs can create a high preference for the present. Saving for a future goal shows a greater willingness to wait.
- **[Trade-off](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#trade-off)** means gaining one benefit while accepting a cost or giving up another benefit.

-->

[Scarcity](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#scarcity) alone does not guarantee [value](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#value). An item may be rare and still have no demand. A useful monetary [asset](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#asset) combines limited supply with properties and demand that make people willing to hold or exchange it.

### Beginner monetary history from [barter](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#barter-bartering) to digital money

Money did not develop in one identical sequence everywhere. Different societies used different goods, records, and institutions. However, a broad pattern helps explain modern money.

> “Money … is by no means the result of an agreement … but is the unintended product of historical development.”
> - Carl Menger, “On the Origin of Money” (1892)

- **Direct exchange and local records:** People used [barter](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#barter-bartering), gifts, obligations, and written or remembered [debts](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#debt). These systems depended strongly on local relationships.

- **[Commodity money](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#commodity-money):** Communities selected goods that were widely valued and easier to trade. Salt, beads, shells, livestock, copper, silver, and [gold](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#gold) have served monetary roles in different places. A commodity has a use or [value](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#value) beyond an [issuer](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#issuer)'s promise.

- **[Standard coins](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#standard-coins):** Authorities measured and stamped metal into standard units. Coins improved recognition and accounting. Authorities could also reduce the precious-metal content of these coins while keeping the same stated [value](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#value). This is called [debasement](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#debasement).

- **[Representative paper](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#representative-paper):** Transporting and protecting metal was difficult. Depositories and banks issued [paper claims](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#paper-claim) that could be redeemed for a stated amount of metal. The paper was easier to move, but users had to trust the [issuer](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#issuer) to keep enough metal and honor redemption. Under different versions of a **[gold standard](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#gold-standard)**, a [currency](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#currency) unit was legally connected to a stated amount of [gold](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#gold). The rules varied by place and period, and governments or banks sometimes suspended or ended [convertibility](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#convertibility).

- **Bank and government money:** Banks issued more claims and credit. Governments created [central banks](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#central-bank) and national [currency](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#currency) systems. Money became increasingly based on institutional records and promises rather than direct possession of a commodity by the population. In many countries, national [currency](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#currency) later became no longer redeemable for a physical [asset](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#asset) such as [gold](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#gold). This separation from commodity backing is a defining feature of today’s **[fiat money](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#fiat-money)** (see below).

- **Digital money:** Much of today's money moves as digital records through banks and payment services. A card or phone does not create a new kind of money by itself. It is usually an interface to a [bank deposit](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#bank-deposit) or another claim denominated in the national [currency](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#currency).

-->

This history shows repeated [trade-offs](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#trade-off). Convenience increased, but users often became more dependent on monetary policy, [issuers](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#issuer), banks, and payment [intermediaries](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#intermediary).

### [Fiat money](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#fiat-money)

> **Definition - [Fiat money](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#fiat-money)**
>
> [Fiat money](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#fiat-money) is [currency](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#currency) issued under government authority, usually through a [central bank](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#central-bank), that is not redeemable for a fixed amount of a commodity such as [gold](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#gold). A country’s laws typically designate its [currency](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#currency) as legal tender and require it for the payment of taxes, although the exact rules for accepting payments and settling [debts](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#debt) vary by jurisdiction.

[Fiat money](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#fiat-money) works because people, businesses, banks, and governments accept it. [Network effects](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#network-effect) reinforce this acceptance: people want the [currency](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#currency) because other people use it and [prices](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#price) are stated in it.

The fiat system has several main participants:

| Participant | Main role |
| --- | --- |
| [Central bank](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#central-bank) | Issues base money, manages monetary policy, supports payment settlement, and may act as lender of last resort. |
| Commercial bank | Holds customer deposits, provides payments, and creates [deposit money](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#bank-deposit) when it issues loans. |
| Government | Collects taxes, spends, borrows, creates laws, and usually defines the national [unit of account](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#unit-of-account). |
| Citizens and businesses | Earn, save, borrow, invest, pay taxes, set [prices](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#price), and exchange goods and services. |

Fiat systems can provide convenient payments, flexible credit, and tools for responding to financial crises. The same flexibility creates **risk**. A small number of institutions can change the supply and cost of money, restrict access, or make decisions that reduce [purchasing power](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#purchasing-power).

[Fiat money](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#fiat-money) requires layers of **trust**. Users trust banks to keep accurate records, payment services to process transfers, [central banks](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#central-bank) to manage the [currency](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#currency), and governments to maintain lawful institutions. These systems can work for long periods, but their rules are not fixed and can be open to neglect or abuse.

### [Fractional reserve banking](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#fractional-reserve-banking)

When you deposit cash in a bank, the bank records a [liability](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#liability) to you: it owes you the account balance. The bank does not normally keep every unit of every deposit as physical cash. It holds liquid reserves and uses its balance sheet to make loans and other investments.

When a commercial bank approves a loan, it usually creates a new deposit in the borrower's account. This expands the amount of bank [deposit money](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#bank-deposit). When the principal is repaid, that [deposit money](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#bank-deposit) is reduced. Banks are limited by [capital](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#capital) rules, [liquidity](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#liquidity) needs, [credit risk](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#credit-risk), regulation, the cost of funding, and demand from [qualified borrowers](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#qualified-borrower).

<!-- VISUAL: BD-V-016
ALT: Simplified bank loan flow showing a loan asset matched by a deposit liability.
ASSET: assets/briefs/bd-v-016-brief.md
-->

Banks generally seek profit by earning interest and fees on loans and investments while paying their operating, funding, and regulatory costs and absorbing losses when borrowers do not repay. These activities and constraints differ by bank and jurisdiction.

> **Definition - [Fractional reserve banking](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#fractional-reserve-banking)**
>
> [Fractional reserve banking](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#fractional-reserve-banking) is the broad idea that banks keep only a fraction of their deposit obligations available as immediately accessible reserves. Exact rules differ between countries and can change over time.

This creates two important risks:

- **[Liquidity risk](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#liquidity-risk):** A bank may own long-term loans that are expected to be repaid but still lack enough cash for many withdrawals today.
- **[Solvency risk](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#solvency-risk):** Failed loans or falling [asset](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#asset) [values](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#value) may leave the bank's total [assets](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#asset) worth less than what it owes.

<!-- VISUAL: BD-V-017
ALT: Side-by-side fictional examples distinguishing liquidity risk from solvency risk.
ASSET: assets/briefs/bd-v-017-brief.md
-->

A **[bank run](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#bank-run)** happens when many depositors try to withdraw at the same time because they fear the bank may fail. [Deposit insurance](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#deposit-insurance) and [central-bank](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#central-bank) lending can reduce panic, but insurance eligibility and coverage limits vary by jurisdiction. These protections depend on institutions and rules, and they can move some losses or risks from an individual bank to the wider system.

Regulation and oversight can reduce risk, but they cannot prevent every case of fraud, poor management, political abuse, or simple error. These failures are possibilities to evaluate, not outcomes of every banking system.

The central lesson is not that every bank is about to fail. It is that a bank balance is the bank's promise to pay you. It is not the same as holding the settlement [asset](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#asset) directly.

### [Inflation](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#inflation) and [purchasing power](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#purchasing-power)

> **Definition - [Price inflation](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#inflation)**
>
> [Price inflation](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#inflation) is a sustained increase in the general [price](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#price) level of goods and services. If your income and savings do not rise as quickly as [prices](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#price), your money buys less.

> **Definition - [Purchasing power](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#purchasing-power)**
>
> [Purchasing power](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#purchasing-power) is the amount of goods and services a unit of money can buy. If a basket of basic goods rises from 100 units of [currency](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#currency) to 110, the same 100 units no longer buy the full basket. Its [purchasing power](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#purchasing-power) in relation to that basket has fallen.

<!-- VISUAL: BD-V-018
ALT: Fixed amount of money buying a smaller basket after prices rise.
ASSET: assets/briefs/bd-v-018-brief.md
-->

[Inflation](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#inflation) can have several causes. These include an expansion of money and credit, supply disruptions, changes in demand, [currency](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#currency) weakness, taxes, regulation, and changes in production costs. The causes and effects are often debated. Avoid explanations that assign every [price](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#price) change to one factor.

[Inflation](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#inflation) does not affect everyone equally. A person who holds mainly cash may lose [purchasing power](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#purchasing-power). A person who owns an [asset](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#asset) that rises in [price](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#price) may be partly protected. Borrowers may repay fixed [debts](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#debt) with money that buys less, while lenders may receive less real [value](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#value) than expected. Wages and [prices](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#price) also adjust at different speeds.

The rate of [inflation](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#inflation) is an average. Your personal experience may differ because your spending is not identical to the measured basket. Food, housing, transport, education, and health costs may change at different rates.

> **Light - Compare [purchasing power](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#purchasing-power) locally**
>
> Choose one ordinary item in your community. Record its [price](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#price) today. Ask what it cost several years ago, then check a reliable record if possible. Did income change at the same rate? What other factors may explain the change?
>
> Then compare your item with an official [inflation](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#inflation) measure. Which goods and services are in that measure's basket? Has the basket or method changed over time, and does the source explain why? A change is not automatically manipulation, but it can affect comparisons and should be documented transparently.

### Why the US dollar is a world reserve [currency](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#currency)

A **reserve [currency](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#currency)** is a [currency](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#currency) that [central banks](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#central-bank) and governments hold and use for international payments. Before the First World War, the British pound played this role most strongly. The war weakened European [currencies](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#currency) and financial centers, while the United States supplied goods and accumulated [gold](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#gold). This increased international use of the dollar.

After the Second World War, the United States had a large economy and much of its financial capacity intact. The 1944 [Bretton Woods](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#bretton-woods) Agreement then organized a postwar system in which many [currencies](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#currency) were linked to the dollar and the dollar was officially linked to [gold](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#gold), placing the dollar at the center of international payments.

In 1971, President Nixon ended the official exchange of dollars for [gold](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#gold) by foreign governments and [central banks](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#central-bank), helping bring the [Bretton Woods](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#bretton-woods) system to an end. The dollar nevertheless remained widely used because of its established role in trade and finance, the size of the U.S. economy, and the depth of U.S. financial markets. It remains the leading reserve [currency](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#currency) today, although [central banks](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#central-bank) also hold other [currencies](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#currency) and its share can change over time.

### Why people look for alternatives

People often seek alternatives when they believe cash will lose [purchasing power](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#purchasing-power). Common choices include precious metals, real estate, and stocks. Each has benefits and risks.

| Alternative | Why people may choose it | Important [trade-offs](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#trade-off) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Precious metals | Long history, physical possession, limited supply | Storage, verification, transport, security, and difficult small payments |
| Real estate | Useful physical [asset](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#asset), possible rent, limited desirable locations | High entry cost, maintenance, taxes, legal dependence, and low [portability](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#portability) |
| Stocks | Ownership in productive businesses, possible income and growth | Business risk, market [volatility](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#volatility), [custody](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#custody) through institutions, and need for financial knowledge |

These [assets](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#asset) are not identical to money. A home may store [value](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#value) but is not practical for buying food. A share may be easy to sell in one country but unavailable to someone without a brokerage account. [Gold](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#gold) may cross long periods of history but be difficult to verify or divide during a small exchange.

People may also choose foreign [currency](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#currency), government [bonds](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#bond), private businesses, land, tools, education, or other [assets](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#asset). Every choice includes [opportunity cost](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#opportunity-cost) and risk. There is no [asset](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#asset) that guarantees [purchasing power](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#purchasing-power) in every period and situation.

**Note:** Tax treatment can also shape these decisions. Rules for income, gains, property, and [transactions](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction-tx) can change the cost or benefit of holding, selling, or using an [asset](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#asset). Tax rules vary by jurisdiction and can change over time; this course does not provide tax advice.

Bitcoin emerged as another proposed alternative: a scarce [digital asset](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#digital-asset) that can be held and transferred without a central [issuer](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#issuer). To evaluate that claim, we must study why Bitcoin was created and how its rules work.

### [Central Bank Digital Currencies](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#central-bank-digital-currency-cbdc) (CBDCs)

> **Definition - [Central bank digital currency](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#central-bank-digital-currency-cbdc) ([CBDC](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#central-bank-digital-currency-cbdc))**
>
> A [central bank digital currency](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#central-bank-digital-currency-cbdc), or [CBDC](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#central-bank-digital-currency-cbdc), is a digital form of [fiat money](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#fiat-money) issued as a [liability](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#liability) of a [central bank](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#central-bank): it is a claim that the central bank owes to its holder. It is different from physical cash and from a normal commercial-bank deposit, although the user experience may look similar.

[CBDC](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#central-bank-digital-currency-cbdc) designs differ. Some are intended mainly for financial institutions. Others are proposed for public use. Depending on the design, a [CBDC](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#central-bank-digital-currency-cbdc) may allow faster settlement, wider access, or new payment features. It may also give institutions greater ability to monitor [transactions](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction-tx), apply rules, limit use, or connect identity directly to payments.

A [CBDC](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#central-bank-digital-currency-cbdc) does not create a fixed monetary supply or remove central control. It changes the technology and distribution of [fiat money](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#fiat-money). The [central bank](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#central-bank) remains the [issuer](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#issuer) and can still apply monetary policy.

<!-- VISUAL: BD-V-019
ALT: Comparison matrix distinguishing cash, bank deposits, a generic retail CBDC, and Bitcoin by control, access, visibility, supply, and verification.
ASSET: assets/briefs/bd-v-019-brief.md
-->

Digital does not automatically mean decentralized, scarce, private, or open to independent verification. A [CBDC](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#central-bank-digital-currency-cbdc) may use digital technology, but it is still [fiat money](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#fiat-money) if its [issuer](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#issuer), supply rules, access rules, and payment permissions remain centrally managed.

When discussing a [CBDC](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#central-bank-digital-currency-cbdc), ask:

- Who issues the money?
- Who can hold or use it?
- What information is collected?
- Can a payment be blocked, limited, or reversed?
- Can the rules change?
- Can ordinary users independently verify the rules?
- What happens without electricity or internet access?
- What legal protections exist?

Do not assume that every proposed [CBDC](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#central-bank-digital-currency-cbdc) has the same design. Use current, local, and reliable information.

### Review: how can money break?

Pause and consider the money you use: what makes it useful in daily life, and which of its rules depend on institutions you must trust? [Barter](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#barter-bartering) is difficult because matching needs is inefficient, while [fiat money](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#fiat-money) makes exchange easier but can involve changing supply, restricted access, and declining [purchasing power](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#purchasing-power). In short, people look for monetary alternatives when they want different [trade-offs](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#trade-off) around supply, control, access, and trust.

#### Key takeaways

- Money helps people exchange, compare [prices](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#price), record [value](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#value), and move [value](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#value) across time.
- The main functions of money are a [medium of exchange](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#medium-of-exchange), a [unit of account](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#unit-of-account), and a [store of value](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#store-of-value).
- Monetary properties such as [durability](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#durability), [portability](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#portability), [divisibility](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#divisibility), [scarcity](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#scarcity), [acceptability](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#acceptability), and [verifiability](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#verifiability) help us compare different forms of money.
- [Barter](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#barter-bartering) is difficult at scale because both people must want what the other offers at the same time and in suitable amounts.
- Money has developed in different ways across societies, often improving convenience while creating new [trade-offs](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#trade-off) in trust, control, and access.
- [Fiat money](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#fiat-money) can support convenient payments and flexible credit, but its supply and rules depend on institutions.
- [Inflation](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#inflation), [fractional-reserve banking](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#fractional-reserve-banking), and [CBDC](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#central-bank-digital-currency-cbdc) design can affect [purchasing power](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#purchasing-power), access, [privacy](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#privacy), and the need to trust institutions.
- Some people look to alternatives such as precious metals when they want different [trade-offs](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#trade-off) around [purchasing power](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#purchasing-power), supply, or control; every alternative has limits and risks.

The next module examines Bitcoin's attempt to create a different set of monetary rules.

## Module 2: How Bitcoin Works

### Why Bitcoin was created

Digital payments existed before Bitcoin. People could send money through banks, card networks, and online payment companies. These systems made commerce easier, but each depended on a trusted organization to keep the [ledger](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#ledger), approve accounts, settle transfers, and resolve disputes.

This dependence creates power and risk. An [intermediary](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#intermediary) can delay a payment, charge a fee, collect  and misuse personal data, restrict an account, reverse a transfer, or fail. The [issuer](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#issuer) of the [currency](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#currency) can also change its supply.

For many years, computer scientists tried to create digital money that did not need a central [issuer](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#issuer) or payment company. The challenge was difficult. Digital information is easy to copy. If a digital coin could be copied and spent twice, it could not function as money. If one company prevented copying, the company became the point of control and failure.

Bitcoin was created to solve this problem with a [peer-to-peer network](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#peer-to-peer-network). Its design allows participants to agree on one [transaction](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction-tx) history without a central [ledger](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#ledger) keeper. It combines a public [ledger](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#ledger) ([blockchain](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#blockchain)), [digital signatures](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#digital-signature), a network of [validating nodes](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#full-node), [proof of work](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#proof-of-work), and economic incentives.

Bitcoin does not remove trust from every part of life. It reduces the need to trust a central authority for the issuance and settlement of its native money.

In this book, **Bitcoin** with a capital B usually means the network, protocol, or wider system. And **bitcoin** with a lowercase b means the monetary unit or [asset](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#asset). **BTC** is the common abbreviation for an amount of bitcoin, especially when writing [prices](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#price) or specific quantities. A wallet or exchange is a service that may connect to Bitcoin; it is not the [Bitcoin network](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#bitcoin-network) itself.

### The search for digital and decentralized money

The growth of personal computers and the internet created new opportunities for communication. It also created new forms of surveillance and control. In the late twentieth century, a group of programmers, mathematicians, and activists known as **[Cypherpunks](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#cypherpunk)** explored how [cryptography](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#cryptography) could protect [privacy](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#privacy) and individual freedom in digital systems.

> **Definition - [Cryptography](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#cryptography)**
>
> [Cryptography](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#cryptography) uses mathematical methods to protect information and prove that an action is authentic. [Encryption](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#encryption) can keep a message private. [Digital signatures](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#digital-signature) can prove that a message or instruction came from the holder of a secret key without revealing that key.

The [Cypherpunks](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#cypherpunk) understood that private communication was not enough. People also needed a way to exchange [value](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#value) online. If every digital payment required a bank or company, that [intermediary](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#intermediary) could observe, permit, or prevent the exchange.

Their goal was not simply digital money. [Bank deposits](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#bank-deposit) were already digital. The harder goal was **decentralized digital money**: a system with no single [issuer](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#issuer), server, or administrator that participants had to trust.

Bitcoin was built upon decades of work by many people. Its design joined earlier ideas in a new way and provided incentives for an open network to keep operating.

### Early digital-money attempts and the [double-spend problem](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#double-spending)

Bitcoin was not the first attempt at digital money. It was built on four decades of experimentation, drawing on ideas from many earlier projects that helped shape its successful design.

- [**DigiCash**](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#digicash) used [cryptography](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#cryptography) to support private digital payments. It depended on a central company and banking relationships. When the company failed, the system could not continue as an independent money network.

- [**Hashcash**](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#hashcash) used [proof of work](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#proof-of-work) to make unwanted email costly. A sender's computer had to perform work before sending a message. The idea showed how computational cost could discourage abuse.

- [**Bit Gold**](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#bit-gold) described a proposed system in which computational work could create scarce digital records. It was not launched as a complete decentralized money system, but it developed important ideas about digital [scarcity](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#scarcity) and proof.

- [**B-money**](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#b-money) and [**reusable proof of work (RPoW)**](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#reusable-proof-of-work) explored ways for participants to create or transfer scarce digital [value](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#value) without relying only on one central database. They were incomplete, but they helped clarify what a working system would need.

- [**BitTorrent**](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#bittorrent) was not money. It showed that a [peer-to-peer network](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#peer-to-peer-network) could let many independent computers share information without one central server controlling the whole system.

Together, these experiments showed useful pieces of the puzzle. They also clarified the remaining problem.

> **Definition - [Double spending](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#double-spending)**
>
> [Double spending](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#double-spending) is an attempt to send the same digital unit to two recipients. Digital data can be copied perfectly, so a digital money system needs a way to establish which [transaction](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction-tx) is valid.

[Centralized systems](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#centralized-system) prevent this by letting one trusted database decide which payment came first. On 31 October 2008, [Satoshi Nakamoto](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#satoshi-nakamoto)'s whitepaper, [*Bitcoin: A Peer-to-Peer Electronic Cash System*](https://bitcoin.org/bitcoin.pdf), explained a different approach: a public system that could establish a shared payment history without a bank, company, or other central authority.

The whitepaper brought together the features needed for that approach:

1. **A [peer-to-peer network](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#peer-to-peer-network):** [transactions](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction-tx) are announced directly to a network of participants.
2. **[Digital signatures](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#digital-signature):** a [private key](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#private-key) is secret information that creates a signature to authorize a spend; only its holder can do this.
3. **Blocks and [mining](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#proof-of-work):** [miners](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#miner) collect [transactions](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction-tx) into blocks and use [proof of work](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#proof-of-work) to compete to add the next block.
4. **Independent [verification](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#verification):** [nodes](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#node) check [transactions](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction-tx) and blocks against Bitcoin's rules.
5. **A shared history with accumulated [proof of work](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#proof-of-work):** participants recognize the [valid chain](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#valid-chain) of [transactions](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction-tx) with the most [accumulated work](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#accumulated-work) as the shared record.

This module explains these concepts in more detail. Together, they gave a practical answer to the [double-spend problem](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#double-spending): the network can reject a second attempt to spend the same bitcoin without relying on a central authority. Bitcoin offered the missing combination that decades of digital-money experiments had been seeking.

### Digital [scarcity](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#scarcity) and credible [scarcity](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#scarcity)

Digital information is normally easy to copy.

> **Definition - Digital [scarcity](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#scarcity)**
>
> Digital [scarcity](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#scarcity) means that a system can distinguish a valid unit from an invalid copy and prevent the same unit from being spent twice.

Bitcoin creates digital [scarcity](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#scarcity) by combining signatures, a public [transaction](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction-tx) history, [proof of work](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#proof-of-work), and independent rule [verification](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#verification). A bitcoin can be transferred, but the network rejects an attempt to spend the same bitcoin again.

[Scarcity](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#scarcity) is more credible when the rules are public, users can verify them independently, and no single [issuer](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#issuer) can quietly change the supply for everyone. This is why Bitcoin's supply limit must be studied together with [decentralization](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#decentralization) and [node](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#node) [verification](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#verification).

### The [Blockchain](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#blockchain) - Bitcoin’s Public [Ledger](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#ledger)

> **Definition - [Blockchain](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#blockchain)**
>
> Bitcoin's [blockchain](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#blockchain) is a public [transaction](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction-tx) record replicated across many independent computers. A block contains a set of [transactions](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction-tx), and each valid block links to the previous one to form a shared history.
>
> [Nodes](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#node) independently check the same rules and converge on the valid history with the most accumulated [proof of work](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#proof-of-work). Changing an earlier record changes its block and breaks the links to later blocks, so a competing history must redo that work and catch up before nodes accept it. Copies do not change simultaneously; nodes receive and validate data independently. Bitcoin targets one new block about every ten minutes on average. The history is difficult and costly to rewrite, not literally impossible to change: temporary competing histories and reorganizations can occur.

### [Decentralization](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#decentralization)

A [centralized system](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#centralized-system) has a controlling point. A bank controls its customer [ledger](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#ledger). A payment company controls its network. A government controls the issuance rules of its [currency](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#currency).

> **Definition - [Decentralization](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#decentralization)**
>
> A [decentralized system](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#decentralized-system) distributes roles and decision enforcement among many independent participants. Bitcoin has no central office that can create accounts, change balances, or approve membership.

Bitcoin's [decentralization](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#decentralization) comes from several features:

- anyone can download and inspect the [open-source software](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#open-source-software)
- users can transact without opening an account with the [Bitcoin network](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#bitcoin-network)
- independent [nodes](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#node) check [transactions](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction-tx) and blocks
- [miners](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#miner) compete to propose blocks
- no [miner](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#miner) can make an invalid block acceptable to honest [nodes](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#node)
- changes to the rules require voluntary adoption by participants
- the [ledger](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#ledger) is copied across many computers and locations

[Decentralization](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#decentralization) is not the same as equal influence. [Mining](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#proof-of-work) equipment, technical knowledge, money, internet access, and software development are not distributed equally. Large service providers can become points of dependence for their own users. Bitcoin's claim is narrower: no single participant has the authority to unilaterally change the [ledger](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#ledger) or monetary rules for everyone.

[Decentralization](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#decentralization) also creates costs. Coordination can be slower. Users must verify information and take more responsibility. There is no central support desk for the [Bitcoin network](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#bitcoin-network). These [trade-offs](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#trade-off) are part of the design, not accidental defects that can be removed without changing the system.

### [Digital signatures](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#digital-signature)

[Digital signatures](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#digital-signature) are one of the key parts of Bitcoin's design described in the whitepaper. In Bitcoin, a wallet uses a [private key](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#private-key) to create a [digital signature](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#digital-signature) that authorizes a [transaction](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction-tx). A wallet is usually an application that manages the keys and creates these signatures for the user. Only the holder of the required [private key](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#private-key) can authorize the spend; the network can verify the signature without learning the [private key](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#private-key) itself.

This allows a person to control bitcoin without asking a bank or other central operator for permission. It also means that control of the [private key](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#private-key) is critical: anyone who obtains it can usually authorize a spend, while a lost key may make the bitcoin irretrievable.

### Peer-to-peer transfer

**Peer-to-peer** means that participants interact through a network without requiring a central coordinator. A Bitcoin user can create and sign a [transaction](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction-tx), then [broadcast](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#broadcast) it to the network. The recipient does not need an account at the sender's bank because Bitcoin itself provides the common settlement system.

Peer-to-peer does not always mean that only two computers communicate directly. A [transaction](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction-tx) may pass through wallets, internet providers, and many network [nodes](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#node). The important point is that no central Bitcoin operator must approve the sender or recipient.

Users can still choose [intermediaries](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#intermediary). An exchange may hold bitcoin for customers. A payment service may simplify an invoice. A hosted wallet may manage keys. These services can be convenient, but the user then depends on the service's rules and security.

Bitcoin makes direct ownership and transfer possible; it does not force every user to avoid every service. The [custody](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#custody) choice determines who has practical control.

Peer-to-peer transfer can be useful when people are in different places or use different banking systems. It also creates responsibility. A valid [transaction](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction-tx) sent to the wrong address cannot usually be reversed.

### Users, [nodes](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#node), and [miners](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#miner)

Bitcoin works because different participants perform different roles.

- **Users** receive, hold, and send bitcoin. A user may control their own keys held in a wallet or use a [custodian](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#custodian). Users give the network economic meaning by choosing whether to accept bitcoin and which rules or services they trust.

- **[Nodes](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#node)** run Bitcoin software and independently verify the rules. A [full node](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#full-node) checks that [transactions](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction-tx) have valid signatures, that bitcoin have not already been spent, that blocks satisfy [proof of work](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#proof-of-work), and that new bitcoin follow the [issuance schedule](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#issuance-schedule). [Nodes](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#node) share valid data with peers and reject invalid data.

- **[Miners](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#miner)** gather pending [transactions](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction-tx), build [candidate blocks](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#candidate-block), and compete through [proof of work](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#proof-of-work). A successful [miner](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#miner) [broadcasts](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#broadcast) a block and may receive the allowed [block subsidy](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#block-subsidy) and [transaction fees](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction-fee-miner-fee). [Miners](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#miner) help order [transactions](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction-tx) and make the [ledger](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#ledger) costly to rewrite.

- **Developers** review, write, and propose software changes. They do not own Bitcoin's rules. A software change matters only if participants choose to run software that follows those rules.

These roles limit one another:

- a user cannot spend bitcoin without the required valid signature
- a [miner](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#miner) can propose a block, but [nodes](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#node) decide whether it follows the rules
- a [node](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#node) verifies using its own rules, but [consensus](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#consensus) requires compatible rules across the network
- developers can propose software, but participants choose whether to run it

Some people perform several roles. A [miner](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#miner) can run a [node](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#node) and also be a user. A beginner does not need to perform every role to explain how the roles balance one another.

### [Proof of work](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#proof-of-work)

> **Definition - [Proof of work](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#proof-of-work)**
>
> [Proof of work](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#proof-of-work) is the process [miners](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#miner) use to compete for the right to propose the next block.

A [hash function](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#hash-function) turns data into a fixed-length result. A small change to the input produces an unpredictable new result. A [miner](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#miner) changes a small piece of data in a proposed block and hashes it repeatedly. A result is valid only when its number is below the network's [target](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#target); a lower target means fewer valid results and more attempts. There is no shortcut, so [miners](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#miner) may need to make many attempts.

When a [miner](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#miner) finds a [valid result](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#valid-result), it [broadcasts](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#broadcast) the block. [Nodes](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#node) can check the proof quickly, even though finding it required significant computation and energy. If the block includes a [transaction](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction-tx), that [transaction](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction-tx) receives its [first confirmation](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#first-confirmation)**. Each valid block added after it adds another [confirmation](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#confirmation), making the [transaction](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction-tx)'s place in the [blockchain](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#blockchain) more costly to rewrite. This difference is important: work is costly to produce and easy to verify.

-->

[Proof of work](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#proof-of-work) serves several purposes:

- it gives independent [miners](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#miner) a way to compete without a central scheduler
- it makes the ordering of [transactions](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction-tx) costly to rewrite
- it links network security to real-world equipment and energy
- it supports a public method for choosing between competing valid histories: the chain with the most [accumulated work](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#accumulated-work)

[Mining](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#proof-of-work) does not solve useful equations for an outside purpose. Its purpose is to secure the ordering of Bitcoin [transactions](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction-tx). The network periodically adjusts the target: when blocks arrive too quickly it lowers the target, and when they arrive too slowly it raises it, aiming for about one block every ten minutes on average as computing power changes.

[Proof of work](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#proof-of-work) involves a real energy cost. Supporters argue that this cost is what anchors Bitcoin's security and can create demand for flexible or otherwise unused energy. Critics question the amount and source of energy used. A responsible evaluation asks what energy is used, where it comes from, what security it provides, and what alternatives are being compared.

### Finite supply

Bitcoin's rules define a predictable [issuance schedule](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#issuance-schedule) and a [maximum supply](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#maximum-supply-hard-cap) of 21 million bitcoin (21,000,000 BTC). New bitcoin enter circulation through the [block subsidy](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#block-subsidy) paid to [miners](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#miner) who produce valid blocks.

> **Definition - [Halving](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#halving)**
>
> Every 210,000 blocks, the subsidy is cut in half. This event is called a [halving](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#halving). Because blocks are produced approximately every ten minutes on average, a [halving](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#halving) occurs roughly every four years. The exact calendar date is not fixed.

| [Halving](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#halving) year | [Block height](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#block-height) | Subsidy after [halving](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#halving) |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 2012 | 210,000 | 25 BTC |
| 2016 | 420,000 | 12.5 BTC |
| 2020 | 630,000 | 6.25 BTC |
| 2024 | 840,000 | 3.125 BTC |
| Expected 2028 | 1,050,000 | 1.5625 BTC |

The subsidy began at 50 BTC per block. It falls by half in repeated steps until it becomes too small to continue. [Miners](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#miner) also receive [transaction fees](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction-fee-miner-fee). Over time, fees are expected to become a more important part of [miner](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#miner) revenue.

<!-- VISUAL: BD-V-028
ALT: Bitcoin issuance chart showing the 21 million supply, block subsidy, and halvings.
ASSET: assets/briefs/bd-v-028-brief.md
-->

The 21 million limit is enforced by [nodes](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#node). If a [miner](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#miner) creates more bitcoin than the rules allow, [validating nodes](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#full-node) reject the block. A software developer can write different code, but cannot force other people to run it.

> **Dark - Fixed supply is a network rule, not a [price](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#price) promise**
>
> Bitcoin's supply rule is public, independently verifiable, and difficult for one party to change because [validating nodes](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#full-node) enforce it. It is not a physical law: it remains effective while participants continue to enforce it. A limited supply does not guarantee a rising market [price](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#price); [price](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#price) also depends on demand.
>
> Many [digital assets](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#digital-asset) can claim a limited supply. Ask whether the limit is credible in practice, not only whether it is written in a document or displayed on a website.

**Spot question:** What are the advantages of Bitcoin having a limited supply? What would be the effect of increasing the supply of Bitcoin on [purchasing power](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#purchasing-power)? Who would reject an increase in supply?

> Light
> Finite supply also does not mean every bitcoin is available for sale; some may be held long term or become inaccessible because keys were lost.

A [satoshi](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#satoshi-sat), often shortened to **[sat](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#satoshi-sat)**, is the smallest unit recorded on Bitcoin's base layer.

| 0.01 BTC | 1,000,000 [sats](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#satoshi-sat) |
| 0.001 BTC | 100,000 [sats](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#satoshi-sat) |

**You do not need to buy or use a whole bitcoin.** Bitcoin software can display an amount in bitcoin or [sats](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#satoshi-sat), just as a currency can use a main unit and smaller units. Many people use [sats](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#satoshi-sat) for everyday amounts because whole numbers are easier to read. Both on-chain and Lightning payment requests can be shown in sats.

**Unit [bias](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#bias)** is the mistaken feeling that a lower [price](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#price) per whole unit makes an [asset](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#asset) cheaper or more accessible. The number of units is a design choice. Owning 100,000 [sats](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#satoshi-sat) and owning 0.001 BTC describe the same amount.

When comparing [prices](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#price), focus on the share of the total supply, the amount of [value](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#value) involved, and the risks. Do not assume that an [asset](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#asset) with many cheap-looking units offers better [value](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#value) than an [asset](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#asset) divided into smaller units.

**Show your understanding:** How many [sats](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#satoshi-sat) are in 0.01 BTC? Why does the [price](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#price) of one whole bitcoin not prevent a person from using a smaller amount?

### Bitcoin as sound digital money

**[Sound money](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#sound-money)** is money whose supply is difficult to expand and whose properties support reliable use across time. The term expresses a judgment based on criteria, so it should be examined rather than accepted as a label.

Bitcoin combines properties that did not previously exist together in one [asset](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#asset):

| Property | Plain-language explanation |
| --- | --- |
| [Durability](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#durability) | The [ledger](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#ledger) is copied across many [nodes](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#node). Access depends on preserving valid keys. |
| [Portability](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#portability) | A signed [transaction](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction-tx) can transfer [value](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#value) through a communications network. |
| [Divisibility](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#divisibility) | One bitcoin (1 BTC) divides into 100,000,000 [sats](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#satoshi-sat). |
| [Fungibility](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#fungibility) | Equal amounts are spendable under the same protocol rules, although public [transaction](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction-tx) history can affect how some services treat particular funds. |
| [Scarcity](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#scarcity) | Issuance follows public rules with a 21 million maximum. |
| [Verifiability](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#verifiability) | A [node](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#node) can verify supply, [transaction](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction-tx) rules, and ownership proofs. |
| [Acceptability](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#acceptability) | Acceptance is growing in some places but remains much lower than national [currencies](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#currency) in many communities. |

ASSET: assets/briefs/bd-v-033-brief.md
-->

Bitcoin can perform the three functions of money, but not equally in every place:

- As a **[store of value](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#store-of-value)**, its supply is predictable, but its market [price](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#price) is volatile.
- As a **[medium of exchange](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#medium-of-exchange)**, it can move without a bank, but acceptance, fees, and payment tools vary.
- As a **[unit of account](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#unit-of-account)**, amounts can be stated in bitcoin or [sats](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#satoshi-sat), but most goods are still priced in fiat [currency](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#currency).

Bitcoin therefore presents both benefits and [trade-offs](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#trade-off).

**Potential benefits** include direct ownership, predictable supply, global transfer, open participation, transparent rules, and resistance to unilateral censorship.

**[Trade-offs](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#trade-off)** include [price](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#price) [volatility](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#volatility), personal security responsibility, the fact that a valid payment is usually difficult or impossible to reverse, changing [transaction fees](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction-fee-miner-fee), dependence on electricity and communications for digital transfer, public [transaction](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction-tx) records, and limited acceptance.

Whether you call Bitcoin [sound money](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#sound-money) should follow from your criteria. Compare it with [fiat money](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#fiat-money), precious metals, and other [assets](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#asset) using the same standards.

**Local-value check:** Does bitcoin have [value](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#value) in your community? Separate three questions in your answer: What market [price](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#price) can be observed? Where, if anywhere, is it accepted? What personal preference or [bias](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#bias) affects your judgment? [Price](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#price), local usefulness, and personal approval are related but not identical.

### Bitcoin, stablecoins, and other crypto-assets

[Digital assets](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#digital-asset) can look similar on a screen while relying on very different systems. Bitcoin has public supply rules and no [issuer](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#issuer) that can create new bitcoin outside those rules. A stablecoin normally depends on an [issuer](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#issuer), reserves, legal claims, or a mechanism intended to track another [currency](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#currency). Other crypto-assets may have different people or organizations controlling issuance, changing rules, or operating important parts of the system.

When comparing any [digital asset](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#digital-asset), ask: Who controls issuance or changes? What can I verify independently? Who holds the keys? Which company, [issuer](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#issuer), or other counterparty must I trust? This Bitcoin-only Diploma uses the comparison only to clarify Bitcoin. It does not recommend acquiring, trading, or promoting any [asset](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#asset).

The examples below identify commonly used names and tickers only. They are not endorsements, recommendations, or a claim that any example is safe, reliable, or suitable for you.

> Dark
> Scams, misleading claims, and unsafe services can affect any digital asset or payment pathway. Check the specific asset, service, and custody arrangement rather than assuming that a category is safe or unsafe by itself.

**bitcoin (BTC)** is a [digital asset](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#digital-asset) on the [Bitcoin network](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#bitcoin-network). Its [maximum supply](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#maximum-supply-hard-cap) and [issuance schedule](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#issuance-schedule) are public rules. People can hold bitcoin directly through keys and can independently verify Bitcoin rules by running suitable software. It can be sent globally without needing a central Bitcoin [issuer](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#issuer) to approve every [transaction](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction-tx). Its market [price](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#price) can change sharply, and losing keys or recovery information can mean permanent loss; a mistaken payment is usually hard or impossible to reverse. Fees, [confirmation](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#confirmation) time, electricity, communications, public [transaction](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction-tx) records, and local acceptance can also affect use. A wallet provider or exchange can add its own counterparty and [custody](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#custody) risks.

Some projects use Bitcoin's code as a starting point but operate under different rules or networks. These are often called forks. A shared code history does not make a fork part of Bitcoin or give it Bitcoin's rules, participants, or security.

**Stablecoins**, such as USD Coin (USDC) and Tether (USDT), are digital tokens designed to track a reference [value](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#value), often one unit of a national [currency](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#currency) such as the U.S. dollar. Some may be useful for moving a token [value](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#value) between compatible services or networks. Their design, reserves, redemption rights, and controls vary substantially. A stablecoin can lose its intended [value](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#value) (“depeg”), and users may depend on an [issuer](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#issuer), reserve [custodian](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#custodian), bank, legal redemption process, [blockchain](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#blockchain) operator, or bridge. [Issuers](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#issuer) or platforms may freeze tokens or restrict access where their rules or laws allow. Reserve quality, transparency, insolvency, regulation, smart-contract errors, scams, and network fees are additional risks.

**Other crypto-assets**, such as Ether (ETH) and Solana (SOL), are a broad category of [assets](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#asset) used by other networks or applications. Their rules may support functions such as paying network fees, staking, governance, or application use. Issuance, control, technical design, and what can be independently verified differ from one [asset](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#asset) to another. [Prices](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#price) and liquidity can change rapidly, and developers, foundations, validators, administrators, or large holders may have significant influence, depending on the system. Rule changes, token issuance, smart-contract bugs, hacks, bridge failures, scams, platform failure, and unclear legal treatment can create loss or access risks. Holding a token does not automatically give a user ownership of a company, guaranteed income, or a right to redeem it for another [asset](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#asset).

| [Asset](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#asset) type | Main design | Key difference |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bitcoin | Public supply rules and no central [issuer](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#issuer). | Its 21 million maximum is enforced by independently verified rules, not by reserves or redemption promises. |
| Stablecoins | A token designed to track a reference [value](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#value). | The intended value depends on its particular rules, reserves, redemption process, and counterparties; these differ by stablecoin. |
| Other crypto-assets | [Digital assets](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#digital-asset) used on networks or applications. | Rules, issuance, and control vary by [asset](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#asset). |

Before using any [digital asset](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#digital-asset), distinguish the [asset](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#asset) itself from the service holding it for you. A familiar ticker, a stable-looking [price](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#price), or a well-designed app does not remove the need to identify and compare [custody](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#custody), counterparty, technical, legal, and loss risks.

### Learning and responsible use are not trading or promotion

Learning how Bitcoin works is different from trying to profit from short-term [price](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#price) changes, persuading others to buy a token, or treating speculation as education. Trading and speculation can involve rapid decisions, loss, platform risk, and pressure to act without clear reasoning. Token promotion can create conflicts of interest and reward persuasion rather than clear explanation.

The purpose of the Bitcoin Diploma is to help you explain Bitcoin, ask better questions, and make safer decisions. It does not provide investment advice or tell you what to buy, sell, or hold.

This origin matters: the creator did not remain as a permanent leader who could issue more coins or approve [transactions](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction-tx).

The [Genesis Block](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#genesis-block) and the 2008 financial crisis give Bitcoin important historical context. However, evaluate Bitcoin by its design and operation, not only by a story about its creator.

In October 2008, [Satoshi Nakamoto](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#satoshi-nakamoto) announced the [Bitcoin whitepaper](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#bitcoin-whitepaper). Its central question was simple: **how can people make online payments without letting the same digital unit be spent twice or putting one intermediary in control?**

### Participant empowerment, risk, and responsibility

Bitcoin's public rules, [peer-to-peer network](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#peer-to-peer-network), and [digital signatures](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#digital-signature) give participants meaningful roles. Users can choose to **receive**, **hold**, and **send** bitcoin; [nodes](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#node) can independently verify the rules; [miners](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#miner) can propose blocks; and developers can propose software changes. No single role controls the network for everyone.

These properties can empower participants, but they also place more responsibility on them. Without a central authority to approve [transactions](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction-tx), recover access, or reverse many errors, each person must be able to identify the role and risks they accept.

For a person who chooses [self-custody](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#self-custody), control of the keys brings direct control of bitcoin. No company can freeze a correctly controlled on-chain balance. No company can restore a lost [seed phrase](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#seed-phrase-recovery-phrase) either.

[Self-custody](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#self-custody) is therefore not just a technical feature. It requires protecting backups, verifying addresses, checking wallet behavior, and creating a recovery plan. The next module explains how to hold and use bitcoin carefully, including how to manage these risks and responsibilities.

> Light
> Personal responsibility does not mean acting alone or refusing help. A cautious learner can use education, peer review, documented procedures, and small tests without giving anyone access to secret information.

### Review: how does Bitcoin respond to fiat problems?

Pause and consider how Bitcoin changes the trust model: how do public rules, finite issuance, peer-to-peer transfer, and independent [verification](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#verification) respond to the concerns raised in the previous module? Ask yourself how [nodes](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#node) and [miners](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#miner) have different roles, why [double spending](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#double-spending) must be prevented, and which new responsibilities arise when a person controls their own keys. Bitcoin replaces some institutional trust with [verification](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#verification) and personal responsibility; it does not remove risk or [trade-offs](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#trade-off).

| Fiat-system issue | Bitcoin's response | New responsibility or [trade-off](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#trade-off) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Supply can change through policy | Predictable issuance and a 21 million maximum | Users must verify and choose to enforce the rules |
| Payments depend on [intermediaries](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#intermediary) | Peer-to-peer [transaction](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction-tx) network | A valid payment is usually difficult or impossible to reverse |
| A central [ledger](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#ledger) keeper controls records | Independent [nodes](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#node) share and verify a public [ledger](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#ledger) | Public records can reveal links between transactions, even when they do not identify a person by themselves |
| Access depends on an institution | Anyone can use the protocol and control keys | The user must secure keys and backups. |

#### Key takeaways

- Bitcoin was created to make digital transfer possible without a central [ledger](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#ledger) keeper while preventing [double spending](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#double-spending).
- Its public [ledger](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#ledger), [digital signatures](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#digital-signature), [peer-to-peer network](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#peer-to-peer-network), [proof of work](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#proof-of-work), and independent [verification](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#verification) work together to maintain a shared [transaction](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction-tx) history.
- Bitcoin is decentralized in the sense that no single participant can unilaterally change balances or the rules for everyone.
- Users choose whether to hold or accept bitcoin; [nodes](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#node) verify the rules; [miners](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#miner) propose blocks; and developers can propose, but not impose, software changes.
- Bitcoin has a public supply schedule with a maximum of 21 million BTC, and [nodes](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#node) reject blocks that break this rule.
- One bitcoin (1 BTC) divides into 100,000,000 [sats](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#satoshi-sat), so people can use smaller units without needing a whole bitcoin.
- Bitcoin can be evaluated as [sound money](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#sound-money) using monetary properties, while still recognizing the trade-offs: [price volatility](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#volatility), limited acceptance, payments that are usually difficult or impossible to reverse, and the need for increased personal responsibility.

## Module 3: Using Bitcoin Safely

This module is about safe first actions, not becoming an expert or taking financial risk. You will practice with prepared simulations, test environments, training wallets, or, where your educator judges it appropriate, a low-stakes live activity. 

> Dark
> You do not need to buy bitcoin or use personal funds to complete the Bitcoin Diploma.

What information can you share safely? What information could give another person control? How would you notice that a payment request has changed? These questions matter before any wallet, payment, or backup becomes important.

Use these three rules throughout the module:

1. **Share receiving information, never secrets.** An address or payment request can receive bitcoin. A [private key](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#private-key) or [recovery phrase](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#seed-phrase-recovery-phrase) can control it.
2. **Pause before approving a send.** Check the person, payment method, destination, amount, and final screen.
3. **Keep mistakes small.** Stop and ask for help when a request is unclear, rushed, or asks for secret information.

By the end, you should be able to explain who controls the keys, complete a supported receive or send practice, respond safely to a scam or mismatch, and show recovery readiness without exposing any real secret. The goal is not speed. It is knowing when to pause.

The module follows three practical questions:

1. **What can I share, and what must remain secret?**
2. **What must I check before I receive or send?**
3. **When should I stop and ask for help?**

Use the explanations below to prepare for the three guided practices: a receive-screen check, a send-and-verify check, and a recovery-readiness or scam-response scenario.

### Wallets

> **Definition - [Bitcoin wallet](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#bitcoin-wallet)**
>
> A [Bitcoin wallet](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#bitcoin-wallet) is a tool that manages the information needed to receive and authorize Bitcoin [transactions](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction-tx). It may be an application, a dedicated device, or a service. Bitcoin is recorded on the shared [ledger](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#ledger); it is not stored on a phone or other device.

What would happen if your phone stopped working? The Bitcoin record would still exist, but access would depend on the keys and recovery method the wallet uses. A wallet helps create receiving information, show balances and [transaction](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction-tx) history, and authorize spending. It does not remove the need to understand who controls access.

> **Dark - A wallet manages access; it does not hold bitcoin**
>
> Bitcoin is recorded on the shared [ledger](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#ledger), not inside your phone. Before using a wallet, know who controls the keys, how access is recovered, and what would happen if the device or service failed.

<!-- VISUAL: BD-V-035
ALT: Wallet interface coordinating keys and transaction instructions while bitcoin remains recorded on the ledger.
ASSET: assets/briefs/bd-v-035-brief.md
-->

The first question about any wallet is: **who controls the keys?** Before using a tool, check who can authorize a payment, how access is recovered, and which payment methods it supports. **[Open-source software](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#open-source-software)** publishes its code for anyone to inspect and review. This can improve transparency and make mistakes easier to find, but it is only one useful factor: it does not prove that a tool is safe, maintained, correctly installed, or suitable for your needs.

Compare [custody](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#custody), recovery, whether the code is publicly inspectable, compatibility, [privacy](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#privacy), cost, maintenance, and available support without treating any wallet as the global choice. Start with an educator's prepared environment; do not choose a tool because a stranger recommends it.

> **Callout - Learning wallets and personal wallets**
>
> During the Diploma, you may create a wallet or write down [recovery words](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#seed-phrase-recovery-phrase) as part of practice. A classroom is a learning environment: information can be seen, copied, shared, or compromised by mistake. For this reason, a training wallet should normally be empty or simulated and should not be used to secure meaningful funds. Where practical, it is closed, reset, or returned at the end of the Diploma. Your educator may choose a low-stakes live activity, but should explain its purpose and the added privacy, security, and loss risks before anyone takes part. A wallet intended to secure meaningful funds needs a new, private setup and new recovery information. Your educator can explain the difference, but you are never required to set up a personal wallet to complete the Diploma.

### Addresses

> **Definition - [Bitcoin address](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#bitcoin-address-wallet-address)**
>
> A [Bitcoin address](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#bitcoin-address-wallet-address) is public receiving information for an [on-chain payment](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#on-chain-payment). A wallet normally shows it as text and a QR code. It is not a password and cannot authorize spending by itself.

If you want to receive an [on-chain payment](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#on-chain-payment), where should the address come from? Open the intended wallet and use its receive screen. A sender can scan the QR code or copy the address. This avoids the mistakes that can happen when long strings are typed by hand.

For every payment request, confirm the intended payment method. An on-chain address and a [Lightning invoice](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#lightning-invoice) are different. A new on-chain address for a new receipt can improve [privacy](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#privacy). ### Keys

Bitcoin ownership is enforced through cryptographic keys.

> **Definition - [Private key](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#private-key)**
>
> A [private key](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#private-key) is secret information that allows a wallet to create valid [digital signatures](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#digital-signature). A signature authorizes the spending of bitcoin controlled by the corresponding key. Anyone who obtains the [private key](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#private-key) can usually spend those funds.

A **[public key](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#public-key)** is mathematically related to the [private key](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#private-key). It helps verify a signature without revealing the [private key](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#private-key). Addresses are created from [public-key](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#public-key) information.

The essential rule is simple:

**Public receiving information can be shared. Private spending information must remain secret.**

A [digital signature](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#digital-signature) proves that the required [private key](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#private-key) authorized a [transaction](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction-tx). It does not place the [private key](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#private-key) inside the [transaction](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction-tx). [Nodes](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#node) verify the signature before accepting the spend.

Modern wallets manage many keys automatically. Beginners should not copy or handle individual [private-key](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#private-key) strings. Protect the wallet's approved backup and follow its recovery procedure.

No legitimate educator, wallet company, exchange employee, technical-support worker, or Bitcoin developer needs your [private key](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#private-key) or [seed phrase](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#seed-phrase-recovery-phrase). Anyone who asks for it should be treated as a threat.

**Try it:** Sort prepared examples into three groups: safe to share for receiving, secret spending information, and information that may be public but should still be kept private when linked to a person.

### [Seed phrases](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#seed-phrase-recovery-phrase)

> **Definition - [Seed phrase](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#seed-phrase-recovery-phrase)**
>
> A [seed phrase](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#seed-phrase-recovery-phrase), also called a [recovery phrase](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#seed-phrase-recovery-phrase), is an ordered list of words that many wallets use to derive and recover a set of [private keys](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#private-key). A private key authorizes a spend; the seed phrase is normally the backup that can recreate the wallet's keys. Common lengths are 12 or 24 words, although wallet designs differ.

The order and spelling of the words matter. If a person has the [seed phrase](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#seed-phrase-recovery-phrase), they can often restore the wallet on another compatible device and spend the funds. The [seed phrase](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#seed-phrase-recovery-phrase) is therefore not only a backup. It is full access.

> **Callout – Never share recovery information**
>
> Public receiving information can be shared. A [private key](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#private-key), [seed phrase](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#seed-phrase-recovery-phrase), password, or recovery code must remain secret. No legitimate educator, wallet company, exchange employee, support worker, or Bitcoin developer needs it. If a [seed phrase](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#seed-phrase-recovery-phrase) may have been seen, copied, entered online, or stored on an untrusted device, treat it as compromised and seek help without revealing it.

<!-- VISUAL: BD-V-038
ALT: Recovery relationship from a secret wallet backup to recreated keys, expected addresses, and transaction access.
ASSET: assets/briefs/bd-v-038-brief.md
-->

Protect a [seed phrase](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#seed-phrase-recovery-phrase) with these rules:

- write it down privately and accurately during wallet creation
- follow the wallet's official backup instructions
- keep it offline
- never photograph it, store it in cloud notes, email it, or send it in a message
- never type it into a website or ordinary computer form
- keep it out of cameras, microphones, other people's view, and places where it may be damaged, lost, or found

Some wallets use additional passphrases, multiple keys, or other backup systems. These are [Advanced Learning](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/programs/advanced-modules/). Do not add complexity you cannot explain and recover from safely. If a [seed phrase](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#seed-phrase-recovery-phrase) may have been seen, copied, entered online, or stored on an untrusted device, treat it as compromised: seek guidance without revealing it and use a new trusted recovery process.

### Custodial and non-custodial use

> **Definition - [Custody](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#custody)**
>
> [Custody](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#custody) answers a practical question: who controls the keys required to spend the bitcoin?

In a **custodial** service, a company or another person controls the keys. Your displayed balance is a promise from the [custodian](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#custodian). You usually sign in with an account and ask the service to process a withdrawal or payment.

In a **non-custodial** or **self-custodial** wallet, YOU control the keys. The wallet signs [transactions](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction-tx) under your authority. There may still be software, hardware, server, or network dependencies, but no [custodian](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#custodian) must approve a valid on-chain spend.

| Question | Custodial use | Self-custodial use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Who holds the keys? | The service | The user |
| Who handles recovery? | Usually the service through an account process | The user through a backup process |
| Main [trade-off](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#trade-off) | Familiar support, with provider dependence | Direct control, with backup and error responsibility |

[Custody](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#custody) is not always obvious from the interface. If you cannot control the recovery method and need a company to approve a withdrawal, you are probably using a [custodian](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#custodian). [Self-custody](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#self-custody) is not a test of personal worth; the important step is to identify the trust and risk involved.

**Try it:** Read two prepared wallet descriptions. For each, state who controls the keys, who could restore access, and one risk to check before using it.

### Hot and [cold wallets](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#cold-wallet-cold-storage)

> **Definition - [Hot wallet](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#hot-wallet)**
>
> A [hot wallet](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#hot-wallet) manages keys on a device connected to the internet, such as a phone or computer. It is convenient for regular payments, but has a larger attack surface because malicious software, insecure updates, phishing, physical access, or device compromise may expose the wallet.

> **Definition - [Cold wallet](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#cold-wallet-cold-storage) and [hardware wallet](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#hardware-wallet)**
>
> A [cold wallet](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#cold-wallet-cold-storage) describes an arrangement that keeps signing keys away from an internet-connected general-purpose device. A [hardware wallet](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#hardware-wallet) describes a dedicated device designed to generate, protect, and use keys while keeping them isolated from the connected computer or phone. A hardware wallet can support a cold arrangement, but the terms are not interchangeable; custody and recovery are separate questions.

### Wallet recovery and [seed phrase](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#seed-phrase-recovery-phrase) testing

What would you do if the original device failed? A recovery plan should let you restore the expected wallet without exposing the recovery information. Practice only with educator-prepared, empty, or simulated materials. Follow the wallet's official recovery method privately, confirm the expected training wallet, and reset or return the temporary material safely.

An educator can observe your process without seeing any [recovery words](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#seed-phrase-recovery-phrase). Never enter a real [recovery phrase](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#seed-phrase-recovery-phrase) into a website, message, or ordinary computer form.

A safe recovery-readiness practice is simple:

1. Use the official instructions for the exact training wallet.
2. Confirm that the wallet and its recovery method are compatible.
3. Work privately, without cameras or observers seeing the secret material.
4. Use the wallet's approved backup-check or recovery method.
5. Confirm the expected receiving information or training history.
6. Reset or clear the temporary training environment as instructed.

Do not use a training wallet or its recovery information to secure meaningful funds after the Diploma. A personal wallet, if you choose to create one later, needs a new private setup and new recovery information.

### Receiving bitcoin

**Practice 1: receive safely**

Receiving does not authorize funds to leave your wallet, but it still needs careful checking. Begin by asking: am I using the intended wallet and the intended payment method?

1. Open the prepared wallet and choose **Receive**.
2. Identify whether the request is on-chain or Lightning.
3. Share only the address, QR code, or payment request.
4. If an amount is shown, check it with the sender.
5. Check the amount and status in your own wallet.

A message or screenshot that says “sent” is not proof. What evidence can you see yourself? Check the receiving wallet or approved [transaction](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction-tx) view. For [on-chain payments](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#on-chain-payment), a wallet may first show the [transaction](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction-tx) as pending. [Confirmation](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#confirmation) status adds settlement confidence; it does not repair a wrong address or scam. A wallet may offer a fresh address for a new receipt; older addresses may still work, but reusing one address can make separate payments easier to link. A fresh address reduces that easy link; it does not make a payment anonymous.

### Sending bitcoin

**Practice 2: pause and check before sending**

Sending needs more care because a valid Bitcoin [transaction](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction-tx) is usually difficult or impossible to reverse. Before you touch the approve button, can you explain who will receive the payment and why?

> **Callout – Stop and verify before sending**
>
> Confirm the recipient, payment method, destination, amount and unit, fee or expiry, and final screen. If anything differs from the trusted request, or someone creates urgency or asks for recovery information, stop and verify independently. Use a small test when the process is unfamiliar.

1. Confirm the recipient and purpose through a trusted channel.
2. Confirm the payment method: on-chain or Lightning.
3. Scan or enter the destination, then compare it with the trusted receiving screen.
4. Check the amount, unit, fee or expiry information, and final summary.
5. Use a small test when the process is unfamiliar.
6. Approve only when every item matches; then check the payment result.

For an [on-chain payment](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#on-chain-payment), the **network fee** is paid for including the [transaction](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction-tx) in a block. It depends mainly on the [transaction](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction-tx) data and demand for [block space](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#block-space), not simply on the amount being sent. Fee estimates and timing can change, so read the final summary rather than assuming that a low-value payment has a low fee.

If the destination changes unexpectedly, the request becomes urgent, or anyone asks for recovery information, what should you do? Stop. Verify the request independently, or ask for help without sharing secrets.

### [Verification](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#verification), [block explorers](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#block-explorer), and irreversible [transactions](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction-tx)

> **Definition - [Transaction ID](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction-identifier-txid) and [block explorer](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#block-explorer)**
>
> A [transaction ID](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction-identifier-txid) (*[txid](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction-identifier-txid)*) identifies an on-chain [transaction](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction-tx). A [block explorer](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#block-explorer) shows public Bitcoin data, such as a [transaction](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction-tx)'s status and [confirmation](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#confirmation) count. It is a useful view, not an authority over Bitcoin and not proof of the recipient's identity or honesty.

What can this public information show, and what can it not show? It can show a [transaction](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction-tx)'s status, destination, amount, fee, and [confirmation](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#confirmation) count; it cannot prove why a payment happened or whether a person is trustworthy. An on-chain [transaction](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction-tx) is unconfirmed after it is [broadcast](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#broadcast) and gains its [first confirmation](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#first-confirmation) when included in a valid block. Later blocks add confidence to its place in the record, but they do not fix a wrong destination or a scam. There is no universal [confirmation](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#confirmation) count for every payment; the suitable check depends on the amount and risk.

To inspect a prepared [transaction](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction-tx), obtain its *[txid](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction-identifier-txid)*, open a trusted explorer for the intended [Bitcoin network](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#bitcoin-network), and check the status, destination, amount, fee, and [confirmations](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#confirmation). An explorer is only an interface. Searching an address or *[txid](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction-identifier-txid)* may reveal to its operator that your device is interested in that information. For practice, use an educator-prepared [transaction](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction-tx) or printed screenshot. Do not search a learner's personal address or [transaction](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction-tx) on a public service. If you later research a personal payment, learn the privacy and security trade-offs of your connection before using a public service.

> **Callout – Public data is not proof of identity or honesty**
>
> A [block explorer](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#block-explorer) can show public [transaction](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction-tx) data, but it cannot prove who controls an address, why a payment happened, or whether a recipient is trustworthy. Use prepared examples for practice and protect [privacy](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#privacy) when searching public services.

The safe response is always: **Pause. Share nothing. Send nothing. Close the channel. Verify independently through a known source. Start with safe practice, a simulation, or a small approved amount; never borrow money to experiment.

### Acquiring and exchanging bitcoin

#### Awareness, not financial advice

People may receive bitcoin through work, a community payment, a peer exchange, an ATM, or a service. Each pathway has different costs, [custody](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#custody), privacy, fraud, legal, and operational [trade-offs](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#trade-off). The Bitcoin Diploma does not recommend a provider or tell you which path to use.

> **Light - Compare a local payment route**
>
> In a prepared local or fictional scenario, compare [custody](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#custody), total cost, identity data, withdrawal limits, counterparty and physical safety, and current local rules. No pathway is automatically best everywhere. This course provides comparison questions, not a provider recommendation or personal financial advice.

- **Earning or receiving:** Bitcoin may be received for work, goods, services, a gift, a refund, or a community payment. The parties still need to agree on the amount, timing, and payment method.
- **Peer-to-peer exchange:** Two people may exchange [fiat money](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#fiat-money) or goods for bitcoin directly or through a matching service. This can introduce counterparty, physical-safety, payment-reversal, and fraud risks.
- **Exchange, broker, ATM, or kiosk:** A service or machine may provide a familiar route, but may involve fees, identity checks, [custody](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#custody), withdrawal limits, or a poor [exchange rate](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#exchange-rate).

Before comparing a path, ask:

### On-chain and Lightning payments

<!-- VISUAL: BD-V-050

> **Definition - On-chain and Lightning payments**
>
> [On-chain payments](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#on-chain-payment) are recorded in Bitcoin's [blockchain](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#blockchain) and gain [confirmations](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#confirmation). Lightning is a payment network built on Bitcoin that can support fast, frequent payments. A Lightning payment may complete quickly or fail, and its wallet can still be custodial or self-custodial.

> **Dark - Payment path does not determine [custody](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#custody)**
>
> On-chain and Lightning payments make different [trade-offs](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#trade-off). A Lightning wallet can be custodial or self-custodial, and a fast successful payment does not prove that a service is suitable for larger balances or every situation.

| Question | On-chain Bitcoin | Lightning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Receiving information | [Bitcoin address](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#bitcoin-address-wallet-address) or [on-chain payment](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#on-chain-payment) request | [Lightning invoice](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#lightning-invoice), offer, or supported [Lightning address](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#lightning-address) |
| Payment result | [Broadcast](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#broadcast) first, then [confirmations](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#confirmation) | Usually completes or fails quickly |
| Common use | [Base-layer settlement](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#base-layer-settlement) or less frequent transfer | Fast, smaller, or frequent payment |
| Beginner checks | Correct address, network, amount, fee, and [confirmation](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#confirmation) status | Correct invoice or request, amount, expiry, [custody](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#custody), and payment result |

Confirm whether they have provided an on-chain address or a [Lightning invoice](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#lightning-invoice) or request before paying. An on-chain wallet cannot directly pay every [Lightning invoice](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#lightning-invoice), and a Lightning wallet may not support every on-chain action. ### Review: what does [self-custody](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#self-custody) responsibility mean?

<!-- VISUAL: BD-V-052

-->

<!-- VISUAL: BD-V-053

Imagine that a friend sends you a payment request while an unfamiliar support account says you must act immediately. What could you check before doing anything? Can you protect a recovery plan, distinguish receiving information from secrets, and verify the person, payment method, destination, amount, and final screen before sending? Whether a service or you control the keys, the practical recap is the same: protect secrets, verify before approving, recognize scam signals, and keep mistakes small while learning.

#### Key takeaways

- A wallet manages the keys and information used to receive, check, and spend bitcoin; bitcoin is not stored on a phone or other device.
- Addresses and payment requests can be shared to receive bitcoin, but [private keys](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#private-key) and [seed phrases](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#seed-phrase-recovery-phrase) must remain secret.
- A [seed phrase](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#seed-phrase-recovery-phrase) can provide full recovery access, so it must stay offline, private, and out of websites and messages.
- Custodial and self-custodial arrangements create different recovery and control responsibilities. [Hardware wallets](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#hardware-wallet) still depend on a safe backup.
- Before receiving or sending, check the correct payment method, destination, amount, and final status; a valid send is usually difficult or impossible to reverse.
- Start with safe practice or small approved amounts. Pause when a request creates urgency, secrecy, or pressure.

### Lightning payments

<!-- VISUAL: BD-V-080
ALT: Lightning address and payment-request distinction for a safe local payment practice.
ASSET: assets/briefs/bd-v-080-brief.md
-->

<!-- VISUAL: BD-V-059
ALT: Six-step guided Lightning payment checklist for recipient, invoice, amount, expiry, confirmation, and safe completion.
ASSET: assets/briefs/bd-v-059-brief.md
-->

Lightning can make fast, small Bitcoin payments practical. It may be suitable for a merchant counter, repeated local payments, online services, or transfers where an on-chain fee would be too large in relation to the payment.

A local Lightning demonstration should identify:

- whether the wallet is custodial or self-custodial
- how the wallet is funded
- whether funding requires an on-chain [transaction](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction-tx) or a service
- what backup or recovery protects the balance
- what type of payment request is being used
- what fees or limits apply
- what happens when a payment fails
- how the recipient confirms payment

A **[Lightning invoice](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#lightning-invoice)** is a payment request created for a specific payment. It can include an amount, destination information, and an expiry time. Read the wallet's [confirmation](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#confirmation) screen before paying.

A **[Lightning address](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#lightning-address)** is a human-readable identifier supported by some services. It may look like an email address, but it is a payment-routing convenience, not an email account. It depends on the provider or domain that supports it.

For a guided payment:

1. The receiver creates the correct request.
2. The sender scans or copies it.
3. The sender checks the recipient, amount, unit, and fee.
4. The sender approves only after the details are correct.
5. Both sides confirm the result in their own wallets.
6. The group explains the [custody](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#custody) and failure risks.

Do not assume that a successful Lightning payment proves the wallet is safe for larger balances. Payment convenience and [custody](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#custody) security are separate questions.

### Daily-life Bitcoin use

<!-- VISUAL: BD-V-060
ALT: Seven everyday Bitcoin-use examples across earning, saving, receiving, sending, spending, and community use.
ASSET: assets/briefs/bd-v-060-brief.md
-->

Bitcoin can function as money in several ways. Spending is only one of them.

**Earning:** A worker, creator, business, or community member receives bitcoin for [value](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#value) provided. Earning can avoid a separate purchase process, but the receiver must agree on pricing, [volatility](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#volatility), records, and [custody](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#custody).

**Saving:** A person holds bitcoin for future use. Predictable supply may be attractive, but the fiat exchange [value](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#value) can fall sharply and key security becomes important.

**Receiving:** A person accepts a gift, donation, refund, remittance, or repayment. The receiver must provide the correct payment request and verify settlement.

**Sending:** A person transfers [value](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#value) to family, friends, a business, or an organization. They must compare payment path, speed, fee, [privacy](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#privacy), and finality.

**Spending:** A person exchanges bitcoin for goods or services. They should identify the [exchange rate](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#exchange-rate), merchant policy, payment method, and local tax consequences that apply.

**Pricing and accounting:** A person or business records [value](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#value) in bitcoin or [sats](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#satoshi-sat). In many places, the fiat [value](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#value) at the time of payment is still needed for accounts or taxes.

**Donating:** A person supports an organization or individual directly. They should verify the recipient's payment request through a trusted source.

For any use, ask three questions:

1. What existing problem does [Bitcoin address](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#bitcoin-address-wallet-address) here?
2. Is Bitcoin better than the available alternative for this case?
3. What new responsibility or risk does Bitcoin create?

Avoid starting with the tool. Start with the need.

Use locally familiar goods and services. The exact item does not matter; the payment reasoning does.

**Scenario 1: A small purchase**

A customer wants to buy an everyday item. The merchant accepts Lightning and on-chain Bitcoin.

- Which path is likely to suit a small, immediate payment?
- Who creates the payment request?
- How does the customer check the amount and unit?
- How does the merchant know the payment succeeded?
- What happens if the customer's wallet shows “sent” but the merchant does not see “received”?

**Scenario 2: Payment for work**

A worker agrees to receive part of a payment in bitcoin.

- Is the [price](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#price) fixed in local [currency](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#currency), bitcoin, or [sats](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#satoshi-sat)?
- At what time is the [exchange rate](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#exchange-rate) determined?
- Who pays any fee?
- Is the receiver using [self-custody](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#self-custody)?
- What income or tax record is required?
- Can the worker safely manage the amount?

**Scenario 3: A transfer between family members**

One person wants to send [value](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#value) to a family member in another place.

- What are the costs and delays of the current method?
- How will each person obtain or use bitcoin?
- Does the recipient want bitcoin or local [currency](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#currency)?
- Which exchange and [custody](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#custody) risks appear at each step?
- Does the full route save time or money after all fees are included?

**Scenario 4: A refund**

A merchant needs to return [value](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#value) after an [on-chain payment](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#on-chain-payment).

- Why should the merchant request a fresh refund address?
- How can the merchant verify that the request really came from the customer?
- Who pays the return [transaction fee](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction-fee-miner-fee)?
- What record should both parties keep?

**Scenario 5: A community donation**

A local organization wants to receive support in bitcoin.

- How will donors verify the real payment request?
- Who controls the receiving wallet and backup?
- Will donations be received on-chain, through Lightning, or both?
- What information should be public for transparency, and what should remain private for donor safety?
- What records are needed for local accounting, tax, or reporting rules?

For a live practice, use a low-value item or a simulation. The learner should be able to explain the complete flow before approving payment.

### [KYC](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#know-your-customer-kyc) and [no-KYC](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#no-kyc-preferred-also-called-non-kyc) [trade-offs](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#trade-off) where locally appropriate

[KYC](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#know-your-customer-kyc) and [no-KYC](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#no-kyc-preferred-also-called-non-kyc) pathways make different [trade-offs](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#trade-off). Neither label removes the need for careful evaluation.

> **Callout – [Privacy](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#privacy) does not remove legal or physical-safety risk**
>
> Neither [KYC](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#know-your-customer-kyc) nor [no-KYC](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#no-kyc-preferred-also-called-non-kyc) is automatically best. Compare the actual pathway, use current local guidance when rules are unclear, and do not break local law or expose yourself to physical danger in pursuit of [privacy](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#privacy).

A [KYC](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#know-your-customer-kyc) service may request a legal name, identity document, address, photograph, source-of-funds information, or other records. This may allow the service to meet local rules, connect to banks, set higher limits, or support account recovery.

[KYC](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#know-your-customer-kyc) also creates data risk. The service may store a permanent connection among identity, payment methods, and bitcoin [transactions](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction-tx). A breach, legal request, internal misuse, or change in policy may expose or restrict that information.

A [no-KYC](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#no-kyc-preferred-also-called-non-kyc) pathway may collect less identity information. This can improve financial [privacy](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#privacy). It may also involve less regulated counterparties, lower liquidity, higher [prices](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#price), reduced support, greater fraud risk, or legal restrictions.

[Privacy](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#privacy) is not secrecy from every risk. It is the ability to control information about yourself. Do not break local law or expose yourself to physical danger in pursuit of [privacy](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#privacy). Seek current local guidance from a qualified person when the rules are unclear.

### Circular economies

<!-- VISUAL: BD-V-065
ALT: Local circular-economy map connecting earners, users, merchants, exchange, support, and risks.
ASSET: assets/briefs/bd-v-065-brief.md
-->

A **[Bitcoin circular economy](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#bitcoin-circular-economy)** is a network in which people earn, spend, save, and exchange bitcoin within a community. [Value](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#value) circulates among workers, customers, merchants, producers, educators, and service providers instead of entering only through a one-time purchase and leaving through immediate conversion.

My First Bitcoin supports [Bitcoin circular economies](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#bitcoin-circular-economy), and many are part of the Bitcoin Education Network. Explore these communities on the [My First Bitcoin dashboard](https://leaderboard.myfirstbitcoin.org/?min10merchants=1).

A circular economy can reduce dependence on distant financial services and create direct local relationships. It can also fail if there is little demand, poor education, unreliable tools, weak security, or pressure to use bitcoin without a real need.

Map a simple local cycle:

1. Who can earn bitcoin?
2. Where can they spend or use it?
3. Which merchants can pay a supplier or worker in bitcoin?
4. Which participants need to exchange to or from fiat?
5. Who provides education and technical support?
6. What happens when internet access fails?
7. What [custody](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#custody) and [price](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#price) risks does each participant carry?

A healthy circular economy depends on voluntary participation. Merchants and workers should be able to identify the payment, pricing, [custody](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#custody), accounting, and [volatility](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#volatility) risks. Adoption numbers alone do not show whether the network is useful.

The strongest local loop solves a real problem for more than one participant. It should not depend permanently on giveaways or on one organizer processing every payment.

### Merchant or community use cases

<!-- VISUAL: BD-V-066
ALT: Merchant payment and community-use flow including pricing, confirmation, custody, refunds, and records.
ASSET: assets/briefs/bd-v-066-brief.md
-->

A merchant needs more than a receive button. The full payment process may include pricing, staff training, receipts, refunds, [custody](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#custody), conversion, accounting, taxes, device security, and support.

Before accepting Bitcoin, a merchant should decide:

- whether [prices](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#price) are displayed in local [currency](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#currency), [sats](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#satoshi-sat), or both
- whether payments use on-chain Bitcoin, Lightning, or both
- whether the wallet is custodial or self-custodial
- who can access the receiving device
- how received funds are secured or moved
- whether some funds are converted to local [currency](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#currency)
- how payment is confirmed at the point of sale
- how refunds and mistakes are handled
- what records local law requires
- what staff do when the network or wallet fails

Community use cases may include:

- paying local workers or creators
- receiving donations transparently
- transferring [value](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#value) between community groups
- supporting a savings group with clearly defined [custody](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#custody)
- helping a remote worker receive international payment
- connecting local producers and merchants
- teaching safe wallet use
- providing emergency support where ordinary payment systems are unavailable

[Community custody](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#community-custody) requires special care. If one organizer holds funds for many people, that organizer becomes a [custodian](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#custodian) and a point of trust. [Shared funds](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#shared-funds) need transparent rules, records, access controls, and legal review where appropriate.

For each use case, define success in terms of the problem solved, not the number of [transactions](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction-tx) completed.

### Local resource maps and community discovery

Detailed local-resource activities, record tables, and changeable listings belong on the [Resources Hub](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/resources/?search=map). Maps and community directories can help you find merchants, educators, meetups, and circular economies, but listings can be incomplete or outdated.

Before relying on a listing, check when it was verified, confirm the information independently, and bring an alternative way to pay or participate. A local community can provide practical support, but membership is not proof of trustworthiness. Do not reveal [seed phrases](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#seed-phrase-recovery-phrase), balances, home addresses, or security arrangements at a meetup. Be cautious when a new contact offers investment management, private exchange, guaranteed returns, or urgent help.

### Review: connect this to your community

Complete this review individually, then compare your reasoning with your team or cohort. Consider the local example you studied: what real need did it address, which function of money was involved, and was [value](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#value) moved on-chain, through Lightning, or both? Ask who controlled the keys, what the strongest benefit and most serious risk were, and how local law, infrastructure, language, or culture affected the result. A responsible next step is one that fits the local context, makes costs and [custody](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#custody) clear, limits errors and scams, and can be evaluated against evidence.

**Module 4 answer:** Bitcoin can function as money when people voluntarily use it to earn, save, receive, send, spend, [price](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#price), or settle [value](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#value). The Connect stage begins with a real need, compares a realistic alternative, makes [custody](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#custody) and costs clear, respects local law and safety, and gives learners enough knowledge to choose freely.

#### Key takeaways

- Bitcoin as money is local: the useful pathway depends on community needs, infrastructure, laws, costs, tools, language, and safety conditions.
- An educator-curated **Module 4** Connect activity should link the Problem, Solution, and Action learning to a real problem or opportunity, realistic alternative, safe practice, and evidence of learning.
- Local workshops should use a safe sequence: observe the process, practice with support, then explain or complete a defined task without exposing secrets.
- Before using a local tool or service, check its purpose, [custody](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#custody) model, source, recovery process, [privacy](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#privacy), costs, compatibility, support, and current local rules.
- On-chain and Lightning payments make different [trade-offs](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#trade-off) in settlement, speed, fees, [custody](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#custody), and failure points; confirm which payment method a recipient expects.
- Local exchange pathways, [KYC](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#know-your-customer-kyc) choices, merchant flows, and community use cases should be compared through their full costs, [custody](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#custody), privacy, safety, and legal [trade-offs](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#trade-off).
- A strong circular economy or community use case is voluntary, solves a real problem, protects participants, and does not make learners dependent on one service, organizer, or promise.

### A safety reminder

<!-- VISUAL: BD-V-072
ALT: Final safety reminder to protect secrets, verify payment details, use small tests, and stop under pressure.
ASSET: assets/briefs/bd-v-072-brief.md
-->

Remember these rules:

- A [seed phrase](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#seed-phrase-recovery-phrase) or [private key](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#private-key) gives control of funds. Never share it.
- Verify the recipient, network, address or invoice, amount, and fee before sending.
- Bitcoin [transactions](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction-tx) are usually irreversible.
- Use a small test when the process is new.
- Do not act under pressure, fear, or promises of guaranteed profit.
- Verify wallet software and support contacts through independent sources.
- Identify who holds the keys.
- Test recovery safely before meaningful funds depend on it.
- Keep financial balances and security arrangements private.
- Laws, taxes, fees, and services change. Check current local information.

Education reduces risk but cannot remove it. If a situation is unclear, stop and learn more before acting.

### Certificate meaning and issuer

The 2026.10 curriculum is published as a teaching package and assessment framework first. Publication does not mean that every educator or local route is ready to issue completion certificates. An educator issues a certificate only after preparing the required tasks, accessible routes, assessment record, and appropriate local safeguards, and after assessing the learner against the approved theoretical and practical requirements.

There are two certificate types:

- A **certificate of participation** confirms attendance or participation. It does not confirm that the learner passed the assessment.
- A **certificate of completion** confirms that the named educator assessed the learner and judged that every required competency met the standard.

The certificate is certification by the named educator or educator's organization. My First Bitcoin does not centrally evaluate the learner, issue the certificate, provide a central verification service, or guarantee the learner's competency. A completion certificate represents assessed beginner competence under supported conditions, not investment skill, professional financial competence, or readiness to manage high-value [self-custody](https://programs.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#self-custody).

If the issuing educator provides a verification method, use only the instructions printed on the certificate or supplied through an independently confirmed educator channel. Check the course edition, educator or issuing organization, completion date, and status while sharing the minimum personal information necessary. A My First Bitcoin name or logo does not turn an educator-issued certificate into central My First Bitcoin certification.

Your most important result is not the certificate. It is the ability to explain money and Bitcoin clearly, recognize risk, verify before acting, and continue learning independently.
