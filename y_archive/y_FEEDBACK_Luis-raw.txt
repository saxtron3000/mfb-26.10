HL about Module 3: 

I can see the huge challenge of presenting the topics on this chapter. Overall, I think the structure and topics are solid. But I have 2 concerns. The first one:

Some safety warnings are vague, refer to unexplained complex issues, and as a reader, it made me feel like it was rather unsafe to use Bitcoin, without enough recourse as to how to make it safe.
For example: p.57, "Verify independently through a known source." My immediate questions would be, what would be a known source? Known to whom? How do I know what source to trust?
Or on p.51, "Prefer well-established open-source wallet software where appropriate, while remembering that open source alone does not prove a tool is safe or suitable." As a reader, my immediate questions are, what even is "open-source." why choose open source, and if that doesn't prove safety, what does? I would feel paralyzed.
None of this should be a problem in a classroom setting, where hopefully an educator can be that source of trust, and give the answers, but for readers studying independently, giving warnings without providing clear, specific solutions could be discouraging (a little like a wet blanket)

luis
2 days ago
My second HL concern about module 3, is that there were some conceptual ambiguities, namely:

Sometimes concepts are not defined before being used. The quote above about open source tools is a good example.
The text doesn't seem to make clear what is the difference between concepts that are overlapping, or similar but not identical, notably cold wallets and hardware wallets (p.54), or a private key and a seed phrase.
In this latter case, each concept is explained well, but not their difference. For example, on p57, the terms are used as if they are 2 separate things: "Protect secrets by keeping seed phrases and private keys offline..." when for practical purposes, most times the seedphrase is HOW we safeguard a private key. My fear is that a student will read that and get tremendously confused.
