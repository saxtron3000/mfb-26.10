# 1. Project Title

Bitcoin Diploma Redesign

---

# 2. Project Purpose

Redesign the Bitcoin Diploma to maintain its relevance, improve effectiveness, increase long-term educational impact, and strengthen its role as a serious standard for grassroots Bitcoin education.

This is not a replacement of the diploma’s identity or foundations. It is a redesign to make the program clearer, shorter, more modular, more practical, more captivating, more teachable, and more scalable.

---

# 3. North Star

## Draft from Source Material

A shorter, clearer, more practical, more modular, and more captivating Bitcoin Diploma that preserves educational quality, empowers independent educators, supports learner competence, and remains philosophically grounded, community-driven, open-source, and globally adaptable.

# 4. Design Philosophy

## Source-Based Principles

### Teach How to Think, Not What to Think
- Learners develop independent reasoning.
- Content should raise questions rather than prescribe answers.
- Educators facilitate discovery.

### Empower Independent Educators
- Curriculum should support grassroots, community-led delivery.
- Educators should be able to adapt to local context without losing standards.
- The Educator Guide should help educators teach, not merely repeat student content.

### Build From First Principles
- Economics, money, technology, and sovereignty should be approached through foundational reasoning.
- Learners should understand why concepts matter before being pushed into technical details.

### Practical Competence Matters
- The diploma should not remain theoretical.
- Learners need practical pathways into Bitcoin usage and participation.
- Practical learning should be structured, not incidental.

### Community and Dialogue
- Social learning, dialogue, and discussion are strengths of the diploma.
- Redesign should preserve and strengthen these features.

# 6. Key Motivations

## Filled from Source Material

### Maintain Relevance
Bitcoin education is maturing. The diploma needs to remain relevant as learner needs, educator needs, and implementation contexts evolve.

### Improve Effectiveness
The diploma must become easier to teach, easier to complete, and easier to evaluate.

### Reduce Attrition
Duration and cognitive load are major risks. High attrition harms long-term program success.

### Improve Scalability
A clearer structure, stronger educator support, and modular design can help more communities deliver the diploma consistently.

### Strengthen Practical Learning
The diploma should build practical orientation and applied confidence, not only conceptual familiarity.

### Empower Educators
Educators need better implementation guidance, facilitation support, timing, classroom structures, and adaptation notes.

### Protect Community Integrity
The diploma should remain serious, grassroots, open-source, and community-driven.

# 7. Focus Areas

## 7.1 Curriculum Scope
- Clarify what the diploma is responsible for teaching.
- Reduce vague scope.
- Distinguish essential, optional, and advanced content.
- Clarify multidisciplinary boundaries.

## 7.2 Learning Objectives
- Make objectives precise and measurable.
- Map objectives to competencies.
- Map competencies to activities and assessment.

## 7.3 Practical Learning
- Structure practical content using PPP.
- Clarify realistic beginner actions.
- Address self-custody constraints.
- Clarify Lightning vs on-chain teaching.

## 7.4 Educator Support
- Redesign the Educator Guide around implementation.
- Include facilitation notes, timing, prompts, misconceptions, review, and adaptations.

## 7.5 Activity Design
- Build an Activity Bank.
- Integrate activities into the diploma flow.
- Support both in-person and online delivery.

## 7.6 Review and Consolidation
- Build structured review.
- Use formative checks and synthesis.
- Reduce inefficient repetition.

## 7.7 Localization
- Adapt examples and analogies to local contexts.
- Preserve curriculum standards and assessment comparability.

## 7.8 Visual and Student Experience Design
- Improve layout, hierarchy, definitions, tables, diagrams, worksheets, and print/digital usability.

# 8. Blind Spots / Risks

## Filled from Source Material

### Duration Blind Spot
- Current timing expectations are unclear.
- It is not clear whether activities are included in estimated module time.

### Scope Blind Spot
- The diploma covers many domains.
- Without clear boundaries, the program risks becoming too broad.

### Mode Blind Spot
- Synchronous, asynchronous, online, in-person, hybrid, and self-paced delivery are not yet clearly distinguished.

### Assessment Blind Spot
- Certification may measure the wrong thing if designed after content.
- Competencies need to be defined first.

### Educator Support Blind Spot
- Educator Guide may not provide enough added value.
- Educators may need much more practical teaching support.

### Localization Blind Spot
- Localization is necessary but can dilute standards if not governed.

### Practical Competence Blind Spot
- Practical content exists but is not consistently structured through PPP.

### Technical Overload Blind Spot
- Some technical details may not be beginner prerequisites.

### Chapter 1 Design Blind Spot
- Chapter 1 has both form and content issues.
- Redesign must not treat layout as the only problem.
