# New Section: Graduate Profile

## Design Question

What should a graduate of the Bitcoin Diploma look like?

The redesign should eventually define:

### Knowledge

What learners know.

### Competence

What learners can do.

### Reasoning

How learners think.

### Confidence

What learners feel capable of doing independently.

### Participation

How learners engage with Bitcoin after completion.

This profile should drive all future design decisions.

---

# New Section: Audience Assumptions

The redesign should explicitly state:

- assumed prior knowledge;
- literacy assumptions;
- technology assumptions;
- educator assumptions;
- language assumptions;
- learning environment assumptions.

Design decisions should avoid hidden prerequisites.

---

# New Section: Adaptation Boundaries

The redesign should distinguish between:

## Fixed

- Learning objectives
- Core outcomes
- Competency expectations

## Adaptable

- Examples
- Case studies
- Activities
- Timing
- Facilitation style

## Flexible With Guardrails

- Module sequencing
- Delivery mode adaptations
- Localization choices

The redesign should make these boundaries explicit.

---

# New Section: Technology Design Principles

The Diploma should be designed with technology diversity in mind.

Questions:

- Can learning occur offline?
- Can activities be delivered with minimal connectivity?
- Can learning occur with only mobile devices?
- What assumptions are being made about internet access?

Technology should support learning rather than become a prerequisite.

---

# New Focus Area: Spiral Review

The redesign should intentionally revisit important concepts.

Review should:

- deepen understanding;
- reinforce retention;
- connect modules together;
- strengthen long-term learning.

Spiral review should be designed rather than accidental.

---

# New Focus Area: Ecosystem Design

The redesign should be viewed as an ecosystem rather than a single document.

Core components:

- Student Book
- Educator Guide
- Activity Bank

Supporting components:

- Website
- GitHub
- PDFs
- Translations
- Future tools

Each component should have a clear purpose.

---

# New Focus Area: Facilitator-Driven Design

The Educator Guide should function as a facilitation engine.

Design should prioritize:

- educator confidence;
- educator consistency;
- educator preparation;
- educator decision-making support.

The educator experience should be intentionally designed rather than treated as secondary.

---

# New Focus Area: Low-Connectivity Design

The redesign should consider:

- offline learning;
- low-bandwidth environments;
- paper-first alternatives;
- technology failure contingencies.

Activities should not assume ideal infrastructure.

---

# Additional Design Risks

## Educator Friction Risk

If the redesign increases educator workload, adoption may decrease.

## Infrastructure Risk

Activities that depend on connectivity, devices, or software may fail in some environments.

## Coherence Risk

Modularity may weaken conceptual progression if not carefully designed.

## Outcome Drift Risk

Adaptation and localization may gradually weaken learning outcomes.

---

# Additional Design Questions

## Learner Journey

- What is the learner journey from beginning to completion?
- What are the key moments of transformation?
- What concepts are foundational?
- What concepts are optional?

## Educator Journey

- What does an educator need before teaching?
- What support is required during delivery?
- What support is required after delivery?

## Ecosystem Questions

- What belongs in the Student Book?
- What belongs in the Educator Guide?
- What belongs in the Activity Bank?
- What belongs on the website?

## Accessibility Questions

- What happens when internet access fails?
- What happens when learners lack devices?
- What happens when classes are interrupted?

# Additional Learner Experience Goals

The learner experience should be:

- coherent;
- modular;
- discussion-rich;
- practical;
- confidence-building;
- outcome-oriented;
- globally adaptable;
- low-friction;
- memorable.

---

# Additional Non-Negotiables

- Preserve narrative coherence.
- Preserve conceptual progression.
- Preserve accessibility.
- Preserve educator usability.
- Preserve grassroots adaptability.
- Preserve community learning.
- Preserve learner autonomy.
- Design for real-world implementation, not ideal conditions.
