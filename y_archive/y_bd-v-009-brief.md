## Detailed visual specification

### Teaching point and learner outcome

Direct barter requires both people in an exchange to want what the other offers. Learners should be able to identify why none of the three proposed direct exchanges satisfies both people and distinguish this mismatch from a claim that barter never works.

### Composition and reading order

Use a full-width landscape human educational scene, arranged as a triangular three-role mismatch pattern in the `teaching-page` print skeleton. Place Amina at left, Luis at upper right, and Mei at lower right, with generous paper-ground negative space between them. Each upright character holds the good they have at torso level; directly above each person, place an upright thought bubble containing the good or service they want. Use direct live labels beside each held good and thought bubble. Connect the three people around the triangle with three directional, straight or right-angle attempted-exchange paths: Amina-to-Luis, Luis-to-Mei, and Mei-to-Amina. Interrupt every path with an unmistakable broken segment and a small crossed failure cue. Read Amina’s has/wants pair first, then Luis’s, then Mei’s, then follow the three failed paths clockwise. The whole composition concludes that no direct exchange works; do not add a next step.

### Content and live text

- Editable, translation-ready person labels: `Amina`, `Luis`, and `Mei`.
- Editable, translation-ready possession labels: `Has: rice`, `Has: shoes`, and `Has: cooking oil`.
- Editable, translation-ready want labels: `Wants: shoes`, `Wants: cooking oil`, and `Wants: repair work`.
- Rice, shoes, cooking oil, and a simple repair-work cue (for example, a hand tool) are direct, recognizable object/service representations; each must remain individually editable.
- Each attempted-exchange path must have an editable failure cue, such as `No direct match`, if a short explanatory label is needed for localization. Do not introduce a final explanatory headline, prices, quantities, currencies, or additional example copy.
- Crossed failure marks communicate a mismatch, not danger, prohibition, or a negative judgement about the people or goods.

## Required visual treatment

Use the Human educational scene recipe and a reusable triangular barter-mismatch variant of the Tier 3 Process flow pattern, placed in the `teaching-page` skeleton. Render flat, print-safe MFB Angular Editorial Illustration on a paper (`#F6F6F8`) or white ground. Use primary purple (`#422C70`) for the restrained structural frame, labels, and attempted-exchange connectors; cool divider (`#D8D8E7`) for quiet separation; body neutral (`#25252B`) for ordinary text and object outlines; and controlled orange (`#F7941F`) only to focus attention on the failure interruption or one key mismatch cue. Pair each orange cue with the broken connector and a cross so colour is not its only meaning.

Use IBM Plex Sans only, with short left-aligned live labels; learner-facing type is at least 12pt. Use one restrained 13° parallelogram framing or section accent, with counter-skewed upright contents; keep people, goods, thought bubbles, icons, and labels upright. Connectors use the approved monoline construction, straight or 90° elbow routes, butt caps, miter joins, and open-chevron arrowheads. Use simple direct object drawings or approved first-party icons only; do not overload the image with icons or decoration.

Use exactly three needed Character Bible roles: C02 as Amina, C04 as Luis, and C03 as Mei. Depict all as capable, active participants in three-quarter or useful-action poses holding their respective goods. The production artist must preserve each approved character’s fixed face, hair, hand construction, outline weight, clothing identity, proportions, and stylised brand-tone skin treatment. The rice, shoes, cooking-oil container, and repair-work cue are new scene props and need Character Bible review before reuse.

Do not use gradients, glossy/3D effects, shadows, gold coins, crypto imagery, generic blockchain symbols, corporate-cartoon proportions, redrawn MFB marks, unrelated background scenes, or decorative clutter.

## Safety, exclusions, and qualifications

- Show failed direct exchange only. Include no money, Bitcoin, currency, wallet, payment interface, QR code, price, balance, successful trade, or solution; BD-V-010 separately introduces a commonly accepted medium.
- Do not imply that barter never works. The visual demonstrates a particular three-person mismatch; nearby guide text qualifies that barter can work in a small exchange and becomes harder to coordinate at scale.
- Do not add financial, legal, tax, accounting, compliance, or investment advice, claims of returns or safety, real identities, or personal/payment data.
- Keep all labels editable and localization-ready. The people and goods are illustrative examples, not endorsements or universal claims about exchange.
- Use the approved character cast only. New poses, outfits, props, or accessibility variants require review against the Character Bible before reuse.
