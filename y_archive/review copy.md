# Executive Summary

The Bitcoin Diploma is already a leading grassroots Bitcoin education program. The redesign is not intended to replace its philosophy, identity, or purpose. The goal is to improve effectiveness, completion rates, educator usability, practical competence, and long-term impact.

The redesign is guided by several decisions that have now been resolved:

- Target duration: maximum 8 hours.
- Working design assumption: 4 modules of approximately 2 hours each.
- In-person first.
- Single Diploma with optional advanced sections.
- Potential content categorization: Core, Nice-to-Have, Advanced.
- Certification design deferred until learning outcomes are finalized.
- Learning objectives will combine knowledge, competency, and outcome-based objectives.
- Practical learning should increase from approximately 20% to approximately 40%.
- All practical skills should follow PPP (Presentation, Practice, Production).
- Advanced technical content should be moved to optional advanced sections.
- The Educator Guide should become an essential teaching companion.
- The Activity Bank should be both embedded and standalone.

---

# Bottom Line

The core problem is not a lack of content.

The core problems are:

- Excessive duration
- Weak competency mapping
- Weak learning objectives
- Unclear scope
- Limited practical learning
- Inconsistent educator support
- Insufficient review systems
- Cognitive overload in some areas

The redesign should produce:

- A shorter diploma
- A more modular diploma
- A more practical diploma
- A more teachable diploma
- A more scalable diploma
- A more evidence-based diploma

while preserving educational quality and philosophical integrity.

# Updated Design Direction

## Duration

Current implementation varies significantly.

The redesign target is:

- Maximum 8 hours total
- Approximately 4 modules
- Approximately 2 hours per module

This remains provisional and may change as learning outcomes and module requirements are finalized.

The guiding principle is:

> Every minute must justify its existence.

---

## Delivery Model

Primary mode:

- In-person

Secondary adaptations:

- Online
- Self-paced
- Hybrid

The Diploma should be designed for face-to-face learning first and adapted elsewhere.

---

## Program Structure

Current direction:

### Core

Essential knowledge, skills, and concepts required by every learner.

### Nice-to-Have

Helpful extensions that improve understanding but are not required.

### Advanced

Technical and theoretical material beyond beginner requirements.

Advanced material should not interfere with beginner competence.

---

## Learning Objectives

Each module should contain:

### Knowledge Objectives

What learners should know.

### Competency Objectives

What learners should be able to do.

### Outcome Objectives

What changes should occur in learner capability, confidence, or behavior.

---

## Practical Learning

The current Diploma appears heavily weighted toward:

- Reading
- Discussion
- Reflection

Practical learning appears to represent approximately 20% of learning time.

The redesign target is approximately 40%.

This does not necessarily mean wallet exercises.

It includes:

- Simulations
- Structured activities
- Decision-making exercises
- Guided application
- Problem-solving
- Real-world scenarios

The goal is active participation rather than passive discussion.

---

## PPP Framework

All practical skills should follow:

1. Presentation
2. Practice
3. Production

This becomes the preferred instructional model for applied learning.

---

## Technical Content

Technical concepts should be categorized as:

### Essential

Required for beginner competence.

### Advanced

Useful but optional.

Advanced content should move into advanced sections rather than remain in the main pathway.

---

## Educator Guide Vision

The Educator Guide should not mirror the Student Book.

Its purpose is to:

- Support implementation
- Improve teaching quality
- Improve consistency
- Demonstrate educational standards

The guide should include:

- Facilitation guidance
- Timing guidance
- Discussion prompts
- Activity procedures
- Practical lesson procedures
- Review techniques
- Assessment ideas
- Classroom management support
- Online adaptations
- Links to Activity Bank resources

The Educator Guide should be considered essential.

---

## Activity Bank Vision

The Activity Bank should exist in two forms:

### Embedded

Referenced throughout the Educator Guide.

### Standalone

Hosted independently and expandable over time.

Student materials should reference activities lightly.

Educator materials should reference them extensively.

---

## Localization

Localization is considered a future phase.

Current direction:

- Keep structure standardized.
- Keep learning objectives standardized.
- Keep Student Book globally neutral.
- Encourage educator personalization.
- Use Activity Bank experimentation to test local adaptations.

---

## AI Adaptation

Future AI systems may adapt:

- Examples
- Analogies
- Activities
- Context
- Explanations

AI should not modify:

- Learning objectives

Learning objectives remain the standard against which adaptation is measured.

# Peer Review Summary

Feedback themes identified from review discussions:

- Diploma is too long.
- Diploma can be too theoretical.
- Regional bias exists in some content.
- Activities need stronger integration.
- Online teaching support is weak.
- Competencies need clearer definition.
- Certification should follow learning outcomes.
