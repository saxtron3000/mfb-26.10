## Executive Summary

The Bitcoin Diploma 2026 is a comprehensive, ~~modular curricul~~um that teaches **how to think about Bitcoin through first principles**, not what to think. It is designed for independent educators and their communities, grounded in our Education Manifesto, and delivered through multiple formats (online, in-person, self-paced). The program emphasizes critical reasoning, experiential learning, and locally-adapted teaching practices.


---

## 1. Pedagogical Foundation

### 1.1 Core Philosophy

The Bitcoin Diploma is built on **three non-negotiable principles**:


1. **Teach HOW to think, not WHAT to think**
   * Students develop independent reasoning skills
   * Content raises questions, doesn't prescribe answers
   * Educators facilitate discovery, not transmission
2. **Empower independent educators**
   * Curriculum is open-source and modular
   * Designed for grassroots, community-led delivery
   * Teachers adapt to local contexts, values, and learner needs
3. **Build the revolution from the mind inward**
   * Bitcoin education is about reclaiming personal agency and sovereignty
   * Economics and money are entry points to understanding power structures
   * Learning objectives center on critical thinking, not compliance

**Source:** MFB Education Manifesto — foundational document for all curriculum design.

### 1.2 Learning Theory & Pedagogical Approach

The Diploma employs **constructivist and experiential learning** principles:

* **Scaffolded inquiry:** Lead questions guide exploration without providing answers
* **Think-pair-share cycles:** Students develop ideas individually, compare with peers, synthesize insights
* **Experiential activities:** Simulations, role-plays, and real-world applications (e.g., trading history simulations)
* **Misconceptions work:** Explicit strategy to identify and address common misunderstandings
* **Talk moves:** Structured dialogue techniques for educators to build reasoning in real-time

**Key resources supporting pedagogy:**

* Talk Moves Library
* Misconceptions Library
* Participation Formats Library
* Educator guides with facilitation strategies

## 2. Curriculum Structure

### 2.1 Program Organization

**Format:** Modular chapters with integrated activities, discussions, and assessments.

**Delivery Modes:**

* **In-person cohort:** Educator-led, typically 40–60 hours over 8–12 weeks
* **Online:** Synchronous (live) or asynchronous (self-paced with educator check-ins)
* **Self-taught:** Independent learner using printed or digital materials
* **Blended:** Combination of the above

**Release Cycle:**

* **26.04 (April):** Innovation release — new content, restructuring, pedagogical improvements
* **26.10 (October):** Consolidation release — refinement, feedback integration, localization

### 2.2 Chapter Structure (Example: Chapter 1)

Each chapter follows a consistent pedagogical architecture:

#### **Lead Questions**

* 4–5 open-ended questions that frame the chapter theme
* Designed to activate prior knowledge and create curiosity
* Used in opening discussion (think-pair-share)

#### **Foundational Content Sections**

* **What is \[concept\]?** — Definition and context
* **Why does \[concept\] matter?** — Real-world relevance
* **How does \[concept\] work?** — Mechanics and principles
* **What are the types/variations?** — Categories and nuances

#### **Guided Discussions**

* Structured prompts for educators to facilitate group dialogue
* Often connected to lead questions or specific scenarios
* Build critical thinking and collective meaning-making

#### **Activities**

* **Individual work:** Diagrams, written reflections, problem-solving
* **Group work:** Simulations, case study analysis, debates
* **Real-world application:** Connections to learners' own experiences (e.g., "When have you experienced scarcity?")

#### **Consolidation & Synthesis**

* Summary of key concepts
* Reflection prompts
* Bridge to next chapter

### 2.3 Content Scope (2026)

**Chapter 1: Money & Economics Fundamentals**

* Why do we need money?
* What is money? (Definitions, functions, properties)
* Types of money (physical, digital fiat, cryptocurrency, stablecoins, CBDCs)
* The psychology of money (scarcity, decision-making, behavior)

**Subsequent Chapters** (structure TBD in ongoing development):

* Bitcoin history and design
* How Bitcoin works (technical foundations)
* Cryptography and security
* Bitcoin as money vs. Bitcoin as asset
* Use cases and adoption
* Risks, criticisms, and limitations

**Cross-cutting Themes:**

* Critical thinking & source evaluation
* Economic incentives and game theory
* Technology literacy (without requiring programming knowledge)
* Global financial systems and power
* Personal sovereignty and agency

## 3. Pedagogical Features & Tools

### 3.1 Facilitation Resources

**For Educators:**

* **Teacher's Guide (2026):** Lesson plan structure, timing, facilitation strategies
* **Talk Moves Library:** Specific dialogue techniques to build reasoning (e.g., "Turn and talk," "Say more about that," "Do you agree or disagree?")

### 3.2 Student-Facing Design

**Digital Format:**

* Fillable/interactive elements (not static PDFs)
* Space for reflection and annotation
* Visual hierarchy and clarity
* Consistent branding (MFB brand colors, typography)

**Printed Format:**

* High-quality paper and binding
* Illustrative assets (diagrams, infographics, photos)
* Space for writing and note-taking
* Dictionary-style definition boxes with structure
* Clean, intentional layout (not dense or overwhelming)

### 3.3 Progression & Scaffolding

* **Conceptual building:** Each chapter builds on prior concepts
* **Cognitive load management:** Complex ideas introduced gradually
* **Support structures:** Worked examples, model answers, reflection prompts
* **Challenge options:** Extension activities for advanced learners

## 4. Learning Objectives & Assessment

### 4.1 Learner Outcomes (2026 — Under Review)

**Cognitive Domain (Bloom's Taxonomy):**

* **Remember:** Define key concepts (money, value, scarcity, Bitcoin)
* **Understand:** Explain functions of money, types of currency systems
* **Apply:** Use economic reasoning to analyze real-world scenarios
* **Analyze:** Compare different monetary systems (fiat, cryptocurrency, barter)
* **Evaluate:** Assess criticisms and benefits of Bitcoin; make reasoned judgments
* **Create:** Design alternative monetary systems or solutions to economic problems

**Affective Domain:**

* Develop agency and self-directed learning
* Build confidence in thinking independently about economic systems
* Cultivate curiosity about technology and governance
* Foster community and peer learning

### 4.2 Assessment Approaches

**Formative (During Learning):**

* Classroom discussions and think-pair-share outputs
* Reflection journal entries
* Exit tickets (quick check-for-understanding)
* Activity completion and peer feedback

**Summative (End of Course):**

* Comprehensive project (e.g., case study analysis, podcast, presentation)
* Written exam or essay (assessing reasoning, not memorization)
* Certification pathway (optional; see Section 5)

**Notes on Assessment:**

* Online and self-paced formats require built-in checkpoint mechanisms
* Assessments should measure reasoning, not compliance
* Educators given rubrics and facilitation guidance for consistency

## 7. Quality Standards & Design Principles

### 7.1 Content Quality Standards

* **Accuracy:** All factual claims verified against primary sources
* **Balance:** Present multiple perspectives (especially on controversial topics)
* **Clarity:** Language accessible to target audience; no jargon without definition
* **Engagement:** Activities and examples relevant to learner lives
* **Pedagogical soundness:** Aligned with learning objectives and best practices

### 7.2 Design & Accessibility

* **Visual design:** Consistent with MFB brand (purple primary, orange accent, white space)
* **Readability:** Clear typography (Open Sans, Raleway, Ubuntu), appropriate spacing
* **Accessibility:** High contrast, alt text for images, plain language, no assumptions about abilities
* **Inclusivity:** Examples and activities reflect diverse cultures, genders, abilities, socioeconomic backgrounds

### 7.3 Localization & Adaptation

**Open-source philosophy:**

* Educators encouraged to adapt content to local context
* Translation workflow: content → AI translation → volunteer proofreading → design & publishing
* Maintained version control and community contribution process
