## **Bitcoin Diploma 2026 Content and Structure Discussion**

**Date/Time:** February 11, 2026, 10:30 AM CST

**Attendees:** Benoit Berenger, gabe@miprimerbitcoin.io, pablo@miprimerbitcoin.io

### **Overall Summary**

The team reviewed field feedback on the Bitcoin Diploma, focusing on length, regional relevance, and the balance between practical competence and theory. The 2026 restructuring is an improvement on paper, but adoption and “real world use” are still unclear. The group discussed two paths: keeping one diploma with optional content, or splitting it into layered tracks (basic practical vs advanced theory). Certification is treated as a competency problem first, and content second. Near term plan is an April beta release with design and content hygiene upgrades, followed by a more structural October release (layering and rebranding). AI was proposed for localization and analogy customization without changing the core curriculum.

### **Context and Field Feedback**

* 2025 diploma feedback: too long, too theoretical, too America-centric, and not aligned enough with field teaching realities.

* 2026 version: shorter and reorganized, but not widely used yet, so impact is still uncertain.

* Strong need for “notes from the field” and more discussion before locking future structural changes.

* Activities are currently designed mainly for in-person teaching; online delivery needs to be better supported.

### **Core Issues Identified**

* **Length and cognitive load:** risk of overload, unclear what is essential vs optional.

* **Regional bias:** content framing too US-centric for a global learner base.

* **Practical engagement:** too much reading and sharing, not enough structured participation.

* **Delivery format gap:** in-person bias, limited online-ready methods.

* **Self-custody constraints:** learners face economic and technical barriers that curricula often ignore.

* **Lightning vs on-chain practicality:** what learners should do in real life vs what’s ideal conceptually.

### **Options Considered**

**Option A: Keep one diploma, modularize**

* Keep current structure but clearly mark non-essential content as optional.

* Build a practical “fast path” through the same material.

**Option B: Split into layered diploma**

* **Layer 1:** essentials and practical use (buying, receiving, storing, self-custody basics, safe usage).

* **Layer 2:** advanced theoretical and technical content (deeper protocol, technical reasoning, organizations, etc.).

* Rebrand “Bitcoin for Organizations” as advanced content rather than a separate track.

### **Questions to Critically Evaluate the 2026 Restructure**

* Did merging “Why” and “What” improve clarity?

* Is the new chapter order more coherent?

* Does splitting on-chain and Lightning improve understanding?

* Is the content more rigorous while staying accessible?

* Does the structure support real-world competence better than before?

### **Pedagogical Direction**

* Shift emphasis from “teaching as reading” to teaching as participation and practice.

* Strengthen the activity bank and integrate it directly into the diploma flow.

* Ensure online-compatible versions of activities exist, not just in-person plans.

### **Certification Principles**

* Certification must be **competency-based** and aligned to **learning outcomes**.

* Certification design should be done before finalizing content, to avoid measuring the wrong thing.

* The core challenge: verifying competence in a decentralized teaching environment.

### **AI Use Proposal**

* AI may support:

  * localization (context, examples, analogies),

  * cultural adaptation,

  * instructional personalization,

  * without changing the core curriculum standards.

* Constraint: AI customization must preserve curriculum integrity and assessment comparability.
