# 1. Project Title

Bitcoin Diploma Redesign

---

# 2. Project Purpose

Redesign the Bitcoin Diploma to maintain its relevance, improve effectiveness, increase long-term educational impact, and strengthen its role as a serious standard for grassroots Bitcoin education.

This is not a replacement of the diploma’s identity, philosophy, or foundations.

The redesign should make the program:

- shorter;
- clearer;
- more modular;
- more practical;
- more captivating;
- more teachable;
- more scalable;
- more educationally effective.

---

# 3. Applied Design Direction

The following design decisions are now applied to this brief:

- The Diploma should remain open source.
- The Diploma should remain community driven.
- The primary design target is in-person delivery.
- The redesign should empower educators.
- The redesign should preserve high-quality pedagogy.
- The target duration is a maximum of 8 hours.
- The current working structure is 4 modules of approximately 2 hours each.
- The Diploma should remain a single diploma with optional advanced sections.
- The emerging structure is Core / Nice-to-Have / Advanced.
- Certification design is deferred until learning outcomes are finalized.
- Each module should include knowledge-based, competency-based, and outcome-based objectives.
- Practical learning should increase from approximately 20% toward approximately 40%.
- All practical skills should follow PPP: Presentation, Practice, Production.
- Advanced technical content should move into optional advanced sections or advanced modules.
- The Educator Guide should become an essential teaching support system.
- The Activity Bank should be both embedded and standalone.
- Localization should be considered during design but treated mainly as a later project.
- AI may adapt examples, analogies, activities, context, and explanations, but should not modify learning objectives.

# 5. Design Philosophy

## Teach How to Think, Not What to Think

- Learners develop independent reasoning.
- Content should raise questions rather than prescribe answers.
- Educators facilitate discovery.
- The Diploma should build judgment, not passive agreement.

## Empower Independent Educators

- Curriculum should support grassroots, community-led delivery.
- Educators should be able to adapt to local context without losing standards.
- The Educator Guide should help educators teach, not merely repeat student content.
- Teaching standards should be visible through structure, prompts, pacing, and activity design.

## Build From First Principles

- Economics, money, technology, and sovereignty should be approached through foundational reasoning.
- Learners should understand why concepts matter before being pushed into technical details.
- Technical depth must not overpower beginner competence.

## Practical Competence Matters

- The Diploma should not remain primarily theoretical.
- Learners need practical pathways into Bitcoin usage and participation.
- Practical learning should be structured, not incidental.
- Practical skills should follow PPP: Presentation, Practice, Production.

## Community and Dialogue

- Social learning, dialogue, and discussion are strengths of the Diploma.
- Redesign should preserve and strengthen these features.
- Discussion should be structured, purposeful, and linked to learning outcomes.

## Improve Effectiveness

# 8. Focus Areas

## 8.1 Duration and Structure

- Target maximum duration: 8 hours.
- Working assumption: 4 modules of approximately 2 hours each.
- Final structure remains subject to module requirements and quality review.
- The redesign should allow modules to be broken into smaller sessions.

## 8.2 Curriculum Scope

- Clarify what the Diploma is responsible for teaching.
- Reduce vague scope.
- Distinguish Core, Nice-to-Have, and Advanced content.
- Clarify multidisciplinary boundaries.
- Prevent scope creep.

## 8.3 Learning Objectives

Each module should include:

### Knowledge Objectives

What learners should know.

### Competency Objectives

What learners should be able to do.

### Outcome Objectives

What changes should occur in learner confidence, capability, or behavior.

## 8.4 Practical Learning

- Increase practical learning from approximately 20% toward approximately 40%.
- Structure practical content using PPP.
- Clarify realistic beginner actions.
- Address self-custody constraints realistically.
- Clarify Lightning vs on-chain teaching.
- Use simulations, scenarios, structured activities, and guided application.

## 8.5 Educator Support

The Educator Guide should:

- absorb teaching instructions from the Student Book;
- provide step-by-step procedures for theoretical and practical lessons;
- include facilitation notes;
- include timing guidance;
- include prompts;
- include review checks;
- include misconception support;
- include activity links;
- help educators deliver quality Bitcoin education consistently.

## 8.6 Activity Design

- Build an Activity Bank.
- Embed Activity Bank references clearly in the Educator Guide.
- Reference activities lightly in the Student Book.
- Host the Activity Bank as a standalone website resource.
- Support individual, pair, group, and whole-class activities.
- Support active participation rather than over-reliance on reading and discussion.

## 8.7 Review and Consolidation

Every module should ideally include:

- Knowledge review.
- Reflection.
- Skills check.
- Competency check.

These should mainly sit in the Educator Guide, with educators choosing how to evaluate based on context.

## 8.8 Localization

- Localization changes content, not structure.
- Multiple localized Diploma versions are out of scope for this phase.
- The Student Book should be as neutral and adaptable as possible.
- The Educator Guide should encourage and demonstrate personalization.
- The Activity Bank should become the main space for testing localized adaptation.
- Localization should be considered during design but carried out later.

## 8.9 AI Adaptation

AI may adapt:

- examples;
- analogies;
- activities;
- context;
- explanations;
- translation support.

AI must not adapt:

- learning objectives.

Guardrails should protect:

- educational quality;
- Bitcoin-only scope;
- open-source principles;
- curriculum integrity;
- learner outcomes.

## 8.10 Visual and Student Experience Design

- Improve layout, hierarchy, definitions, tables, diagrams, worksheets, and print/digital usability.
- Improve clarity without reducing seriousness.
- Separate student-facing learning content from educator-facing teaching instructions.

# 9. Blind Spots / Risks

## Scope Creep

The Diploma covers many domains. Without clear boundaries, the program risks becoming too broad.

## Technical Overload

Some technical details may not be beginner prerequisites. These should move to optional advanced sections.

## Weak Competency Mapping

Objectives, competencies, activities, and evidence of learning must be aligned.

## Inconsistent Delivery

Without strong educator support, delivery quality may vary too much between communities.

## Duration Blind Spot

Current timing expectations are unclear. Some resources reference 60, 90, or 120 minutes. Activity timing is not always clearly included.

## Mode Blind Spot

The primary design target is now in-person, but online, self-paced, and hybrid adaptations still need definition.

## Assessment Blind Spot

Certification may measure the wrong thing if designed before learning outcomes are clear. Certification design is deferred.

## Educator Support Blind Spot

The Educator Guide must not simply mirror the Student Book. It must provide implementation value.

## Localization Blind Spot

Localization is necessary but can dilute standards if not governed.

## Practical Competence Blind Spot

Practical content exists but is not consistently structured through PPP.

## Chapter 1 Design Blind Spot

Chapter 1 has both form and content issues. Redesign must not treat layout as the only problem.
