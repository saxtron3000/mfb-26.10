**Feedback on BD 26.10**

This is my overall perspective:

The new structure is very interesting. The content is much more concise and focused than in previous versions. One very important point to highlight is that I find it much more efficient that the context of money is now contained within a single chapter (block). In previous versions, we received feedback that too much time was spent discussing money throughout the diploma program.

I understand that this version is designed so educators can adapt it from a neutral starting point to their local context, and I really like that. One difference I notice compared to previous versions is that there are no numbered activities within the blocks, for example: Activity 1, 2, 3, etc. I assume this is intended to give educators the flexibility to decide how to manage their classes. My suggestion is that the Educator Guide could include more detailed guidance on where and how to implement the activities.

## **Proposal for time management from the perspective of classroom delivery**

After analyzing and reading each block, I believe that a time range of approximately two hours is not enough to teach an entire block in a single class, especially Block 1 and Block 3\.

In theory it may work, but in practice it is a bit challenging because the content in this version (26.10) encourages more discussion and critical thinking. If we include all the content (even with some concepts summarized), discussions, questions, activities, and local adaptations, the available time may not be sufficient, especially considering that many educators dedicate only 1–2 hours per class. Most educators will probably adapt the content to fit their available time, and if they have limited time, they will inevitably have to sacrifice some content.

This is the structure I propose for you to review and perhaps take some ideas from.

### **Block 1**

**Part 1**

* Opening questions about money  
* Why money exists  
* Functions and properties of money  
* Barter and the double coincidence of wants  
* Scarcity, value, and exchange  
* Beginner monetary history from barter to modern fiat

**Part 2**

* Fiat money  
* Fractional reserve banking at beginner level  
* Inflation and purchasing power  
* CBDCs as a beginner fiat-system example where appropriate  
* Why people look for alternatives (Precious Metals, Real Estate, Stocks)  
* Review: what problem does fiat money create?

### **Block 2**

I believe this content can be comfortably delivered within the estimated time range.

### **Block 3**

**Part 1**

* Wallets  
* Addresses  
* Keys  
* Seed phrases  
* Custodial and non-custodial use  
* Hot and cold wallets  
* Wallet recovery and seed phrase testing  
* Receiving bitcoin  
* Sending bitcoin

**Part 2**

* Verification, block explorers, and irreversible transactions  
* Safety checklist: seed phrases, address checks, scams, and starting small  
* Acquiring and exchanging bitcoin as pathway awareness, not financial advice  
* On-chain and Lightning payments at beginner level  
* Review: what does self-custody responsibility mean?

### **Block 4**

I believe this content can also be delivered within the estimated time range.

This approach would give educators a clearer framework for adapting their classes while preserving the original **four-block structure.**

My philosophy is that, for a class, it is always better to have extra time than to run out of time.

## **Some feedbacks about the content**

### **Beginner monetary history from barter to modern fiat**

I think it is important to mention some key historical events (such as the Nixon Shock). In my opinion, we could include Executive Order 6102 within a section like **Representative paper** or **Bank and government money**. It does not need to be a long explanation, but a short and well-structured context would be valuable. Based on my experience teaching this material, this is a key moment where people clearly understand how the fiat system came to be controlled.

### **Finite supply**

The explanation and context are  excellent for a classroom setting. However, for someone who downloads the PDF and reads it independently, I think it would be helpful to include a table of the previous halvings in this section.
